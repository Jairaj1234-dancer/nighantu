## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Bhaishajya Ratnavali, Shoolaroga Adhikara, Chapter 30, Verses 142-147; also referenced in Rasaratna Samuchaya

## How does it work?

- [Loha Bhasma](/herb/loha-bhasma/) provides iron, which the body uses for hemoglobin synthesis and erythropoiesis
- Amalaki (Emblica officinalis) is rich in vitamin C, markedly enhancing non-heme iron absorption in the GI tract and acting as a potent [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/)
- [Yashtimadhu](/herb/yashtimadhu/) (licorice) provides gastroprotective and anti-inflammatory effects, reducing GI irritation from iron supplementation
- [Guduchi](/herb/guduchi/) (Tinospora cordifolia) has [immunomodulatory](/reference/immunomodulatory/) and [hepatoprotective](/reference/hepatoprotective/) properties, supporting liver function for optimal iron metabolism
- Ushna virya (mildly heating potency) of [Loha Bhasma](/herb/loha-bhasma/) kindles Agni (digestive fire), transforming Ama (metabolic toxins) and improving Dhatu (tissue) nourishment

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Pandu Roga (iron deficiency anemia): traditionally used for this condition. Clinical studies have reported improvement in hemoglobin levels.
- Digestive disorders with acid peptic symptoms: traditionally used for these complaints. The formulation includes Amalaki and [Yashtimadhu](/herb/yashtimadhu/) as components.

## What do recent studies show?

- Srikanth N, Rao BC, Yadav B and others 2021. [Dhatri Lauha in the management of iron deficiency anemia: A prospective open-label single-arm multi-center trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37153071/). *Ayu*. PMID [37153071](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37153071/) · [doi:10.4103/ayu.ayu_379_21](https://doi.org/10.4103/ayu.ayu_379_21)  
  Multi-center trial across 12 centers (40 participants/site) showed Dhatri Lauha 500 mg twice daily for 45 days significantly improved hemoglobin, RBC, and iron metabolism parameters in IDA patients; well tolerated and clinically safe

## Recent safety updates

- The multi-center trial listed above reported Dhatri Lauha was well tolerated with no significant adverse effects at 500 mg twice daily for 45 days
- Prolonged unsupervised use may lead to iron overload (hemochromatosis-like symptoms) - serum ferritin monitoring recommended beyond 8 weeks
- Contraindicated in patients with hemochromatosis, thalassemia major, or conditions with existing iron overload
- [Loha Bhasma](/herb/loha-bhasma/) toxicity study showed safety at therapeutic dose and 5x therapeutic dose; only at highest doses were biochemical alterations evident, confirming dose-dependent safety profile
- Heavy metal contaminant testing should confirm compliance with API limits for lead, arsenic, and mercury

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Vati/tablet

**Standard Dosage:** 250–500 mg (1–2 tablets) twice daily

**Optimal Timing:** After meals with honey or warm water

**Storage:** Airtight container; cool, dry place

**Marker Compounds:** Iron oxide (from [Loha Bhasma](/herb/loha-bhasma/)), Ascorbic acid/[tannins](/reference/tannins/) (from Dhatri), [Triphala](/herb/triphala/) [polyphenols](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#polyphenols)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Honey, warm water, or Amla juice
