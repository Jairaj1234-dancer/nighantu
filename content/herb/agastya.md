## How does it work?

- [Antioxidant](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antioxidant/): Phenolic compounds and [flavonoids](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/flavonoids/) (kaempferol, [quercetin](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/quercetinquercetin/)) scavenge ROS and inhibit lipid peroxidation
- [Anticancer](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/anticancer/): Flavonoids and [alkaloids](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) activate caspase cascades and induce apoptosis through mitochondrial pathway
- Anti-fibrotic: Leaf extracts modulate TGF-beta signaling and reduce collagen deposition in fibrotic tissue

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Anti-inflammatory use validated through inhibition of pro-inflammatory mediators and reduction of edema in animal models
- [Hepatoprotective](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/hepatoprotective/) traditional use confirmed by reduction of liver [enzymes](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes) (ALT, AST) in hepatotoxicity models
- Alpha-glucosidase inhibitory activity of leaf flavonoids supports traditional [antidiabetic](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antidiabetic/) use

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Phenolic Composition, Antioxidant and Anti-fibrotic Effects of Sesbania grandiflora** (2022): Aqueous ethanol extract of leaves demonstrated significant antioxidant and anti-fibrotic capacity, with leaves showing greater activity than flowers.
- **Distribution, Phytochemical Insights, and Cytotoxic Potential of the Sesbania Genus** (2025): Comprehensive review revealing activation of caspase cascades and induction of apoptosis in cancer cells, attributed to flavonoids and alkaloids.
- **Chemical Components of Sesbania grandiflora Root and Their Antituberculosis Activity** (2013): Isoflavanoids (isovestitol, medicarpin, sativan) and betulinic acid exhibited antituberculosis activity against M. tuberculosis H37Rv with MIC values of 50-100 mcg/mL.

## Recent safety updates

- Widely consumed as a food vegetable in Southeast Asia; flowers and leaves are edible with a long history of safe dietary use
- No significant adverse effects reported in animal toxicity studies; however, pregnant women should consult practitioners before medicinal use due to limited safety data

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder), Kashayam (decoction), Pushpa swarasa (flower juice), Capsule, Lehyam (confection)

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6g powder twice daily; 50-100ml decoction; 10-20ml flower juice

**Bioavailability:** Good oral bioavailability for water-soluble constituents (flavonoids, amino acids). Lipophilic compounds benefit from co-administration with ghee or warm milk. The high protein and free amino acid content enhances overall nutrient absorption.

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals with warm water or honey for respiratory conditions; with food for nutritional supplementation

**Standardized Extract:** Standardized to minimum 1.5% total flavonoids. Flower extract standardized to oleanolic acid content (min 0.5%).

**Shelf Life:** 18 months (powder); 2 years (capsules/tablets); 3 months (fresh juice, refrigerated)

**Storage:** Store in airtight containers away from light. Powder and capsules below 25°C, RH < 60%. Fresh preparations require refrigeration (2-8°C).

**Marker Compounds:** Oleanolic acid, Kaempferol, [Quercetin](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/quercetinquercetin/), Cyanidin [glycosides](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides) (red variety), Sesbanimide

### Extraction Methods

- Aqueous decoction (traditional Kashaya method)
- Hydroalcoholic extraction (60:40)
- Cold-pressed juice from fresh flowers
- Soxhlet extraction with ethanol for alkaloid enrichment

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Vasa](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/vasa/) (Adhatoda) for respiratory conditions
- With [Haridra](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/haridra/) for anti-inflammatory synergy
- With [Yashtimadhu](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/yashtimadhu/) for bronchial support
