## How does it work?

- Anti-inflammatory activity via triterpenoid-mediated (madhucic acid, amyrins) inhibition of COX-2 and lipoxygenase pathways
- [Antidiabetic](/reference/antidiabetic/) action through saponin-mediated (mowrin) enhancement of insulin secretion and improvement of [glucose](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#glucose) tolerance
- [Hepatoprotective](/reference/hepatoprotective/) effects via flavonoid-mediated ([quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/), myricetin) reduction of oxidative stress and restoration of liver [enzymes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes)
- Antiurolithiatic mechanism through inhibition of crystal nucleation, aggregation and [diuretic](/reference/diuretic/) action

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Traditionally used for inflammation and as an [analgesic](/reference/analgesic/). Animal studies have reported anti-inflammatory and analgesic activity in paw edema and hot plate models.
- Animal studies have reported antiulcer activity in aspirin-induced and ethanol-induced gastric ulcer models.
- Traditionally used for oral health. In vitro studies have reported antibacterial activity against Streptococcus mutans.
- The seed oil (mahua butter) is traditionally used as a skin emollient. Studies have reported moisturizing and skin barrier activity.

## Recent safety updates

- Toxicity studies reveal non-toxic effects even at larger doses in preclinical models; flower-based preparations are widely consumed by tribal communities as food
- Mahua flowers contain natural sugars that ferment to produce alcohol; distilled mahua liquor should be consumed responsibly. Seed oil (mahua butter) is edible but may contain [saponins](/reference/saponins/) requiring processing.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder), Kwatha (decoction), Taila (Mahua oil), Capsules, Tablets, Avaleha (confection), Asava/Arishta, [Ghrita](/herb/ghrita/) (medicated ghee)

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6 g bark/flower powder per day; 20-50 mL decoction twice daily; 5-10 mL Mahua oil externally; 500-1000 mg standardized extract daily

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals for gastric protective effects; morning for tonic and [Rasayana](/reference/rasayana/) effects; topical application at night for skin conditions

**Storage:** Bark powder below 25 deg C in airtight containers. Flower powder is extremely hygroscopic - store with desiccant, RH below 40%. Mahua oil in cool, dark conditions. Asava at room temperature in glass containers.

**Marker Compounds:** Mowrin (Mi-saponin), Bassic acid, Madhucic acid, [Quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/), Dihydroquercetin, Beta-amyrin, Oleic acid, [Stearic acid](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#stearic-acidstearic-acid)
