## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Sugars: [fructose](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-d-g/#fructose) (38%), [glucose](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-d-g/#glucose) (31%), maltose, [sucrose](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sucrose)
- [Enzymes](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes): glucose oxidase, diastase, invertase, catalase
- Hydrogen peroxide (generated by glucose oxidase)
- Organic acids: gluconic acid (primary), acetic, citric, formic
- [Polyphenols](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-o-q/#polyphenols) and [flavonoids](/nighantu/reference/flavonoids/) ([chrysin](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-c-d/#chrysin), [quercetin](/nighantu/reference/quercetinquercetin/), [kaempferol](/nighantu/reference/kaempferol/), [pinocembrin](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-o-q/#pinocembrin))
- Methylglyoxal (MGO) - especially in Manuka honey
- Minerals: [potassium](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-o-q/#potassium), calcium, [magnesium](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-l-o/#magnesium), iron, zinc
- [Vitamins](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-t-z/#vitamins): B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, C
- Amino acids (proline predominant)
- Volatile aromatic compounds

## How does it work?

- Yogavahi (bioenhancer): Osmotic properties, enzyme content, and low molecular weight compounds enhance absorption and deep tissue penetration of co-administered drugs
- Antibacterial: Dual mechanism via hydrogen peroxide generation (glucose oxidase) and non-peroxide factors (MGO, defensin-1, phenolic acids); effective against MRSA and VRE
- Wound healing: Creates moist healing environment, reduces inflammation via [antioxidant](/nighantu/reference/antioxidant/) polyphenols, promotes angiogenesis and fibroblast proliferation
- Anti-inflammatory: Flavonoids (chrysin, [quercetin](/nighantu/reference/quercetinquercetin/)) inhibit COX-2 and NF-kB pathways; gluconic acid maintains optimal acidic wound pH

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Anupana (drug carrier): Scientific evidence confirms honey enhances bioavailability of herbal compounds through osmotic facilitation, enzyme-mediated absorption, and tissue penetration - validating Yogavahi concept
- Vrana Ropana (wound healing): Extensive clinical evidence supports honey's antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and tissue-regenerative properties in both acute and chronic wounds
- Kasa-Shwasa (cough and respiratory disorders): WHO recommends honey for childhood cough; clinical trials show honey is more effective than dextromethorphan for nocturnal cough relief

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Review on the Rationality of Using Madhu (Honey) as Anupana** (2025): Sharangadhara Samhita features 54 formulations using Madhu as Anupana; Madhu's Yogavahi property as a bioavailability enhancer has pharmacological basis in its enzyme content, osmotic properties, and ability to penetrate tissues.
- **Modern Honey-Based Delivery Systems for Wound Healing: A Review of Current Trends** (2025): Honey-containing hydrogels with nanoparticle integration show superior wound healing outcomes; medical-grade honey exhibits lowest MBC against S. pseudintermedius, E. faecalis, and P. aeruginosa.
- **Mechanistic Roles of Different Varieties of Honey on Wound Healing** (2024): Different honey varieties have distinct wound healing mechanisms; antibacterial efficacy depends on MGO content, hydrogen peroxide production, and pH, with medical-grade honey significantly reducing infection rates and hospital stays.

## Recent safety updates

- Heating honey above 40 degrees C produces hydroxymethylfurfural (HMF), a potentially toxic compound; Ayurvedic prohibition of heated honey validated by modern chemistry
- Infant botulism risk: Honey should not be given to children under 12 months due to Clostridium botulinum spores
- Diabetic patients must use cautiously due to high glycemic load despite lower GI than sugar; can cause blood sugar spikes
- Adulteration with corn syrup, inverted sugar, and rice syrup is widespread; quality testing (C4 sugar ratio, diastase activity) essential

## What is it made of?

### Nutritional Profile

- **Sugars:** ~80% (Fructose 38%, Glucose 31%)
- **Water:** 17-20%
- **Enzymes:** Diastase, Invertase, Glucose oxidase
- **Organic acids:** Gluconic acid (primary)
- **Minerals:** Potassium, Calcium, Phosphorus
- **Phenolics:** Flavonoids, Phenolic acids (varies by floral source)
- **HMF:** <40mg/kg (freshness indicator)

**Analytical Methods:** HPLC (sugars, HMF), Refractometry (moisture), Diastase activity

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Raw honey, Vehicle/adjuvant, Topical application, Component in Leha/Avaleha

**Standard Dosage:** 5–15 mL; as vehicle: sufficient quantity

**Bioavailability:** Bioenhancer — increases absorption and tissue penetration of co-administered herbs

**Optimal Timing:** Not with hot substances (Ayurvedic contraindication); morning preferred for Kapha conditions

**Standardized Extract:** Raw, unprocessed (Apakva Madhu); aged 1+ year (Purana Madhu) for therapeutic use

**Shelf Life:** Indefinite if unadulterated; best within 1–2 years

**Storage:** Room temperature; never refrigerate; glass or food-grade plastic

**Marker Compounds:** Fructose/glucose (60–75%), Hydrogen peroxide, Methylglyoxal, Defensin-1, Polyphenols

**Quality Parameters:** Moisture <20%, HMF <40 mg/kg, diastase activity >8 DN, no added sugars (C4 sugar ratio test)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Premier Yogavahi (bioenhancer/vehicle) in Ayurveda; Kapha-reducing vehicle; used in >200 formulations
