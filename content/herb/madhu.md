## How does it work?

- Yogavahi (bioenhancer): Osmotic properties, enzyme content, and low molecular weight compounds enhance absorption and deep tissue penetration of co-administered drugs
- Antibacterial: Dual mechanism via hydrogen peroxide generation (glucose oxidase) and non-peroxide factors (MGO, defensin-1, phenolic acids); effective against MRSA and VRE
- Wound healing: Creates moist healing environment, reduces inflammation via [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) polyphenols, promotes angiogenesis and fibroblast proliferation
- Anti-inflammatory: Flavonoids (chrysin, [quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/)) inhibit COX-2 and NF-kB pathways; gluconic acid maintains optimal acidic wound pH

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Anupana (drug carrier): traditionally used as a carrier for other herbs, reflecting the Yogavahi concept. Studies have reported that honey may enhance the bioavailability of herbal compounds through osmotic facilitation, enzyme-mediated absorption, and tissue penetration.
- Vrana Ropana (wound healing): traditionally used on wounds. Clinical studies have reported antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and tissue-regenerative properties of honey in both acute and chronic wounds.
- Kasa-Shwasa (cough and respiratory disorders): traditionally used for cough and respiratory complaints. Clinical studies have reported that honey reduced nocturnal cough in children more than dextromethorphan.

## Recent safety updates

- Classical Ayurvedic practice prohibits heating honey. Chemical analyses have reported that heating honey produces hydroxymethylfurfural (HMF), a potentially toxic compound.
- Infant botulism risk: Honey should not be given to children under 12 months due to Clostridium botulinum spores
- Diabetic patients must use cautiously due to high glycemic load despite lower GI than sugar; can cause blood sugar spikes
- Adulteration with corn syrup, inverted sugar, and rice syrup is widespread; quality testing (C4 sugar ratio, diastase activity) essential

## What is it made of?

### Nutritional Profile

- **Sugars:** ~80% (Fructose 38%, Glucose 31%)
- **Water:** 17-20%
- **Enzymes:** Diastase, Invertase, Glucose oxidase
- **Organic acids:** Gluconic acid (primary)
- **Minerals:** Potassium, Calcium, Phosphorus
- **Phenolics:** Flavonoids, Phenolic acids (varies by floral source)
- **HMF:** <40mg/kg (freshness indicator)

**Analytical Methods:** HPLC (sugars, HMF), Refractometry (moisture), Diastase activity

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Raw honey, Vehicle/adjuvant, Topical application, Component in Leha/Avaleha

**Standard Dosage:** 5–15 mL; as vehicle: sufficient quantity

**Bioavailability:** Bioenhancer, increases absorption and tissue penetration of co-administered herbs

**Optimal Timing:** Not with hot substances (Ayurvedic contraindication); morning preferred for Kapha conditions

**Standardized Extract:** Raw, unprocessed (Apakva Madhu); aged 1+ year (Purana Madhu) for therapeutic use

**Shelf Life:** Indefinite if unadulterated; best within 1–2 years

**Storage:** Room temperature; never refrigerate; glass or food-grade plastic

**Marker Compounds:** Fructose/glucose (60–75%), Hydrogen peroxide, Methylglyoxal, Defensin-1, Polyphenols

**Quality Parameters:** Moisture <20%, HMF <40 mg/kg, diastase activity >8 DN, no added sugars (C4 sugar ratio test)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Premier Yogavahi (bioenhancer/vehicle) in Ayurveda; Kapha-reducing vehicle; used in >200 formulations
