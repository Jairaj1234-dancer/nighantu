## How does it work?

- Arsenic sulfide (As2S3) in processed form has demonstrated [immunomodulatory](/reference/immunomodulatory/) and anti-proliferative properties - similar to the mechanism by which arsenic trioxide (As2O3) is used in modern oncology for acute promyelocytic leukemia treatment
- Anti-dermatological action: Arsenic compounds modulate keratinocyte proliferation and immune cell activity in skin, relevant to traditional use in skin diseases; may act through inhibition of NF-kB and modulation of apoptotic pathways
- The insoluble sulfide form provides a slow-release mechanism with lower peak blood arsenic levels compared to soluble arsenic compounds, potentially providing therapeutic effects at reduced toxicity

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Skin diseases (Kushtha): traditionally used for psoriasis and chronic dermatoses. Arsenic was historically used in Western medicine for psoriasis (Fowler's solution). Arsenic has been reported to have anti-proliferative effects on keratinocytes. Western medicine abandoned this practice because of carcinogenic risk.
- Fever and respiratory conditions: traditionally used for Jwara (fever) and Shwasa (asthma). Arsenic has been reported to have immunomodulatory properties. No clinical trials specific to Rasamanikya have been reported for these uses.

## Recent safety updates

- CRITICAL: Arsenic is classified as a Group 1 carcinogen by IARC. Chronic arsenic exposure even at low levels is associated with skin cancer, lung cancer, bladder cancer, and cardiovascular disease
- Clinical pilot study showed increased blood arsenic levels after just 15 days of use - while acute clinical toxicity was not observed, the long-term carcinogenic risk of elevated blood arsenic is well-established
- Historically, arsenic was used in Western medicine for psoriasis (Fowler's solution) but was abandoned due to recognized carcinogenic and chronic toxicity concerns - the same scientific evidence applies to Rasamanikya
- The arsenic sulfide form (As2S3) is less acutely toxic than arsenic trioxide (As2O3) due to lower solubility, but chronic exposure still poses carcinogenic risk; no long-term carcinogenicity studies specific to Rasamanikya exist
- Cases of chronic arsenic poisoning following Ayurvedic medication use (including Rasamanikya) have been documented in medical literature; symptoms include skin hyperpigmentation, keratoses, neuropathy, and organ damage

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Vati or powder

**Standard Dosage:** 60–125 mg twice daily

**Storage:** Airtight, labeled container; Schedule E1
