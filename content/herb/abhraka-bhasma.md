## How does it work?

- Anti-cancer activity through dose-dependent cytotoxicity, apoptosis induction via mitochondrial pathway, and inhibition of tumor cell proliferation in breast and lung cancer cell lines
- DNA repair enhancement through stimulation of base excision repair (BER) pathway, protecting against genotoxic damage and chromosomal aberrations
- [Immunomodulatory](/nighantu/reference/immunomodulatory/) action through modulation of innate and adaptive immune responses, supporting [Rasayana](/nighantu/reference/rasayana/) (rejuvenation) properties described in classical texts
- [Hepatoprotective](/nighantu/reference/hepatoprotective/) effect at therapeutic doses through [antioxidant](/nighantu/reference/antioxidant/) and cellular membrane stabilization, supporting traditional use in liver disorders
- Respiratory system support through anti-inflammatory and mucolytic properties derived from multi-mineral nanoparticle composition

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Hepatitis and liver disorders: Hepatoprotective effects confirmed at therapeutic doses in animal models, validating Yakrit Roga (liver disease) indication
- Respiratory diseases (Shwasa, Kasa): Anti-inflammatory and respiratory support properties demonstrated through preclinical studies on lung cancer cells
- Cancer management: Cytotoxicity against breast and lung cancer cell lines validates traditional use for Arbuda (tumor) conditions, though clinical translation pending
- Rejuvenation (Rasayana): DNA repair enhancement and immunomodulatory activities support the classical Rasayana (rejuvenation/anti-aging) claims

## Recent safety updates

- GHS CLASS V (SAFE): LD50 > 2000 mg/kg in acute toxicity per OECD guidelines. Classified as non-toxic at single dose level.
- DOSE-DEPENDENT HEPATOTOXICITY: Mid and high doses induced REVERSIBLE hepatotoxicity in subacute studies. Low therapeutic doses remain non-toxic.
- NOT GENOTOXIC: Confirmed non-genotoxic and non-reproductive toxic in mouse models. Actually shows DNA repair enhancement against chemical mutagens.
- BATCH VARIABILITY CONCERN: Different preparation methods yield physically different end-products. Standardization across manufacturers is a key challenge for consistent safety profiles.
- FUTURE CLINICAL USE: Would require stringent ethical approval and robust pharmacovigilance systems for clinical application. Post-marketing surveillance essential.
