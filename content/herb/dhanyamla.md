## How does it work?

- Proposed (unverified) anti-inflammatory action through organic acid-mediated modulation of local tissue pH, in its traditional use for musculoskeletal disorders
- Lekhana (scraping/fat-reducing) effect through fermentation-derived bioactive compounds enhancing lipid metabolism
- Digestive stimulation (Deepana-Pachana) through enhancement of appetite and digestive enzyme activity via probiotic microflora and organic acids
- Proposed (unverified): [Analgesic](/reference/analgesic/) effect in Dhara therapy through transdermal absorption of anti-inflammatory compounds and thermal vasodilation

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Dhanyamla Dhara: traditionally used for musculoskeletal disorders such as arthritis and myalgia. Clinical observations have reported reduced pain in arthritis and myalgia.
- Deepana (digestive stimulant): traditionally used to stimulate appetite and digestion, an action attributed to its fermented acids.
- Sthoulya Chikitsa (obesity): used in classical practice for obesity, based on its Lekhana (fat-scraping) property.
- Vata Vyadhi (neurological disorders): traditionally used as Dhara therapy in stroke rehabilitation.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe for external Panchakarma applications (Dhara, Kayaseka); internal use (Virechana, Vasti) should be administered only under qualified Ayurvedic practitioner supervision
- Acidic pH (~4.13) may cause skin irritation in sensitive individuals during prolonged external application; not recommended for open wounds or severely inflamed skin

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Liquid preparation (fermented), Parisheka (pouring therapy), Dhara (stream pouring for external therapy), Avagaha (sitz bath additive)

**Standard Dosage:** External use primarily: 500ml-2L for Dhara/Parisheka therapy (30-45 min sessions); Internal use: 15-30ml twice daily before meals (as digestive, in some traditions)

**Optimal Timing:** External Dhara therapy: morning sessions preferred, patient should not be fasting. Internal use: before meals for digestive stimulation

**Storage:** Unpasteurized: refrigerate at 2-8 deg C. Pasteurized: store in airtight glass containers at room temperature away from direct sunlight. Do not use metal containers (acid corrosion). Traditional: earthen pot storage at cool ambient temperature.

**Marker Compounds:** Lactic acid, Acetic acid, [Citric acid](/glossary/compounds-c-d/#citric-acidcitric-acid), Total organic acids, Alcohol content (ethanol), B-vitamins (B1, B2, B6, B12)

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Dashamoola](/herb/dashamoola/) Kashayam for combined Dhara therapy in Vata disorders
- With [Eranda](/herb/eranda/) Taila post-Dhanyamla Dhara for enhanced pain relief
- With rock salt (Saindhava) added to fermentation for mineral enrichment
- Combined with [Triphala](/herb/triphala/) for internal digestive use
