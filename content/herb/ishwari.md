## Names and identification

| Language | Name |
|----------|------|
| English | Ishwari |
| Latin/Botanical | *Aristolochia indica L.* |

## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Aristolochic acid I and II (CARCINOGENIC)
- Aristolactams
- Aristolochine and aristolochene
- Ishwarone and ishwarane (sesquiterpenes)
- Cepharanthine (bisbenzylisoquinoline alkaloid)
- Aristolindiquinone

## How does it work?

- CARCINOGENIC: Aristolochic acid forms DNA adducts (dA-AA and dG-AA) leading to characteristic A:T to T:A transversion mutations in TP53 tumor suppressor gene
- Nephrotoxic: Causes progressive tubulointerstitial fibrosis and renal failure (aristolochic acid nephropathy)
- Anti-snake venom (traditional): Root extract shows neutralization of phospholipase A2 and protease activities of snake venoms in vitro
- Anti-inflammatory: Aristolactams show COX-2 inhibition but this benefit does not justify the carcinogenic risk

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Snake bite antidote (Sarpa-visha-hara) - partial in vitro validation of anti-venom properties but UNSAFE for human use due to aristolochic acid content
- Emmenagogue (Artava-janana) - traditional use confirmed pharmacologically but CONTRAINDICATED due to carcinogenicity
- Anti-inflammatory (Shotha-hara) - confirmed but completely outweighed by cancer and kidney damage risk
- NOTE: ALL traditional uses of Aristolochia indica are SUPERSEDED by safety concerns; use of this herb is NOT recommended under any circumstances

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Herbal products containing aristolochic acids: A call to revisit the context of safety** (2023): Called for comprehensive safety reassessment of all herbal products containing aristolochic acids; emphasized that even low-dose chronic exposure poses significant cancer and nephrotoxicity risk.
- **Characterization of the aqueous extract of the root of Aristolochia indica: evaluation as an antidote for snake bites** (2023): Identified aristolochic acid content of root extract as 3.08 +/- 1.88 x 10^-3 mg/mL; acute and sub-chronic studies showed no toxicity at lower doses, but high doses caused liver and kidney damage.
- **IARC Monographs on Aristolochic Acid Species - Group 1 Classification** (2024): Reaffirmed IARC Group 1 classification (sufficient evidence of carcinogenicity in humans) for aristolochic acids; linked to Balkan endemic nephropathy and urothelial cancers worldwide.

## Recent safety updates

- BANNED in multiple countries including USA, Canada, Germany, UK, France, Belgium, Austria, and Japan due to confirmed carcinogenicity and nephrotoxicity of aristolochic acids
- IARC Group 1 carcinogen (confirmed human carcinogen); causes aristolochic acid nephropathy (AAN) and significantly increases risk of urothelial/bladder cancer
- Even low aristolochic acid concentrations pose cumulative cancer risk with chronic exposure; no safe dose established for internal human use; traditional Ayurvedic Shodhana methods do NOT reliably eliminate aristolochic acids

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder), Kashayam (decoction), Capsule, Swarasa (fresh juice)

**Standard Dosage:** 1-3g powder twice daily; 30-60ml decoction

**Bioavailability:** Aristolochia indica root contains aristolochic acids (CAUTION: nephrotoxic and carcinogenic) and [terpenoids](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-t-z/#terpenoids). Aristolochic acids are well absorbed orally but are highly toxic. REGULATORY WARNING: Aristolochic acid-containing herbs are BANNED or restricted in many countries (USA, EU, UK, Australia). Modern formulations must demonstrate absence of aristolochic acids below detection limits or use aristolochic acid-free chemotypes if available.

**Optimal Timing:** Traditional use only under strict physician supervision; NOT recommended for modern formulation

**Standardized Extract:** NOT RECOMMENDED for modern extract standardization due to aristolochic acid toxicity. Traditional aqueous decoction with Shodhana only under qualified Vaidya supervision.

**Shelf Life:** Not applicable for modern commercial products due to regulatory restrictions

**Storage:** If held for research purposes: controlled substance storage, clearly labeled as toxic/restricted material. Not for commercial distribution in most markets.

**Marker Compounds:** Aristolochic acid I (toxic marker - must be below detection/regulatory limit), Aristolochic acid II (toxic marker), Aristolactam, Ceryl alcohol, Beta-[sitosterol](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol)

### Extraction Methods

- Aqueous decoction (traditional)
- Hydroalcoholic extraction - MUST test for aristolochic acid content
- Traditional Shodhana processing (may partially reduce but not eliminate toxic components)

### Synergistic Combinations

- With Maricha for snakebite antidote (traditional use)
- With [Pippali](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/pippali/) for respiratory conditions (traditional use only)
