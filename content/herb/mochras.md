## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Shamimicin (unique compound)
- Bombasin and bombamalone
- Bombamaloside (glycoside)
- [Tannins](/reference/tannins/) (gallic acid, ellagic acid derivatives)
- [Flavonoids](/reference/flavonoids/)
- [Lupeol](/reference/lupeol/) and beta-[sitosterol](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol) ([terpenoids](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#terpenoids)/[sterols](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sterols))

## How does it work?

- Styptic and [hemostatic](/glossary/pharmacology/#hemostatic) action through tannin-mediated protein precipitation and vasoconstriction at wound sites
- Anti-inflammatory activity via flavonoid-mediated inhibition of COX and LOX pathways
- [Antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) mechanism through gallic acid and ellagic acid-mediated free radical scavenging
- Anti-diarrheal effect through astringent tannins reducing intestinal secretion and motility

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Hemostatic and styptic (Raktastambhana): traditionally used to stop bleeding in wound care. This action is attributed to its astringent tannins.
- Anti-diarrheal (Atisara-nashaka): traditionally used for diarrhea. This action is attributed to its tannins, which are described as reducing intestinal secretion and hypermotility.
- Bone and tissue regeneration (Asthi-Sandhanakara): traditionally known as a bone setter. Studies have reported anti-inflammatory and wound healing activity.
- [Aphrodisiac](/glossary/pharmacology/#aphrodisiac) ([Vajikarana](/glossary/pharmacology/#vajikarana)): [Shalmali](/herb/shalmali/) resin is traditionally used for sexual health. Studies have looked at its nutritional value and reported hormonal modulation.

## Recent safety updates

- Resin (Mochras) is traditionally used externally and internally at Ayurvedic doses, primarily as an astringent and hemostatic
- Limited formal toxicological studies on the resin specifically; some tannin-rich preparations may reduce iron absorption with prolonged use

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (gum resin powder), Capsules, Tablets, Kwatha (decoction of bark), Lepa (topical paste), Avaleha (confection)

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6 g gum resin powder per day; 50-100 mL bark decoction twice daily; 500 mg extract capsules twice daily; topical paste as directed

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals for GI astringent effect; between meals for hemostatic action; topical application 2-3 times daily for wound healing

**Storage:** Gum resin in airtight containers at room temperature; hygroscopic, protect from moisture. Bark powder below 25 deg C. Capsules in blister packs with desiccant. Topical preparations at 15-25 deg C.

**Marker Compounds:** Lupeol, Shamimicin, Gallic acid, [Mangiferin](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#mangiferin), Bombax ceiba tannins, Beta-sitosterol, Hentriacontane, [Kaempferol](/reference/kaempferol/)
