## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Lactic acid bacteria (Lactobacillus bulgaricus, Streptococcus thermophilus)
- Lactic acid (responsible for sour taste)
- [Proteins](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-o-q/#proteins): casein, whey proteins, bioactive [peptides](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-o-q/#peptides)
- Calcium (higher bioavailability than milk due to lactic acid)
- [Vitamins](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-t-z/#vitamins): B2 ([riboflavin](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-q-t/#riboflavin)), B12, [folate](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-d-g/#folate)
- [Phosphorus](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-o-q/#phosphorus), [potassium](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-o-q/#potassium), [magnesium](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-l-o/#magnesium), zinc
- Conjugated linoleic acid (CLA)
- Exopolysaccharides (from bacterial fermentation)
- Short-chain fatty acids (produced during fermentation)
- Bacteriocins ([antimicrobial](/nighantu/reference/antimicrobial/) peptides)

## How does it work?

- Probiotic action: Live Lactobacillus cultures colonize the gut, compete with pathogenic bacteria, produce bacteriocins, and strengthen intestinal barrier function
- Digestive enhancement (Deepana): Lactic acid lowers gut pH, stimulates gastric acid and enzyme secretion, improving protein and mineral digestion
- Immunomodulation: Probiotic bacteria stimulate mucosal IgA production, activate dendritic cells and natural killer cells, and modulate T-helper cell balance
- Calcium absorption: Lactic acid chelation increases calcium bioavailability by 30-40% compared to raw milk; supports bone health

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Grahani (malabsorption/IBS): Probiotic restoration of gut flora and enhanced digestion scientifically validated as therapeutic for IBS, malabsorption, and chronic diarrhea
- Deepana-Pachana (appetizer and digestant): Lactic acid-mediated stimulation of gastric secretions and enzyme activity confirms traditional classification as a digestive stimulant
- Aruchi (anorexia/loss of appetite): Sour taste stimulates salivary and gastric secretion reflex; probiotic metabolites improve gut-brain signaling for appetite regulation

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Therapeutic use of Curd in Digestive Disorders in Ayurveda** (2023): Comprehensive review confirmed curd's therapeutic role in digestive disorders including diarrhea, IBS, constipation, and gastroenteritis; its probiotic bacteria and lactic acid normalize gut flora and enhance nutrient absorption.
- **A comprehensive review of herbs utilized in milk products of dairy industry: Insights from Ayurveda** (2024): Ayurvedic herb-infused dairy products (including Siddha Dadhi) show enhanced bioactive properties; herbal fermentation with curd cultures produces novel probiotic-phytochemical synergies.
- **An Ayurvedic Classical Review of Dadhi (Curd)** (2023): Classical texts describe Siddha Dadhi (curd processed with specific herbs) as therapeutic for different conditions; modern probiotic research validates the gut-health mechanisms described in ancient texts.

## Recent safety updates

- Ayurvedic restriction against nighttime consumption validated: heavy, sour, and hot properties may exacerbate acid reflux and disturb sleep; increases Kapha when consumed at night
- Regular excessive consumption may aggravate Rakta (blood) disorders, skin diseases, and Kapha conditions per Ayurvedic texts; modern evidence links excess dairy to acne in susceptible individuals
- Contraindicated in Hematemesis (Raktapitta), bleeding disorders, and during fever according to Ayurvedic texts
- Lactose intolerant individuals may tolerate yogurt better than milk due to bacterial lactase, but sensitivity varies

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Fresh curd, Buttermilk base, Medicated curd

**Standard Dosage:** 50–200 mL with meals

**Bioavailability:** Enhanced mineral absorption due to lactic acid fermentation

**Optimal Timing:** With lunch; avoid at night per Ayurvedic texts

**Standardized Extract:** N/A — fermented whole food

**Shelf Life:** 24–48 hours at 4°C

**Storage:** Refrigerated; ceramic or glass containers preferred

**Marker Compounds:** Lactobacillus spp., Lactic acid, Calcium, B-vitamins

**Quality Parameters:** pH 4.0–4.5, live culture count >10^6 CFU/mL, no artificial thickeners

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Vehicle for digestive and appetizer formulations
