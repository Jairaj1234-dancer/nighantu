## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Iron oxide (from calcination of ferrous sulfate)
- Residual sulfate compounds
- Trace copper and zinc
- Organic compounds from herbal processing
- Calcium and [magnesium](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#magnesium) from processing media

## How does it work?

- Enhanced iron supplementation through calcined ferrous sulfate, supporting hemoglobin synthesis
- Deepana (appetite stimulation) and Pachana (digestive fire strengthening) through iron-mediated metabolic enzyme activation in the gastrointestinal tract
- [Hepatoprotective](/reference/hepatoprotective/) activity, with [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) activity and cellular membrane stabilization, has been reported in studies of CCl4-induced liver toxicity.
- Traditionally described as Raktavardhaka (blood-enhancing), attributed to its iron content

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Iron-deficiency anemia (Pandu Roga): traditionally used for Pandu Roga. Clinical evaluations have reported improved hemoglobin and ferritin levels without the GI side effects of conventional iron.
- Liver disorders: traditionally used for liver disorders. Animal studies have reported hepatoprotective activity against CCl4-induced hepatotoxicity.
- Digestive disorders (Agnimandya, Ajirna): traditionally used for its Deepana-Pachana properties. Clinical observations have reported improved appetite and digestion.

## Recent safety updates

- PROCESSING: Classical texts hold that calcined [Kasis Bhasma](/herb/kasis-bhasma/) is less toxic than unpurified Kasisa, and that Shodhana and Marana are essential for safety.
- PREGNANCY WARNING: POSSIBLY UNSAFE due to emmenagogue effects. Not recommended during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- LONG-TERM CAUTION: Like all iron preparations, prolonged unsupervised use may cause iron overload. Periodic monitoring recommended.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Bhasma

**Standard Dosage:** 125–250 mg

**Optimal Timing:** With honey or [Triphala](/herb/triphala/) Kwatha; after meals to reduce GI irritation

**Storage:** Airtight, moisture-proof container (hygroscopic)

**Marker Compounds:** Iron oxide (Fe2O3), Sulfur compounds, Trace minerals

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Honey, [Triphala](/herb/triphala/) Kwatha, or [Mandura Bhasma](/herb/mandura-bhasma/) combination
