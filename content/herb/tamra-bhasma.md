## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Copper oxide (CuO) nanoparticles
- Copper sulfide from [Gandhaka](/nighantu/glossary/concepts-a-m/#gandhaka) processing
- Trace iron and zinc from processing
- [Silica](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-q-t/#silica) and organic residues from herbal media
- Sulfur compounds

## How does it work?

- [Hepatoprotective](/nighantu/reference/hepatoprotective/) action at therapeutic doses through [antioxidant](/nighantu/reference/antioxidant/) enzyme modulation and free radical scavenging, protecting liver cells from oxidative damage
- [Antimicrobial](/nighantu/reference/antimicrobial/) activity via copper ion release disrupting microbial cell membrane integrity and enzyme function, effective against both gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria
- Digestive fire (Agni) enhancement through stimulation of digestive enzyme secretion and improvement of metabolic processes in the GI tract
- Anti-inflammatory effect through copper-mediated modulation of inflammatory cytokines and suppression of oxidative stress pathways

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Digestive disorders (Agnimandya): Multiple clinical studies confirmed efficacy in improving digestive function with no adverse effects at therapeutic doses
- Skin diseases (Kushtha): Antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties validated through in-vitro studies supporting traditional dermatological applications
- Liver disorders (Yakrit Vikara): Hepatoprotective action demonstrated in animal models, though this is dose-dependent and reverses to hepatotoxicity at high doses

## Recent safety updates

- CRITICAL PREPARATION DEPENDENCY: Unpurified (Ashodhita) copper Bhasma is TOXIC even at therapeutic doses, causing hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, and gastric mucosal damage. Only properly purified (Shodhita) preparations should be used.
- AMRITIKARANA ESSENTIAL: The additional Amritikarana process after Marana significantly improves safety profile by removing residual impurities. Products without this step carry higher risk.
- DOSE-DEPENDENT TOXICITY: Safe at 1x and 5x therapeutic doses when properly prepared. At 10x therapeutic dose, even properly prepared Tamra Bhasma causes organ damage. Strict dose adherence essential.
- COPPER TOXICITY RISK: Excess copper can cause Wilson disease-like symptoms (hepatolenticular degeneration). Liver function monitoring recommended during prolonged use.
- HEAVY METAL CONTENT: Properly prepared Tamra Bhasma converts metallic copper to oxide form, reducing free copper ion toxicity. However, batch-to-batch variability in commercial products remains a concern.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Bhasma

**Standard Dosage:** 30–60 mg (strict dosing — copper preparation)

**Bioavailability:** Good; copper oxide nanoparticles with controlled release

**Optimal Timing:** With honey or specific anupana; after meals

**Standardized Extract:** Calcined copper bhasma

**Shelf Life:** Indefinite

**Storage:** Airtight container; avoid moisture

**Marker Compounds:** Copper oxide (CuO), Copper nanoparticles, Trace sulfur compounds

**Quality Parameters:** Copper content >60%, free copper <1 ppm (critical), standard bhasma tests, Nischandratva

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Honey, [Triphala](/nighantu/herb/triphala/) Kwatha, or as part of specific formulations
