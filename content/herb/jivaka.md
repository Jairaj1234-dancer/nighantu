## How does it work?

- Anti-inflammatory mechanism involving inhibition of pro-inflammatory enzyme pathways (COX-2, 5-LOX) and cytokine production, as demonstrated in recent metabolite profiling studies
- [Antioxidant](/nighantu/reference/antioxidant/) and photoprotective action through free radical scavenging and UV-damage protection, mediated by phenolic and flavonoid constituents
- Rejuvenating and [adaptogenic](/nighantu/reference/adaptogenic/) mechanism through modulation of stress response pathways and enhancement of cellular energy metabolism

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Anti-inflammatory and photoprotective properties validated through in vitro enzyme inhibition assays, supporting traditional topical applications
- Rejuvenating ([Rasayana](/nighantu/reference/rasayana/)) role partially validated through antioxidant profiling showing potent free radical scavenging activity
- [Aphrodisiac](/nighantu/glossary/pharmacology/#aphrodisiac) traditional use indirectly supported by adaptogenic properties demonstrated in related species of Ashtavarga

## Recent safety updates

- Traditionally used safely as an Ashtavarga component in [Chyawanprash](/nighantu/formulation/chyawanprash/) and other classical formulations; no documented toxicity in Ayurvedic literature
- Critically endangered orchid species with severe habitat loss; most commercial products contain substitutes, making safety evaluation of actual Jivaka specimens difficult

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (tuber powder), Kashayam (decoction), Capsule, Avaleha (confection), [Chyawanprash](/nighantu/formulation/chyawanprash/) ingredient

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6g tuber powder twice daily; 50-100ml decoction; as per [Chyawanprash](/nighantu/formulation/chyawanprash/) dosage

**Bioavailability:** Microstylis wallichii (syn. Malaxis acuminata) tuber is one of the Ashtavarga (eight rare Ayurvedic herbs). Contains mucilage, glucosides, and [starch](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-q-t/#starch). Limited modern pharmacokinetic data. Mucilaginous polysaccharides act locally in GI tract with demulcent properties. Glucoside components likely have moderate oral absorption after enzymatic hydrolysis.

**Optimal Timing:** With warm milk or ghee as Rasayana (rejuvenative tonic); as part of Chyawanprash, 1-2 teaspoons daily

**Standardized Extract:** No standardized extract established due to rarity. Aqueous extract with HPTLC/DNA barcode authentication if authentic material obtained.

**Shelf Life:** 2 years (dried tuber powder); 2 years (capsule)

**Storage:** Cool, dry place below 25°C, protected from moisture (mucilaginous material is hygroscopic). Airtight containers.

**Marker Compounds:** Glucosides (general profiling), Mucilage polysaccharides, DNA barcode authentication (ITS/matK regions) is primary identification method

### Extraction Methods

- Aqueous decoction (traditional)
- Hydroalcoholic extraction (50:50)
- Traditional processing as part of Ashtavarga for Chyawanprash
