## How does it work?

- Bioavailability enhancement: Lipophilic medium enhances absorption of fat-soluble drugs and herbs; Siddha Ghrita preparations increase bioavailability of active phytochemicals across the blood-brain barrier
- Anti-inflammatory via butyrate: Butyric acid inhibits NF-kB pathway, reduces inflammatory cytokines, strengthens intestinal barrier integrity, and supports healthy gut microbiome
- [Neuroprotective](/nighantu/reference/neuroprotective/): Butyrate increases brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), promotes neurogenesis, and reduces neuroinflammation; fat-soluble vitamin content supports myelin health
- Digestive fire (Agni) enhancement: Short and medium-chain fatty acids are rapidly absorbed and metabolized, stimulating hepatic bile secretion and pancreatic enzyme activity

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Medhya [Rasayana](/nighantu/reference/rasayana/) (cognitive enhancement): Butyrate-mediated BDNF increase and fat-soluble vitamin transport validate traditional use of medicated ghees (Saraswata Ghrita, [Brahmi](/nighantu/herb/brahmi/) Ghrita) for cognitive improvement
- Agni Deepana without Pitta aggravation: Rapid metabolism of short/medium-chain fatty acids stimulates digestion while cooling virya prevents inflammatory overactivation - unique dual mechanism validated
- Snehana (oleation therapy in Panchakarma): Lipophilic drug delivery and membrane fluidity enhancement scientifically support ghee's role as the primary internal oleation medium

## Recent safety updates

- Moderate consumption (1-2 tablespoons/day) shows neutral cardiovascular effects; excessive intake increases LDL cholesterol and cardiovascular risk
- Patients with hyperlipidemia, obesity, or existing cardiovascular disease should limit ghee intake; Ayurvedic contraindications for Kapha disorders align with modern guidance
- Ghee has high smoke point (250 degrees C) making it safe for cooking; does not generate trans fats or acrolein at normal cooking temperatures
- Quality depends on source (grass-fed cow ghee preferred); commercial ghee may contain added vegetable oils or be from grain-fed cattle with altered fatty acid profile

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Plain ghee, Medicated ghee (Siddha Ghrita), Panchagavya component

**Standard Dosage:** 5–10 mL orally; variable in Snehapana (oleation therapy, up to 200 mL)

**Bioavailability:** Excellent lipid-based vehicle; enhances absorption of lipophilic compounds 3–5x

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals for Snehana; with meals for nutritive purposes

**Standardized Extract:** N/A — used as base/vehicle for medicated ghees

**Shelf Life:** 12–24 months if properly prepared (moisture <0.5%)

**Storage:** Room temperature in airtight container; avoid moisture ingress

**Marker Compounds:** Butyric acid, Conjugated linoleic acid, Fat-soluble vitamins A/D/E/K, Short-chain fatty acids

**Quality Parameters:** Moisture <0.5%, FFA <1%, peroxide value <5 meq/kg, no rancidity

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Premier lipophilic vehicle (Sneha) in Ayurveda; used for >100 medicated ghee preparations

### Extraction Methods

- Traditional: slow heating of butter (Navaneetam) until moisture evaporates and milk solids settle
- Modern: centrifugal clarification at 110–120°C

### Stability Notes

Anhydrous preparation; stable if moisture kept below 0.5%. Rancidity indicates degradation.
