## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Short-chain fatty acids: butyric acid (3-4%), caproic acid
- Medium-chain fatty acids: caprylic, capric acid
- Long-chain fatty acids: oleic acid (25%), palmitic, stearic, myristic
- Conjugated linoleic acid (CLA)
- Fat-soluble [vitamins](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#vitamins): A, D, E, K2
- Cholesterol (0.3%)
- Sphingolipids and [phospholipids](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#phospholipids)
- [Carotenoids](/glossary/compounds-a-c/#carotenoids) (beta-carotene)
- Phenolic antioxidants (from Murchhana process)
- Trace minerals ([selenium](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#selenium), zinc)

## How does it work?

- Bioavailability enhancement: Lipophilic medium enhances absorption of fat-soluble drugs and herbs
- Anti-inflammatory via butyrate: Butyric acid inhibits NF-kB pathway, reduces inflammatory cytokines, strengthens intestinal barrier integrity, and supports healthy gut microbiome
- [Neuroprotective](/reference/neuroprotective/): Butyrate increases brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), promotes neurogenesis, and reduces neuroinflammation; fat-soluble vitamin content supports myelin health
- Digestive fire (Agni) enhancement: Short and medium-chain fatty acids are rapidly absorbed and metabolized, stimulating hepatic bile secretion and pancreatic enzyme activity

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Medhya [Rasayana](/reference/rasayana/) (cognitive enhancement): medicated ghees such as Saraswata Ghrita and [Brahmi](/herb/brahmi/) Ghrita are traditionally used to support cognition.
- Agni Deepana without Pitta aggravation: traditionally used to stimulate digestion. Its cooling virya is traditionally held to keep this from aggravating Pitta.
- Snehana (oleation therapy in Panchakarma): used in classical practice as the primary medium for internal oleation.

## Recent safety updates

- Moderate consumption (1-2 tablespoons/day) shows neutral cardiovascular effects; excessive intake increases LDL cholesterol and cardiovascular risk
- Patients with hyperlipidemia, obesity, or existing cardiovascular disease should limit ghee intake; Ayurvedic contraindications for Kapha disorders align with modern guidance
- Ghee has high smoke point (250 degrees C)
- Quality depends on source (grass-fed cow ghee preferred); commercial ghee may contain added vegetable oils or be from grain-fed cattle with altered fatty acid profile

## What is it made of?

### Nutritional Profile

- **Fat:** 99.5g/100g
- **Saturated fatty acids:** 62%
- **Monounsaturated fatty acids:** 29%
- **Butyric acid:** 3-4%
- **Conjugated linoleic acid (CLA):** 0.3-0.5%
- **Vitamin A:** 3069 IU/100g
- **Vitamin E:** 2.8mg/100g
- **Vitamin K2:** Present

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Plain ghee, Medicated ghee (Siddha Ghrita), Panchagavya component

**Standard Dosage:** 5–10 mL orally; variable in Snehapana (oleation therapy, up to 200 mL)

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals for Snehana; with meals for nutritive purposes

**Storage:** Room temperature in airtight container; avoid moisture ingress

**Marker Compounds:** Butyric acid, Conjugated linoleic acid, Fat-soluble vitamins A/D/E/K, Short-chain fatty acids

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Premier lipophilic vehicle (Sneha) in Ayurveda; used for >100 medicated ghee preparations

### Stability Notes

Anhydrous preparation; stable if moisture kept below 0.5%. Rancidity indicates degradation.
