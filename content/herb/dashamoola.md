## How is it used traditionally?

One of the most important compound formulations. Five large-tree roots (Brihat Panchamoola) balance Vata-Kapha; five small-plant roots (Laghu Panchamoola) add anti-inflammatory action. Used for Shula (pain), Shotha (inflammation), and Sutika Roga (post-natal disorders).

## Where is it described in the classical texts?

- Charaka Samhita
- Sushruta Samhita
- Ashtanga Hridaya
- Sharangadhara Samhita

## Recommended Dosage

Kwath (decoction): 50-100ml twice daily; Powder: 3-6g/day

## Safety, contraindications and cautions

- Pregnancy (certain components)
- Pitta conditions in excess

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Kashayam (decoction), Kwath Ghana (solid decoction extract), Churna (powder), Tablet, Capsule, Taila (medicated oil), [Ghrita](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/ghrita/) (medicated ghee), Aristha (fermented preparation)

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6g powder twice daily; 50-100ml Kashayam twice daily; 500-1000mg extract tablet/capsule twice daily; Taila for external application as directed

**Bioavailability:** Dashamoola is a polyherbal combination of 10 roots (5 Brihat and 5 Laghu), providing a diverse phytochemical matrix. The combined [alkaloids](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids), [flavonoids](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/flavonoids/), and [terpenoids](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-t-z/#terpenoids) show variable individual bioavailabilities. The traditional decoction form (Kashayam) provides good dissolution and gastric absorption of water-soluble constituents. Ghrita (ghee) preparation enhances absorption of lipophilic terpenoids. The polyherbal matrix may provide natural bioenhancement through constituent interactions. [Piperine](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/piperine/) co-administration not traditionally indicated but may enhance absorption of specific alkaloids.

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals with warm water for Vata disorders; Taila application followed by steam (Swedana) for musculoskeletal conditions

**Standardized Extract:** Kwath Ghana (spray-dried aqueous decoction concentrate), extract ratio 8:1 to 12:1. Standardized by HPTLC fingerprint matching reference standards for all 10 components. Total alkaloid content NLT 0.5%.

**Shelf Life:** 2 years (powder/Kwath Ghana); 3 years (tablet); 3 years (Taila); 5-10 years (Aristha, due to self-generated alcohol preservation)

**Storage:** Powder and solid extracts: airtight containers below 25 deg C, protected from moisture. Taila: amber glass bottles, room temperature. Kashayam (liquid): refrigerate and use within 72 hours, or add permitted preservative for 6-month shelf life.

**Marker Compounds:** Desmodianone (from Shalaparni), [Lupeol](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/lupeol/) (from [Bilva](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/bilva/)/[Patala](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/patala/)), [Aegeline](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-1-a/#aegeline) (from Bilva), Paedalin (from [Shyonaka](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/shyonaka/)), Beta-[sitosterol](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol) (common across roots), Ursolic acid (from [Gambhari](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/gambhari/))

### Extraction Methods

- Traditional Kashayam: coarse powder boiled in 16 parts water, reduced to 1/4th
- Hydroalcoholic extraction (60:40)
- Sequential aqueous extraction for Kwath Ghana (spray-dried)
- Taila Paka: traditional oil processing with Kalka and Kwath in sesame/[coconut oil](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-a-m/#coconut-oil)

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Guggulu](/ayurveda-atlas/formulation/guggulu/) for musculoskeletal inflammation (Dashamoola Guggulu)
- With [Eranda](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/eranda/) Taila for Vata disorders and joint pain
- With [Bala](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/bala/) (Sida cordifolia) for neuromuscular conditions
- With [Ashwagandha](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/ashwagandha/) for comprehensive Vata pacification
- With [Rasna](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/rasna/) (Pluchea lanceolata) for enhanced anti-inflammatory action
