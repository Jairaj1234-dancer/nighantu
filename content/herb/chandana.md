## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- alpha-Santalol (41-55% of essential oil - primary bioactive sesquiterpene alcohol)
- beta-Santalol (16-24% of essential oil)
- epi-beta-Santalol
- alpha-exo-Bergamotol
- alpha-Santalen and beta-Santalen (sesquiterpene hydrocarbons ~6%)
- Santalic acid
- Teresantalol
- cis-Nuciferol

## How does it work?

- alpha-Santalol COX-2 inhibition: Directly inhibits cyclooxygenase-2 enzyme, reducing prostaglandin E2 synthesis and inflammatory response in skin
- MMP-1 collagenase suppression: Sandalwood oil inhibits MMP-1 expression induced by environmental pollutants and UV, protecting dermal collagen matrix from degradation
- ROS scavenging and [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) defense: Sesquiterpene alcohols neutralize free radicals generated by oxidative stress, UV radiation, and pollution, protecting skin cell membranes and mitochondria
- Anti-proliferative epidermal regulation: alpha-Santalol modulates hyperproliferative epidermal keratinocyte activity relevant to psoriasis and wart conditions

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Complexion enhancement (Varnya): traditionally used to enhance complexion. Clinical studies have reported skin brightening, anti-wrinkle and anti-aging effects. Antioxidant activity has also been reported.
- Anti-inflammatory skin soothing (Daha-prashamana): traditionally used for soothing inflamed skin. Laboratory and clinical studies have reported anti-inflammatory activity of alpha-santalol in the context of eczema and dermatitis.
- [Antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) wound care: traditionally used in wound care. Laboratory studies have reported broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity against acne-causing bacteria and skin pathogens, including S. aureus.

## Recent safety updates

- Contact sensitization rate is low (0.1-2.4% in routine patch testing), but allergic contact dermatitis to sandalwood oil has been reported; patch testing recommended before widespread use
- ISO 3518:2002 quality standards ensure authenticity - adulteration with synthetic santalol or other Santalum species (S. spicatum) may alter safety profile. Only S. album oil meets pharmacological standards

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder), Taila (oil), Lepa (paste), Kashayam (decoction), Capsule, Tablet, Chandanasava (fermented), Chandanadi Vati

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6g heartwood powder; 5-15 drops essential oil (external); 500mg extract capsule; 15-30ml Chandanasava; paste externally as needed

**Optimal Timing:** Paste applied morning and evening for skin care; internal use after meals with cool water for Pitta conditions; Chandanasava after meals; oil for aromatherapy anytime

**Storage:** Essential oil in amber glass with tight closure (oil improves with age if properly stored). Powder in airtight containers. Cool, dry conditions. Heartwood blocks can be stored indefinitely in dry conditions.

**Marker Compounds:** Alpha-santalol, Beta-santalol, Santalic acid, Teresantalol, Alpha-santalene, Santene

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Ushira](/herb/ushira/) (Vetiver) for cooling and Pitta-pacifying formulations
- With Kumkuma ([saffron](/herb/saffron/)) for complexion-enhancing preparations
- With [Yashtimadhu](/herb/yashtimadhu/) for urinary tract health
- With [Gokshura](/herb/gokshura/) in urinary formulations
- With [Manjishtha](/herb/manjishtha/) for skin radiance formulations
