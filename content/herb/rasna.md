- 1'-Acetoxychavicol acetate (ACA, Alpinia)
- Beta-[sitosterol](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol)

## How does it work?

- Anti-inflammatory: Pluchine and [quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/) inhibit COX-2 and 5-LOX [enzymes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes), reducing prostaglandin and leukotriene synthesis; ACA (from Alpinia) suppresses NF-kB activation
- Anti-arthritic: [Triterpenoids](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#triterpenoids) and [flavonoids](/reference/flavonoids/) reduce synovial inflammation and cartilage degradation by inhibiting MMP-3 and MMP-9
- [Immunomodulatory](/reference/immunomodulatory/): Taraxasterol and [quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/) modulate macrophage cytokine production, balancing Th1/Th2 immune responses

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Amavata: traditionally used for rheumatic and arthritic conditions. Animal studies have reported reduced inflammation markers and joint edema.
- Vedana-sthapana: traditionally used as an [Analgesic](/reference/analgesic/). Studies using hot plate and tail flick assays have reported reduced pain responses.
- Used in classical practice as a [Rasayana](/reference/rasayana/). Studies have reported immunomodulatory activity, including enhanced phagocytic function and antibody response.

## Recent safety updates

- The source notes report no significant toxicity for Pluchea lanceolata in animal studies; the studies are not named
- Alpinia galanga preparations may cause mild gastric irritation in sensitive individuals; ensure correct botanical identity to avoid substitution-related safety issues

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (root/leaf powder), Kwatha (decoction - Rasnadi Kwatha, Maharasnadi Kwatha), Capsule, Tablet, Taila (medicated oil - Rasnadi Taila), [Ghrita](/herb/ghrita/), [Guggulu](/formulation/guggulu/) combination

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6 g powder daily; 20-30 mL decoction twice daily; 500 mg - 1 g standardized extract twice daily; Taila for external massage

**Bioavailability:** Pluchea lanceolata (Rasna) contains flavonoids and [terpenoids](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#terpenoids) with moderate oral bioavailability (20-35%). [Quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/) and isorhamnetin [glycosides](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides) are absorbed in the small intestine. Taraxasterol and other [triterpenes](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#triterpenes) have limited oral bioavailability (~10-20%) improved by lipid-based delivery. Taila is traditionally applied externally for joint and muscle complaints.

**Optimal Timing:** Decoction: morning and evening between meals with warm water. Taila: warm oil massage before bath or at bedtime. Guggulu combination: after meals with warm water.

**Standardized Extract:** Rasna root extract (6:1) standardized to total flavonoids >4% (as quercetin), taraxasterol >0.5%. Maharasnadi Kwatha: standardized decoction with validated HPTLC multi-herb fingerprint.

**Shelf Life:** Dried root/leaf: 18-24 months; Powder: 18-24 months; Extract: 24 months; Taila: 24-36 months; Guggulu formulation: 24-36 months

**Storage:** Dried material: airtight containers, below 25 deg C, RH <55%, protected from pest infestation. Taila: amber glass, room temperature. Kwatha concentrate: refrigerated, use within 48 hours. Tablets: standard pharma storage below 25 deg C.

**Marker Compounds:** Quercetin, Isorhamnetin, Taraxasterol, Moretenol, Pluchein, Beta-sitosterol, Neolupenol, [Stigmasterol](/reference/stigmasterol/)

### Extraction Methods

- Aqueous decoction (traditional Kwatha)
- Hydroalcoholic extraction (50-60% ethanol)
- Traditional Taila Paka (oil preparation with [sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila) base)
- Methanol extraction for flavonoid profiling
- Cold maceration in water for 12-24 hours
