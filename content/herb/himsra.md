## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Glucocapparin (major aliphatic glucosinolate)
- [Rutin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#rutin) and [quercetin](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/quercetinquercetin/) ([flavonoids](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/flavonoids/))
- Spermidine [alkaloids](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) (in roots)
- Indole-3-acetonitrile (indole alkaloid)
- [Stachydrine](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#stachydrine) (proline [betaine](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-a-c/#betaine))
- [Tocopherols](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-t-z/#tocopherols) and [carotenoids](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-a-c/#carotenoids)

## How does it work?

- [Hepatoprotective](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/hepatoprotective/) activity via rutin and [quercetin](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/quercetinquercetin/)-mediated [antioxidant](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antioxidant/) protection and anti-inflammatory action - key ingredient in Liv.52 formulation
- [Antidiabetic](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antidiabetic/) effect through reduction of fasting blood [glucose](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-d-g/#glucose), HbA1c, and triglycerides (clinically validated at 400 mg TID for 2 months)
- Anti-inflammatory mechanism via glucosinolate-derived isothiocyanate modulation of NF-kB and MAPK pathways
- [Neuroprotective](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/neuroprotective/) activity through antioxidant and anti-inflammatory protection of neuronal cells

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Hepatoprotective (Yakrit-hara) - strongly validated as key ingredient in Liv.52; clinical studies confirm protection against drug-induced and alcoholic liver damage
- Antidiabetic (Prameha-hara) - validated through clinical trial showing significant blood glucose and HbA1c reduction
- Anti-inflammatory and [analgesic](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/analgesic/) (Vedana-sthapana) - confirmed through multiple preclinical anti-inflammatory models
- Digestive stimulant (Rochana) - traditional appetizer use validated through clinical observations of improved appetite

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Capparis spinosa: A rich source of phenolic compounds - comprehensive review of phytochemistry, health benefits, and biotechnological applications** (2024): Comprehensive review establishing caper as a rich source of phenolic compounds with documented antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective, and neuroprotective properties.
- **Capparis L. (Capparaceae): A Scoping Review of Phytochemistry, Ethnopharmacology and Pharmacological Activities** (2025): Scoping review covering the entire genus documenting the diversity of bioactive compounds and their validated pharmacological activities including antidiabetic and [anticancer](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/anticancer/) potential.
- **Caper bush adaptation to harsh soils: bioactive compounds and antioxidant capacity** (2025): Demonstrated that caper plants adapted to harsh soil conditions maintain high levels of bioactive compounds including [glucosinolates](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-d-g/#glucosinolates), phenolics, and flavonoids with strong antioxidant capacity.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally recognized as safe for culinary and medicinal use; clinical trial at 400 mg TID for 2 months showed no adverse effects on liver or kidney function
- May cause allergic contact dermatitis in sensitive individuals from fresh plant sap; excessive consumption of raw buds may cause bloating

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder), Tablet, Capsule, Kashayam (decoction), Syrup, Standardized extract

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6g powder twice daily; 250-500mg extract twice daily; 15-30ml decoction

**Bioavailability:** Capparis spinosa (caper bush) contains flavonoids (rutin, [quercetin](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/quercetinquercetin/)) and alkaloids (stachydrine) with moderate oral bioavailability. Rutin bioavailability is enhanced when deglycosylated to quercetin by gut microbiota. Stachydrine shows good water solubility and absorption. Lipid-based formulations may enhance flavonoid absorption.

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals with water for hepatoprotective action; twice daily for chronic liver support

**Standardized Extract:** Hydroalcoholic extract (1:4 drug-extract ratio) standardized to minimum 0.5% rutin and 0.1% stachydrine; aqueous extract as used in classical Ayurvedic liver formulations

**Shelf Life:** 2 years (powder); 3 years (standardized extract tablet/capsule); 2 years (syrup)

**Storage:** Cool, dry place below 25°C, protected from light. Flavonoids degrade in light; amber containers recommended.

**Marker Compounds:** Rutin, Quercetin, Stachydrine, p-Methoxy benzoic acid, [Kaempferol](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/kaempferol/), Beta-[sitosterol](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol)

### Extraction Methods

- Hydroalcoholic extraction (70:30 ethanol:water)
- Aqueous extraction
- Standardized aqueous-ethanolic extract (used in Liv.52 formulation)
- Supercritical CO2 for alkaloid-rich fraction

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Kasani](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/kasani/) (Cichorium) for hepatoprotective synergy (as in Liv.52)
- With Bhringaraj for liver regeneration
- With [Katuki](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/katuki/) (Picrorhiza) for cholagogue activity
- With [Kalmegh](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/kalmegh/) for hepatic protection
