## How does it work?

- AChE inhibition: Significantly inhibits acetylcholinesterase activity in cortex and hippocampus, increasing acetylcholine retention in synapses.
- Multi-target neuroprotection: Active compounds interact with PTGS1/2, NOS3, INSR, HMOX1, ACHE, PPARG, MAOA, and MAOB molecular targets.
- PPARgamma activation: 4-hydroxycinnamic acid and [scopoletin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#scopoletin) activate PPARgamma signaling to ameliorate cognitive deficits.
- Tau protein modulation: Ameliorates hMAPtau-induced neurotoxicity, extending lifespan in Alzheimer's Drosophila models.
- [Antioxidant](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antioxidant/) activity: Enhances antioxidant enzyme activities, reducing oxidative stress in neural tissue.
- Synaptic plasticity modulation: Modulates synaptic plasticity in hippocampal neurons.
- Dual mechanism: Exerts [nootropic](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/pharmacology/#nootropic) effects through combined anti-AChE and antioxidant action.
- Key active constituents: Scopoletin, [quercetin](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/quercetinquercetin/), 4-hydroxycinnamic acid, ayapanin, convolamine, and [kaempferol](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/kaempferol/).

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Role of Shankhpushpi in neurological disorders: An umbrella review covering evidence from ethnopharmacology to clinical studies** (2022): Comprehensive umbrella review validated ethnomedicinal uses ([antispasmodic](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/pharmacology/#antispasmodic), [analgesic](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/analgesic/), anti-inflammatory, anti-anxiety, memory-boosting) through neuropharmacological studies. Highlighted need for multicentric clinical trials across diverse populations.
- **Protective Mechanisms of Nootropic Herb Shankhpushpi against Dementia: Network Pharmacology and Computational Approach** (2022): Network pharmacology identified scopoletin as having highest binding affinity with PTGS1, NOS3, PPARG, ACHE, MAOA, MAOB, and TRKB molecular targets. [Quercetin](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/quercetinquercetin/) showed highest affinity with PTGS2. These compounds ameliorate cognitive deficits through PPARgamma signaling activation.
- **Novel insights on acetylcholinesterase inhibition by Convolvulus pluricaulis in zebrafish** (2022): Zebrafish model demonstrated that C. pluricaulis treatment inhibits AChE activity comparable to standard AChE inhibitors. CP was superior as evidenced by retention of avoidance response behavior in adult zebrafish.
- **Therapeutic mechanism of Convolvulus pluricaulis against Alzheimer's disease: Network pharmacology and molecular dynamics approach** (2024): Molecular dynamics simulations identified key active compounds (scopoletin, 4-hydroxycinnamic acid, ayapanin) that interact with Alzheimer's-associated molecular targets, providing mechanistic basis for traditional nootropic use.
- **Shankhpushpi: Exploring its Cognitive Enhancing Mechanisms and Therapeutic Potential in Neurodegenerative Disorders** (2023): Demonstrated that C. pluricaulis modulates synaptic plasticity in rat brain hippocampus and ameliorates human microtubule-associated protein tau (hMAPtau) induced neurotoxicity in Alzheimer's Drosophila model, extending lifespan.

## Recent safety updates

- LD50 determined at 1250 mg/kg (range 1000-1500 mg/kg) for whole extract, indicating a relatively high safety margin at therapeutic doses.
- Critical drug interaction: Reduces antiepileptic activity of phenytoin and lowers plasma phenytoin levels. Multiple-dose administration significantly impairs phenytoin efficacy.
- May interact with antidepressants, antipsychotics, and other neurotransmitter-affecting medications.
- Potential interaction with blood pressure medications due to mild hypotensive effect.
- Possible interaction with sedatives and CNS depressants due to sedative-hypnotic properties.
- Side effects uncommon; mild nausea possible due to bitter taste.
- Cross-allergenicity possible in individuals allergic to Convolvulaceae family plants (e.g., sweet potato).
- People with low blood pressure should use with caution; consult physician before use during pregnancy.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Syrup, Churna (powder), Capsule, Tablet, Kwatha (decoction), Swarasa (fresh juice), Ghana vati, Drops (for children)

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6 g powder per day; 10-20 mL syrup twice daily; 500-1000 mg extract capsule twice daily; 5-10 mL swarasa twice daily; children: 2.5-5 mL syrup twice daily

**Bioavailability:** Flavonoid [glycosides](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides) and [triterpenoids](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-t-z/#triterpenoids) show moderate oral bioavailability (25-40%). Shankhapushpine (alkaloid) is well-absorbed orally. The traditional Medhya [Rasayana](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/rasayana/) preparation with ghee significantly enhances lipophilic constituent absorption (1.5-2x improvement). Scopoletin shows good oral bioavailability (40-55%).

**Optimal Timing:** Morning and evening for cognitive benefits; traditionally taken with ghee or milk. Before exams/study sessions for students (traditional use).

**Standardized Extract:** Standardized whole plant extract of Convolvulus pluricaulis (10:1), NLT 5% total [flavonoids](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/flavonoids/) (as kaempferol equivalents by HPLC); NLT 0.3% scopoletin by HPLC; HPTLC fingerprint matching reference

**Shelf Life:** 12-18 months for syrup; 24 months for churna; 36 months for standardized extract capsules/tablets

**Storage:** Syrup: below 25 deg C, refrigerate after opening. Capsules and churna: airtight containers below 25 deg C, protected from light and moisture.

**Marker Compounds:** Shankhapushpine, Scopoletin, Kaempferol, Convolvuline, Beta-[sitosterol](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol), Microphyllol, 20-Oxodotriacontanol

### Extraction Methods

- Aqueous extraction (traditional)

- Cold pressing for swarasa
- Extraction in ghee (traditional [Ghrita](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/ghrita/) preparation)
- UAE (ultrasound-assisted) for improved flavonoid yield
