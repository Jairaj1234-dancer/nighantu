## How does it work?

- Glucosinolate metabolites (benzyl isothiocyanate, sulforaphane) activate Nrf2-ARE [antioxidant](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antioxidant/) pathway, upregulating phase II detoxification [enzymes](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes) and providing chemopreventive effects
- Mucilage polysaccharides form protective gel layer on gastrointestinal mucosa, reducing gastric acid damage and providing gastroprotective and [laxative](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/laxative/) effects
- Lepidine alkaloids and [isothiocyanates](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-g-l/#isothiocyanates) exhibit [galactagogue](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/pharmacology/#galactagogue) activity through modulation of prolactin secretion pathways, enhancing milk production in lactating women

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Galactagogue (milk-promoting) activity validated through traditional use documentation and preliminary pharmacological evidence supporting Ayurvedic use as Stanyajanana (milk-promoting herb)
- Bone strengthening and fracture healing validated through preclinical studies showing enhanced calcium absorption and osteoblast activity, supporting traditional use for Asthi-Sandhaniya (bone healing) in Ayurveda
- Antiasthmatic and [expectorant](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/expectorant/) effects confirmed in preclinical models supporting traditional use for Shwasa (asthma) and Kasa (cough)

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Nutritional composition, nutraceutical aspects, and medicinal benefits of garden cress (Lepidium sativum) seeds - A geographical and processing perspective** (2024): Comprehensive analysis of bioactive compounds showing significant variation based on geographic origin and processing methods, with documented antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and bone-strengthening properties.
- **Research Update on the Therapeutic Potential of Garden Cress (Lepidium sativum Linn.) with Threatened Status** (2024): Updated review documenting diverse pharmacological activities including galactagogue, anticarcinogenic, [antidiabetic](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antidiabetic/), antiasthmatic, and antidiarrheal properties with emphasis on need for human clinical trials.
- **Anti-inflammatory and [Immunomodulatory](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/immunomodulatory/) Properties of Lepidium sativum** (2022): Garden cress seed extract significantly inhibited carrageenan-induced paw edema, reduced yeast-induced hyperpyrexia, and demonstrated dose-dependent [analgesic](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/analgesic/) activity.

## Recent safety updates

- Acute and subchronic toxicity studies in Wistar rats (14 weeks, 1-10% dietary incorporation) showed no mortality, no significant changes in body weight, organ weight, or hematological parameters - considered non-toxic and safe
- Majority of studies are animal-based; there is a critical need for human clinical efficacy and safety trials
- Potential goitrogenic effects due to glucosinolate content; caution in patients with thyroid disorders; avoid high doses during pregnancy due to potential uterotonic effects

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (seed powder), Capsule, Tablet, Modaka (sweetmeat), Laddoo (traditional preparation), Oil

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6g seed powder twice daily with warm milk; 500mg extract capsule; seeds soaked in water overnight and consumed; seed oil 5-10ml

**Bioavailability:** Lepidium sativum (garden cress) seed [glucosinolates](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-d-g/#glucosinolates) require [myrosinase](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-l-o/#myrosinase) enzyme activation (released upon crushing/chewing) to form bioactive isothiocyanates. Isothiocyanates show rapid oral absorption (>80%). Iron content (5-6mg/100g) is moderately bioavailable; ascorbic acid co-administration enhances iron absorption. Mucilage content (20-25%) provides sustained release of embedded compounds. Omega-3 fatty acids (alpha-linolenic acid: 28-32% of seed oil) are well absorbed.

**Optimal Timing:** With warm milk after meals for bone health; traditional Laddoo postpartum with meals; soaked seeds early morning for general health; oil with meals

**Standardized Extract:** Seed extract standardized to total glucosinolates NLT 3%; seed oil with alpha-linolenic acid NLT 28%. Iron content specification: NLT 5mg/100g seed.

**Shelf Life:** 2 years (whole seeds); 1 year (seed powder); 1 year (oil, nitrogen-flushed); 3 months (Laddoo)

**Storage:** Whole seeds in airtight containers (very stable). Powder susceptible to moisture absorption due to mucilage—airtight with desiccant. Oil in amber glass, refrigerated after opening. Laddoo in airtight containers, consume within 3 months.

**Marker Compounds:** Glucotropaeolin (benzyl glucosinolate), Benzyl isothiocyanate, Alpha-linolenic acid, Linoleic acid, Tocopherols, Beta-[sitosterol](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol), [Campesterol](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-a-c/#campesterol), Iron, Calcium

### Extraction Methods

- Mechanical grinding of seeds for powder
- Cold pressing for seed oil (oil yield: 20-25%)
- Aqueous soaking for mucilage extraction
- Hydroalcoholic extraction (60:40) for glucosinolate-rich fractions
- Supercritical CO2 for high-quality seed oil

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Ashwagandha](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/ashwagandha/) for bone health and strength building
- With [Shatavari](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/shatavari/) for galactagogue (lactation) support
- With [Methi](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/methi/) (fenugreek) for postpartum recovery formulations
- With Jaggery and ghee in traditional Laddoo for iron supplementation
- With Til (sesame) for calcium and bone health
