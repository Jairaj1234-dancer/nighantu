## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- [Cucurbitacins](/glossary/compounds-c-d/#cucurbitacins) (cucurbitacin B, D, E)
- Flavone C-[glycosides](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides) and beta-glycosides
- [Triterpenoids](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#triterpenoids) and [sterols](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sterols)
- [Pectin](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#pectin) and dietary soluble fibers
- Beta-carotene and ascorbic acid
- [Saponins](/reference/saponins/)

## How does it work?

- [Cardioprotective](/reference/cardioprotective/) activity through reduction of serum lipids and modulation of cardiac enzyme markers
- [Hepatoprotective](/reference/hepatoprotective/) mechanism via [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) enzyme restoration and reduction of lipid peroxidation
- Anti-inflammatory activity through inhibition of COX and LOX pathways by [flavonoids](/reference/flavonoids/)
- Hypoglycemic effect via alpha-glucosidase inhibition and enhanced insulin sensitivity

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Cardiotonic and heart-protective (Hridya): traditionally used to support the heart. Preclinical studies have reported reductions in cholesterol and triglycerides.
- Cooling and [diuretic](/reference/diuretic/) (Sheetala, Mutrala): traditionally used as a cooling and diuretic herb. Pharmacological studies have reported diuretic activity.
- Hepatoprotective (Yakrit-shodhana): traditionally used to protect the liver. Animal studies have reported protection against drug-induced liver toxicity.
- Obesity: traditionally used for weight reduction. Several studies have reported antihyperlipidemic and anti-obesity effects.

## Recent safety updates

- Bitter variety of bottle gourd (containing high cucurbitacin levels) is toxic and has caused fatalities; only the non-bitter edible variety should be used medicinally
- Fresh juice consumption in excess can cause gastrointestinal irritation, nausea, and vomiting; moderate consumption is generally safe
- Bitter bottle gourd juice has been linked to cases of hematemesis and shock; taste-testing before consumption is strongly advised

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Swarasa (fresh juice), Churna (dried powder), Capsule, Pinda (bolus for Pinda Sweda), Lehyam (confection)

**Standard Dosage:** 20-50ml fresh juice twice daily; 3-6g dried powder; as dietary vegetable (ad libitum)

**Optimal Timing:** Fresh juice traditionally taken early morning on an empty stomach. Avoid at night (diuretic effect).

**Storage:** Fresh juice must be consumed within hours; refrigerate if necessary (2-8°C). Dried powder in airtight containers, cool and dry place.

**Marker Compounds:** Cucurbitacin B (below safety limit), Cucurbitacin D (below safety limit), Beta-[sitosterol](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol), Saponins, Vitamin C, [Potassium](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#potassium) content

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Arjuna](/herb/arjuna/) for cardioprotective action
- With [Punarnava](/herb/punarnava/) for diuretic effect
- With [Guduchi](/herb/guduchi/) for hepatoprotective support
- With [Gokshura](/herb/gokshura/) for urinary tract health
