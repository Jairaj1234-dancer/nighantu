## How does it work?

- Anti-inflammatory action via triterpenoid-mediated (betulinic acid) inhibition of 5-LOX and COX-2 pathways
- [Anticancer](/nighantu/reference/anticancer/) activity through betulinic acid-induced mitochondrial apoptosis and caspase cascade activation in cancer cells
- [Anxiolytic](/nighantu/reference/anxiolytic/) and anticonvulsant effects via GABAergic receptor modulation by bark alkaloid fractions
- Oral health protection through [antimicrobial](/nighantu/reference/antimicrobial/) activity against Streptococcus mutans and astringent tannin effects on gingival tissue

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Dental and gum health use validated through antimicrobial activity against S. mutans and periodontal pathogens
- Anti-ulcer activity confirmed in aspirin and ethanol-induced gastric ulcer models in rats
- Heart tonic (Hridya) use supported by [cardioprotective](/nighantu/reference/cardioprotective/) effects in isoproterenol-induced myocardial infarction models
- Snakebite first-aid use partially validated through anti-venom and anti-inflammatory preliminary studies

## Recent safety updates

- Bark and flower decoctions are traditionally used for dental care and are generally well-tolerated at recommended Ayurvedic doses
- Formal human clinical safety trials are limited; acute toxicity studies in rodents indicate LD50 > 2000 mg/kg for most extracts

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder), Kashayam (decoction), Kwath (decoction concentrate), Manjan (tooth powder), Taila (oil), Arishta (fermented preparation)

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6g powder twice daily; 50-100ml decoction; bark powder 1-3g for dental use

**Bioavailability:** Moderate oral bioavailability due to tannin content. Enhancement via co-administration with lipids or [Pippali](/nighantu/herb/pippali/) (bioenhancer). Saponin [glycosides](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides) show improved absorption with warm water vehicle. Tannin-rich fractions may benefit from enteric coating to prevent gastric binding.

**Optimal Timing:** After meals for general use; as gargle/mouthwash after meals for dental applications; decoction on empty stomach for uterine tonification

**Standardized Extract:** Bark extract standardized to not less than 10% total [tannins](/nighantu/reference/tannins/); flower extract standardized to [quercetin](/nighantu/reference/quercetinquercetin/) content (NLT 0.5%)

**Shelf Life:** 2 years (powder); 3 years (tablet); 1 year (decoction/arishta)

**Storage:** Cool, dry place away from light and moisture. Flower preparations require airtight amber containers.

**Marker Compounds:** Mimusopside, Quercetin, Ursolic acid, Beta-[sitosterol](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol), Gallic acid, Lupeol, Mi-saponin A

### Extraction Methods

- Aqueous decoction (1:8, reduced to 1:4)
- Hydroalcoholic extraction (50:50 ethanol:water)
- Cold maceration for flower extracts
- Sequential extraction (hexane then ethanol) for bark

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Khadira](/nighantu/herb/khadira/) (Acacia catechu) for oral health formulations
- With [Lodhra](/nighantu/herb/lodhra/) for astringent gynecological preparations
- With [Yashtimadhu](/nighantu/herb/yashtimadhu/) for throat and gum care
- With [Jatiphala](/nighantu/herb/jatiphala/) for dental pain relief
