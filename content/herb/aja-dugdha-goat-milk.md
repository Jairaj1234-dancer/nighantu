## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- A2 beta-casein (predominant casein type)
- Alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA) and lactic acid
- Oligosaccharides (prebiotic)
- Medium-chain fatty acids (capric, caprylic acid)
- Calcium (13% more than cow milk)
- Vitamin A (47% more than cow milk)
- Vitamin B6 (25% more than cow milk)
- [Lactoferrin](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#lactoferrin) and [immunoglobulins](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#immunoglobulins)
- Bioactive [peptides](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#peptides) with ACE-inhibitory activity
- Conjugated linoleic acid (CLA)

## How does it work?

- Proposed lower allergenicity: Contains predominantly A2 beta-casein, which has been proposed not to release beta-casomorphin-7 (BCM-7) and so to limit [histamine](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#histamine) and TNF-alpha release from mast cells; goat milk is not hypoallergenic and may still cause reactions (see safety updates)
- Superior digestibility: Smaller fat globules and softer curd formation enable easier enzymatic digestion compared to cow milk
- Prebiotic effect: Oligosaccharides promote beneficial gut microbiota growth and modulate intestinal immunity
- Anti-inflammatory: Medium-chain fatty acids and bioactive peptides have been proposed to reduce inflammation and oxidative stress; effects in humans are not established

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Rajayakshma (tuberculosis/wasting diseases): traditionally used in debilitated patients with wasting conditions. Studies have reported [immunomodulatory](/reference/immunomodulatory/) peptides and easily absorbable nutrients in goat milk.
- Raktapitta (bleeding disorders): traditionally used for its cooling potency and [hemostatic](/glossary/pharmacology/#hemostatic) properties. Studies have reported anti-inflammatory activity.
- Pediatric nutrition and cow milk allergy: used as an alternative milk for children with cow milk protein intolerance. Studies have reported A2 beta-casein and lower allergenicity.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally well tolerated; may still cause reactions in individuals with severe casein allergy (not just beta-lactoglobulin sensitivity)
- Must be pasteurized to eliminate Brucella, Listeria, and other zoonotic pathogens; raw goat milk carries infection risk
- Lower [folate](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#folate) and vitamin B12 content than cow milk; supplementation may be needed in exclusive goat milk diets for infants

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Fresh milk, Boiled milk, Medicated milk (Kshirapaka)

**Standard Dosage:** 100–200 mL twice daily

**Optimal Timing:** Morning or before bed; warm

**Storage:** Refrigerated at 4°C

**Marker Compounds:** A2 beta-casein, Medium-chain triglycerides, Calcium, [Phosphorus](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#phosphorus)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Used as anupana for Rasayana herbs and bhasmas
