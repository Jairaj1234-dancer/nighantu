## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- A2 beta-casein (predominant casein type)
- Alpha-hydroxy acid (AHA) and lactic acid
- Oligosaccharides (prebiotic)
- Medium-chain fatty acids (capric, caprylic acid)
- Calcium (13% more than cow milk)
- Vitamin A (47% more than cow milk)
- Vitamin B6 (25% more than cow milk)
- [Lactoferrin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-g-l/#lactoferrin) and [immunoglobulins](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-g-l/#immunoglobulins)
- Bioactive [peptides](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-o-q/#peptides) with ACE-inhibitory activity
- Conjugated linoleic acid (CLA)

## How does it work?

- Hypoallergenic action: Contains predominantly A2 beta-casein which does not release beta-casomorphin-7 (BCM-7), avoiding [histamine](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-g-l/#histamine) and TNF-alpha release from mast cells
- Superior digestibility: Smaller fat globules and softer curd formation enable easier enzymatic digestion compared to cow milk
- Prebiotic effect: Oligosaccharides promote beneficial gut microbiota growth and modulate intestinal immunity
- Anti-inflammatory: Medium-chain fatty acids and bioactive peptides reduce systemic inflammation and oxidative stress

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Rajayakshma (tuberculosis/wasting diseases): Validated by modern evidence showing [immunomodulatory](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/immunomodulatory/) peptides and easily absorbable nutrients support recovery in debilitated patients
- Raktapitta (bleeding disorders): Cooling potency and [hemostatic](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/pharmacology/#hemostatic) properties confirmed through traditional clinical observation; modern studies support its role in reducing Pitta-related inflammation
- Pediatric nutrition and cow milk allergy: A2 beta-casein and lower allergenicity scientifically validated as a suitable alternative for children with cow milk protein intolerance

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **An Insight into Therapeutic and Nutritional Profile of [Aja Dugdha](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-a-m/#aja-dugdha) (Goat's Milk): A Review** (2023): Goat milk possesses [anticancer](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/anticancer/), [hepatoprotective](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/hepatoprotective/), [cardioprotective](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/cardioprotective/), and [antihypertensive](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/pharmacology/#antihypertensive) effects; its A2 beta-casein does not release BCM-7, making it hypoallergenic and suitable for cow milk protein allergy patients.
- **Bioactive peptides with [antioxidant](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antioxidant/) and ACE inhibitory properties in goat milk protein hydrolysates** (2025): Peptidomics and molecular docking studies identified novel bioactive peptides in goat milk hydrolysates with significant antioxidant and ACE-inhibitory (antihypertensive) properties.
- **[Aja Dugdha](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-a-m/#aja-dugdha): Bridging Ayurvedic Tradition and Modern Nutrition** (2024): Modern studies confirm goat milk's rich nutrient profile, hypoallergenic properties, and beneficial bioactive components validate its traditional Ayurvedic use as a [Rasayana](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/rasayana/), particularly in Kumkadi Taila formulation for hyperpigmentation.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally well tolerated; may still cause reactions in individuals with severe casein allergy (not just beta-lactoglobulin sensitivity)
- Must be pasteurized to eliminate Brucella, Listeria, and other zoonotic pathogens; raw goat milk carries infection risk
- Lower [folate](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-d-g/#folate) and vitamin B12 content than cow milk; supplementation may be needed in exclusive goat milk diets for infants

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Fresh milk, Boiled milk, Medicated milk (Kshirapaka)

**Standard Dosage:** 100–200 mL twice daily

**Bioavailability:** High; easily digestible due to smaller fat globules and A2 casein

**Optimal Timing:** Morning or before bed; warm

**Standardized Extract:** N/A — used as whole food/vehicle

**Shelf Life:** Fresh: 24–48 hours refrigerated

**Storage:** Refrigerated at 4°C

**Marker Compounds:** A2 beta-casein, Medium-chain triglycerides, Calcium, [Phosphorus](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-o-q/#phosphorus)

**Quality Parameters:** Fat content 3.5–4%, SNF >8%, no adulteration ([starch](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#starch)/urea negative)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Used as anupana for Rasayana herbs and bhasmas
