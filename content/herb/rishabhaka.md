## Names and identification

| Language | Name |
|----------|------|
| Sanskrit | Rishabhaka |
| Latin/Botanical | *Malaxis muscifera (Lindl.) Kuntze* |
| Family | Orchidaceae |
| Official substitute | [Vidari](/herb/vidari/) (*Pueraria tuberosa*) |

Rishabhaka belongs to the Ashtavarga, the group of eight drugs in four pairs. It is paired with [Jivaka](/herb/jivaka/), and the classical substitute for the pair is Vidari.

It is a different drug from Rajadana or Kshirini (*Manilkara hexandra*), a fruit tree.

## Where does the identification come from?

- Virk JK, Gupta V, Kumar S, Singh R and others 2017. [Ashtawarga plants: suffering a triple standardization syndrome](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29034185/). *Journal of Traditional and Complementary Medicine*. PMID [29034185](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29034185/) · [doi:10.1016/j.jtcme.2016.12.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtcme.2016.12.011)  
  Tabulates the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia's identification of Rishabhaka as *Malaxis muscifera*, with *Pueraria tuberosa* as the official substitute, and describes how the scarcity of the Ashtavarga orchids leads to substitution and adulteration in the market.

## Recent safety updates

- Because the authentic orchid is scarce, products sold as Rishabhaka may contain a substitute or an adulterant. Ask the supplier which plant is used.
