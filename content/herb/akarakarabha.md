## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- [Pellitorine](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#pellitorine) (N-isobutylamide)
- Pyrethrin
- Anacycline
- [Sesamin](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sesamin)
- [Inulin](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#inulin) (fructans)
- Santolina alcohol (40.7% of essential oil)
- Pyrrolo[3,2-g]isoquinoline [alkaloids](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) (12 newly described, 2024)

## How does it work?

- Anti-inflammatory activity through inhibition of nitric oxide (NO) production in macrophages via novel alkaloid compounds
- Animal studies have reported [aphrodisiac](/glossary/pharmacology/#aphrodisiac) and testosterone-enhancing effects, possibly through modulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis
- [Analgesic](/reference/analgesic/) and sialagogue action via N-isobutylamide (pellitorine) stimulation of nerve endings
- [Immunomodulatory](/reference/immunomodulatory/) activity through modulation of innate and adaptive immune responses

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- [Vajikarana](/glossary/pharmacology/#vajikarana): traditionally used as an aphrodisiac and for male fertility. Animal studies have reported increased testosterone and improved sperm parameters.
- Dantavedanashaka: traditionally used for oral health and toothache. Studies have reported analgesic activity of pellitorine on nerve endings.
- Medhya: traditionally used as a cognitive and nervine tonic. Neuropharmacological studies have reported memory enhancement.
- Inflammatory and rheumatic conditions: traditionally used for inflammation and rheumatic complaints. In vivo studies have reported anti-inflammatory activity.

## Recent safety updates

- High doses may cause excessive salivation, oral mucosal irritation, and gastrointestinal discomfort due to pellitorine content
- Not recommended during pregnancy and lactation due to potential uterine stimulant effects; limited toxicological data for long-term use

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder), Tablet, Capsule, Taila (medicated oil, external), Gutika (pills), Manjan (tooth powder)

**Standard Dosage:** 500mg-2g powder twice daily; 250-500mg extract capsule; external oil as needed for local application

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals with warm milk or honey for systemic indications; as tooth powder or local application for dental/oral conditions

**Storage:** Airtight, moisture-proof containers. Store below 25°C. Protect from light. Roots store better whole, powder just before use.

**Marker Compounds:** Pellitorine, Anacyclin, N-isobutyldienediynamide, Hydrocarolin, Inulin

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/) for nervous system toning and Vajikarana (aphrodisiac)
- Traditionally combined with [Jatiphala](/herb/jatiphala/) (nutmeg) for stammering and speech disorders
- With [Lavanga](/herb/lavanga/) (clove) and Karpura ([camphor](/herb/camphor/)) for toothache
- With [Pippali](/herb/pippali/) for bioenhancement and respiratory conditions
