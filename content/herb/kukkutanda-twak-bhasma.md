## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Calcium (22.75% and 41.16% in two bhasma samples by ICP-AES; Singh et al. 2017)
- Calcium oxide (CaO) nanoparticles after calcination
- Copper, zinc, [magnesium](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#magnesium) as trace minerals
- [Strontium](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#strontium) and [phosphorus](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#phosphorus) traces
- Organic matrix [proteins](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#proteins) from eggshell membrane

## How does it work?

- Bioavailable calcium supplementation through CaO nanoparticles providing elemental calcium for bone mineralization, muscle contraction, nerve impulse transmission, and blood clotting
- [Antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) action through CaO nanoparticle-mediated disruption of microbial cell membranes; more effective as antifungal agent than antibacterial
- Proposed alkaline buffering through calcium oxide dissolution, which may neutralize excess stomach acid (not clinically established)
- Proposed to support bone mineralization (not clinically established) through release of calcium with trace minerals (Mg, Zn) essential for optimal bone metabolism

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Calcium deficiency and bone disorders: traditionally used for Asthi Kshaya (bone depletion), rickets, and osteoporosis. Analytical studies have reported a high calcium content with high bioavailability.
- Leukorrhea (Shweta Pradar): traditionally used in gynecological practice for this condition.
- Urinary tract infections: traditionally used for Mutra Krichra (dysuria). Laboratory studies have reported antimicrobial activity of CaO nanoparticles and an alkaline pH.

## Recent safety updates

- LIKELY SAFE: Classified as LIKELY SAFE AND WELL TOLERABLE in most people. No significant side effects reported with regular use for 3-6 months.
- EGG ALLERGY CAUTION: Patients with egg allergies should use with caution, though eggshell protein content is minimal after calcination

## What is it made of?

### Mineral/Elemental Profile

- **Calcium carbonate (CaCO3):** Major component of raw eggshell (>94%)
- **Calcium (Ca):** 22.75% and 41.16% in two bhasma samples (Singh et al. 2017)
- **Magnesium (Mg):** Trace
- **Phosphorus (P):** Trace
- **Strontium (Sr):** Trace

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Bhasma

**Standard Dosage:** 250–500 mg twice daily

**Optimal Timing:** With meals for calcium absorption

**Storage:** Airtight, dry container

**Marker Compounds:** Calcium carbonate (94% in raw eggshell), Magnesium carbonate, Calcium phosphate, Strontium

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Honey or warm milk
