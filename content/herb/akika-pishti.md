## How does it work?

- Pitta-pacifying cooling action: [Silica](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#silica)-based mineral matrix provides systemic cooling effect, calming aggravated Pitta dosha in conditions of heat, inflammation, and acute irritability
- Cardiac tonic: Trace minerals (iron, [magnesium](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-l-o/#magnesium), calcium) in the agate matrix support cardiovascular function; the cooling property reduces stress-related cardiac symptoms
- [Hemostatic](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/pharmacology/#hemostatic): [Silicon](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#silicon) dioxide and trace minerals may contribute to strengthening vascular walls and supporting coagulation, relevant to traditional use in bleeding disorders

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Pitta disorders (heat, inflammation, irritability): The cooling mineral composition of silica-based agate provides mechanistic rationale for Pitta-pacifying claims, though rigorous clinical validation is lacking
- Cardiac conditions and bleeding disorders: Trace mineral content (iron, magnesium, calcium) provides partial mechanistic support, but no controlled clinical trials specifically validate these indications

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Akik Pishti analytical and safety review** (2020): Akik Bhasma/Pishti contains rich amounts of silicon dioxide, which is classified as safe when ingested orally. The non-toxic nature and compatibility with long-term use under qualified supervision make Akik Pishti a relatively safe mineral-based remedy. However, inhalation must be strictly avoided as it can cause silicosis.
- **Review on Pishti Kalpana in Ayurveda (comprehensive review including Akik Pishti)** (2022): Recent scientific interest in Pishti preparations has surged due to microfine particle size and rapid absorption potential, positioning them within the emerging domain of traditional nanomedicine. However, high-quality clinical trials on Akik Pishti are scarce.

## Recent safety updates

- GENERALLY SAFE for oral ingestion: Silicon dioxide is classified as safe when taken orally at recommended doses; no significant toxicity concerns at therapeutic doses
- CRITICAL WARNING: Must NOT be inhaled - inhalation of silica powder can cause silicosis, a serious and irreversible lung fibrosis condition
- Lab testing should verify heavy metal levels (lead, arsenic, mercury) are within Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia limits, as natural agate may contain trace impurities depending on geological source
- Clinical trial evidence is extremely limited - most data comes from small observational studies, case reports, or animal experiments

## What is it made of?

### Mineral/Elemental Profile

- **Primary component:** Calcined mineral/metal oxide (after Shodhana and Marana)
- **Particle size:** Sub-micron to nanoparticle range
- **Note:** Exact composition depends on source material and preparation method

**Analytical Methods:** XRD, ICP-OES/ICP-MS, SEM-EDS, Particle size analysis, Namburi phased spot test

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Pishti (fine trituration with rose water)

**Standard Dosage:** 125–250 mg twice daily

**Bioavailability:** Superior to bhasma due to low-temperature processing retaining natural matrix

**Optimal Timing:** With honey, cream of milk (Malai), or Gulkand

**Standardized Extract:** Agate stone triturated with Gulab Jal (rose water) — cold-processed

**Shelf Life:** Indefinite

**Storage:** Airtight container; cool, dry place

**Marker Compounds:** Silicon dioxide (chalcedony), Iron oxide, Trace minerals

**Quality Parameters:** Particle size <50 μm (Pishti fineness), heavy metals within AYUSH limits

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Honey, Gulkand, cream of milk (Malai), or pomegranate juice
