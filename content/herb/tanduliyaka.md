## Names and identification

| Language | Name |
|----------|------|
| Sanskrit | Tanduliyaka, Tanduliya; synonyms Meghanada, Alpamarisha, Vishaghna, Bhandira |
| Hindi | Kanta chaulai |
| English | Spiny amaranth, prickly amaranth |
| Latin/Botanical | *Amaranthus spinosus* L. |
| Family | Amaranthaceae |

Charaka's chapter on food and drink (Sutrasthana 27, verse 94) places Tanduliyaka among the leafy vegetables, and the [Charak Samhita Online](https://www.carakasamhitaonline.com/index.php/Annapanavidhi_Adhyaya) edition identifies it as *Amaranthus spinosus* Linn., the prickly amaranth. A 2018 review in the International Journal of Ayurvedic Medicine ([Chandrashekhar 2018](https://ijam.co.in/index.php/ijam/article/download/1169/454/2533)) traces the same identification through the nighantus and collects about a hundred synonyms, of which Meghanada and Alpamarisha are the most widely repeated. The [GBIF Backbone Taxonomy](https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Amaranthus%20spinosus) accepts *Amaranthus spinosus* L. in the family Amaranthaceae.

The name Kshavaka has sometimes been attached to this plant. In Charaka, Kshavaka is a nasal-clearing drug identified as the sneezeweed *Centipeda minima*; see [Kshavaka](/herb/kshavaka/).

We could not find a monograph for Tanduliya in the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India, Part I, Volumes I to VII. The Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, does use Tanduliyaka root (24 g) as an ingredient of Asoka Ghrta (entry 6:4, from Bhaishajya Ratnavali, Strirogadhikara 17-20).

## Ayurvedic pharmacology (Dravyaguna)

| Property | Value | Description |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| **Rasa** (Taste) | Madhura | Primary taste |
| **Guna** (Quality) | Laghu, Ruksha | Physical quality |
| **Virya** (Potency) | Sheeta | Heating/Cooling effect |
| **Vipaka** (Post-digestive) | Madhura | Post-digestive effect |

Charaka (Sutrasthana 27.94) describes the vegetable as dry, sweet in taste and after digestion, and cold in potency. The 2018 review tabulates the same profile, with Laghu added, across Sushruta, Ashtanga Hridaya, Dhanvantari Nighantu, Madanapala Nighantu, Kaiyadeva Nighantu and Bhavaprakasha Nighantu, and reports that the texts hold it to pacify Pitta and Kapha.

## How is it used traditionally?

- Charaka says it counteracts intoxication (mada) and poison (visha) and is beneficial in raktapitta (Sutrasthana 27.94). The 2018 review adds that Charaka also uses it in pradara, that Sushruta uses it in arsha and mushika visha, and that Chakrapani recommends it in atisara.
- Raja Nighantu (verses 5.73-75, as quoted by the [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/tanduliya)) describes it as cooling, sweet and an antidote to poisons, and as an appetiser that is wholesome (pathya). The Kashyapa Samhita includes it in a nasal antidote for snake poison, according to the same entry.
- The 2018 review tabulates folk uses from across India, among them root decoctions taken as a diuretic in Gujarat and Kerala, leaf poultices on boils and wounds in Karnataka, and the young shoots cooked as a vegetable in several states.

## Key Phytochemical Constituents

As summarised in the 2018 review from the Wealth of India and the primary papers it cites:

- Amaranthine and isoamaranthine (betacyanin pigments), other betalains
- [Rutin](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#rutin), [quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/) and [kaempferol](/reference/kaempferol/) [glycosides](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides), hydroxycinnamates
- [Stigmasterol](/reference/stigmasterol/), alpha-spinasterol, hentriacontane (leaves and stems)
- [Saponins](/reference/saponins/) of alpha-spinasterol (root)
- Linoleic acid; squalene and tocotrienols in the seed oil
- Oxalic acid in the leaves (see safety)

## Recent safety updates

- The leaves contain a large amount of oxalic acid, 116.4 mg per 100 g fresh weight according to Shingade 1995 as cited in the 2018 review. This is a consideration for anyone advised to limit dietary oxalate.
- The review notes that the pollen is a recognised aeroallergen in India.
- Material sold under the name Kshavaka may be this plant or the sneezeweed *Centipeda minima*; confirm the identity with the supplier.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Svarasa (fresh juice), Churna (powder), Kwatha (decoction), Capsule, Tablet

**Optimal Timing:** Traditionally taken before meals; morning administration with honey or jaggery as traditional vehicle

**Storage:** Store in airtight, light-resistant containers at 15-25 deg C. Betalain-containing products require protection from light, heat, and humidity. Freeze-dried forms stored at 2-8 deg C for maximum stability.

**Marker Compounds:** Amaranthine, Isoamaranthine, [Rutin](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#rutin), [Quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/), [Kaempferol](/reference/kaempferol/), beta-[Sitosterol](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol), Gallic acid, Ferulic acid

### Synergistic Combinations

Combinations listed in the source notes, without supporting studies:

- [Guduchi](/herb/guduchi/)
