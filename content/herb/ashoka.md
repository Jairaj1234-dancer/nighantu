## Key Ingredients

- Saraca asoca bark (primary drug)
- Key phytoconstituents: [catechin](/reference/catechin/), [epicatechin](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#epicatechin), [kaempferol](/reference/kaempferol/), [rutin](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#rutin), gallic acid, salicylic acid, procyanidin B2, [luteolin](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#luteolin), beta-[sitosterol](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol), lignin [glycosides](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides)

## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Charaka Samhita (Chikitsa Sthana) and Sushruta Samhita reference Ashoka for Pradara (menorrhagia). Bhavaprakasha Nighantu describes it under Pushpa Varga. Ashoka Vriksha referenced in ancient texts as sacred tree of Kamadeva.

## How does it work?

- Estrogenic activity: Phytoestrogens (catechin, epicatechin) bind estrogen receptors, modulating reproductive hormone levels (FSH, LH, estradiol, progesterone).
- Aromatase inhibition: Procyanidin B2 and luteolin inhibit aromatase enzyme, relevant for PCOS and estrogen-dependent conditions.
- [Antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) action: [Flavonoids](/reference/flavonoids/) (kaempferol, rutin) and gallic acid scavenge free radicals and reduce malonaldehyde levels.
- Uterine smooth muscle modulation: Oxytocic effect on uterine musculature, producing rhythmic contractions.
- Anti-inflammatory pathway: Inhibition of COX-2 and pro-inflammatory cytokines, contributing to [analgesic](/reference/analgesic/) and [antipyretic](/reference/antipyretic/) effects.

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Menorrhagia and metrorrhagia: traditionally used for these conditions. Clinical studies have reported [hemostatic](/glossary/pharmacology/#hemostatic) and uterotonic effects.
- PCOS: traditionally used in this context. Studies have reported hormonal modulation and aromatase inhibition.
- Antioxidant and skin health: traditionally used for skin health. Studies have reported flavonoid-mediated free radical scavenging.
- Antipyretic action: traditionally used for fever. Preclinical studies of seed extract have reported antipyretic activity.
- Anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects: traditionally used for inflammation and pain. Studies have reported COX-2 inhibition and cytokine modulation.

## Recent safety updates

- Acute oral toxicity studies show safety up to 2000 mg/kg body weight (ethanolic flower extract) with no mortality or toxic symptoms observed.
- At doses of 250-1000 mg/kg, aqueous bark extract revealed significant changes in hematological and biochemical parameters, with potential toxic effects on hematopoietic and metabolic systems, and significant variation in relative organ weight of liver, spleen, and kidney.
- Contraindicated during pregnancy due to uterotonic effects. Should be used under medical supervision for long-term use.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (bark powder), Kashayam (decoction), [Ashokarishta](/formulation/ashokarishta/) (fermented preparation), Capsule, Tablet, Ghana Vati (extract tablet), Syrup

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6g bark powder twice daily; 50-100ml decoction; 15-30ml [Ashokarishta](/formulation/ashokarishta/) after meals; 500mg-1g extract capsule

**Optimal Timing:** Ashokarishta after meals; bark powder with rice water or milk before meals; for menstrual disorders, start 3-5 days before expected cycle

**Storage:** Bark powder in airtight containers, cool and dry. Ashokarishta in amber glass at room temperature. Capsules with desiccant.

**Marker Compounds:** Catechin, Epicatechin, Procyanidin B2, [Leucocyanidin](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#leucocyanidin), Haematoxylin, Beta-sitosterol, [Quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/), Kaempferol, Gallic acid

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Lodhra](/herb/lodhra/) for menorrhagia and leucorrhea
- With [Nagakesara](/herb/nagakesara/) for uterine bleeding disorders
- With [Shatavari](/herb/shatavari/) for comprehensive female reproductive health
- With [Dashamoola](/herb/dashamoola/) for uterine disorders with inflammation
- Part of Ashokarishta with [Dhataki](/herb/dhataki/) pushpa, Musta, [Haritaki](/herb/haritaki/)
