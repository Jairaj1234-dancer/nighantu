- 3-Deoxysappanchalcone (chalcone)
- Sappanchalcone

## How does it work?

- Brazilin melanin synthesis suppression: Brazilin inhibits forskolin-stimulated cAMP-mediated MITF expression, downregulating tyrosinase and TRP-1/TRP-2 transcription, directly reducing melanin production in melanocytes for skin brightening
- Brazilin anti-inflammatory NO and PGE2 inhibition: Brazilin and protosappanins suppress iNOS and COX-2 expression, reducing nitric oxide and prostaglandin E2 production in activated macrophages, alleviating skin inflammation
- Sappanone homoisoflavonoid [antioxidant](/nighantu/reference/antioxidant/) protection: Sappanone A and B scavenge superoxide and hydroxyl radicals, protecting skin cells from oxidative damage and preventing photoaging
- Collagen synthesis and wound closure promotion: Brazilin-rich extract stimulates fibroblast migration and proliferation, enhances collagen deposition, and accelerates wound epithelialization

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Complexion enhancement (Varnya): Validated by demonstrated brazilin-mediated melanin synthesis suppression in human melanoma cells
- Wound healing (Vrana Ropana): Validated by 2024 animal study showing significant improvement in incision wound healing with 6.5% sappanwood extract
- Anti-inflammatory skin care (Daha Prashamana): Validated by demonstrated inhibition of NO and PGE2 production through iNOS and COX-2 suppression

## Recent safety updates

- Generally regarded as safe in traditional medicine at recommended doses. Heartwood has been used as a natural food coloring and traditional medicine for centuries. No significant toxicity reported in animal studies at therapeutic doses
- Red pigment (brazilin/brazilein) may stain skin and fabrics. Limited formal clinical safety data in humans; most evidence from animal models. Species is sometimes confused with Caesalpinia echinata (pernambuco wood) - proper botanical identification essential

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (heartwood powder), Kwatha (decoction), Capsule, Tablet, Lepa (paste), Taila (oil)

**Standard Dosage:** 1-3 g heartwood powder twice daily; 15-20 mL decoction twice daily; 500 mg standardized extract twice daily

**Bioavailability:** Brazilin from Caesalpinia sappan (Pattanga/sappanwood) has moderate oral bioavailability (~30-40%) with Tmax of 1-2 hours. Brazilin undergoes oxidation to brazilein (deeper red color) which also has pharmacological activity. Protosappanin derivatives show 20-35% bioavailability. Homoisoflavonoids are moderately absorbed and undergo hepatic conjugation. Lipid-based formulations can improve brazilin bioavailability by 30-40%.

**Optimal Timing:** After meals with warm water for systemic effects. Traditional use specifies intake with honey for blood-related disorders.

**Standardized Extract:** Pattanga heartwood extract (10:1) standardized to brazilin >3%, total homoisoflavonoids >5%. Aqueous extract with validated color index and HPLC fingerprint.

**Shelf Life:** Heartwood: 36-60 months (very stable); Powder: 24-36 months; Extract: 24-36 months; Tablets/Capsules: 24-36 months

**Storage:** Heartwood pieces: cool, dry, pest-free storage, can last years. Powder and extracts: airtight, below 25 deg C, protected from light and oxidation. Nitrogen-flushed packaging for brazilin-standardized extracts.

**Marker Compounds:** Brazilin, Brazilein, Protosappanin A, Protosappanin B, Sappanone A, Sappanchalcone, 3-Deoxysappanone, Hematoxylol
