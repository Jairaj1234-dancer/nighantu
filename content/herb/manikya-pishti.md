## How does it work?

- [Anticancer](/nighantu/reference/anticancer/): 70nm aluminum oxide nanoparticles induce mitochondrial-dependent apoptosis in cancer cells through loss of mitochondrial membrane potential, cytochrome c release, and caspase cascade activation - a genuine nanomedicine mechanism
- [Neuroprotective](/nighantu/reference/neuroprotective/): Aluminum oxide nanoparticles at therapeutic doses demonstrate protective effects on neural tissue, possibly through modulation of oxidative stress pathways
- [Immunomodulatory](/nighantu/reference/immunomodulatory/): Ruby nanoparticles may interact with immune cells to enhance innate immunity; the chromium trace content could play a role in [glucose](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-d-g/#glucose) metabolism regulation

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Immunostimulation and tuberculosis (Rajayakshma): The documented anticancer/apoptotic and immunomodulatory properties of ruby nanoparticles provide preliminary mechanistic support for traditional immune-enhancing claims
- Cognitive enhancement and neuroprotection: Preclinical neuroprotective studies validate the traditional use for improving Buddhi (intellect) and Smriti (memory)

## Recent safety updates

- Aluminum oxide (Al2O3) is the primary component - while generally considered inert and safe at low doses, chronic aluminum exposure has been controversially linked to neurodegenerative conditions
- Preclinical toxicity study showed safety of traditionally prepared formulation in rats, but long-term human safety data is lacking
- The nanoparticle size (70nm) raises questions about tissue penetration and accumulation that are relevant to modern nanotoxicology frameworks
- Must be taken strictly under medical supervision; recommended dose 125-250 mg once or twice daily; exceeding recommended doses not advised
