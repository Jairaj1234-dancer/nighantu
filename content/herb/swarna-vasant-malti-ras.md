## How does it work?

- [Swarna Bhasma](/herb/swarna-bhasma/) (gold nanoparticles) is traditionally regarded as a [Rasayana](/reference/rasayana/); immunomodulatory effects have been proposed but are not established in humans
- Mukta Bhasma provides bioavailable calcium and conchiolin [peptides](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#peptides) that exert cooling, anti-inflammatory and antacid effects, pacifying Pitta dosha
- Gold preparations have been reported to affect sperm motility and prostatic activity; this is not established, and its relevance to the traditional use as a [Vajikarana](/glossary/pharmacology/#vajikarana) ([aphrodisiac](/glossary/pharmacology/#aphrodisiac)) is unclear

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Rejuvenative and [immunomodulatory](/reference/immunomodulatory/): traditionally used as a Rasayana (rejuvenative). A clinical study reported systemic bioavailability of gold and no acute toxicity. Larger controlled trials are needed.
- Chronic fever and respiratory conditions: traditionally used for Jirna Jwara (chronic fever), Kasa (cough), and Shwasa (asthma) in clinical practice. Rigorous clinical trials of these uses are lacking.

## What do recent studies show?

- Sharma DC, Jha J, Sharma P and others 2001. [Evaluation of safety and efficacy of a gold containing Ayurvedic drug](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11831371/). *Indian journal of experimental biology*. PMID [11831371](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11831371/)  
  20 male individuals taking 100 mg twice daily for 40 days (cumulative gold intake: 160 mg at 4 mg/day) showed no toxic effects based on clinical examination, unaltered body weight, absence of urinary pathology, and 30 sensitive biochemical and enzymatic tests. Gold was found in plasma, erythrocytes, and semen.

## Recent safety updates

- The only published clinical safety study (2002) was small (n=20, male only) and of short duration (40 days); no long-term safety data exists in peer-reviewed literature
- Contains mercury as HgS and multiple metal bhasmas - heavy metal accumulation is a concern with prolonged use; gold was detected in plasma, erythrocytes, urine, and semen indicating systemic distribution
- Strictly contraindicated in pregnancy, lactation, and children; not recommended for patients with pre-existing renal or hepatic disorders due to heavy metal content
- Must be sourced from GMP-certified manufacturers with batch-wise heavy metal testing; counterfeit or poorly prepared products pose significant toxicity risks

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Vati

**Standard Dosage:** 125 mg once or twice daily

**Optimal Timing:** Morning with honey and ghee; or with [Chyawanprash](/formulation/chyawanprash/)

**Storage:** Airtight, premium container

**Marker Compounds:** Gold nanoparticles, Pearl calcium, Mercury sulfide, [Saffron](/herb/saffron/) (Kesar)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Honey + ghee, [Chyawanprash](/formulation/chyawanprash/), [Guduchi](/herb/guduchi/) Swarasa, or milk
