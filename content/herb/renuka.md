## How does it work?

- Prolactin suppression via clerodane diterpene-mediated dopamine D2 receptor agonism on pituitary lactotrope cells
- Phytoestrogenic modulation through flavonoid binding to estrogen receptors (ER-alpha and ER-beta) with selective effects
- Opioidergic activity through binding to mu-opioid and kappa-opioid receptors, contributing to menstrual pain relief
- Anti-inflammatory action via casticin-mediated inhibition of NF-kB pathway and pro-inflammatory cytokines

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Menstrual regulation and PMS relief - strongly validated through multiple clinical trials showing significant PMS symptom reduction
- Prolactin lowering (Stanya-shodhana) - confirmed through clinical studies demonstrating prolactin reduction via dopamine D2 receptor mechanism
- Menopausal symptom management - validated in clinical studies showing reduction of vasomotor symptoms, mood disorders, and sleep disturbances
- Female reproductive health (Stri-roga) - broadly validated through clinical evidence supporting menstrual cycle normalization and fertility support

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Vitex agnus-castus in Menopause: Phytochemistry, Mechanistic Insights, Clinical Applications, and Safety Perspectives** (2025): Comprehensive review of VAC in menopause management documenting phytoestrogenic, dopaminergic, and opioidergic mechanisms; reviewed clinical evidence for vasomotor symptom reduction and hormonal balance.
- **Vitex agnus castus effects on hyperprolactinaemia** (2024): Demonstrated that VAC extract inhibits prolactin release through interaction with dopamine D2 receptors; clinical evidence supports efficacy in treating hyperprolactinemia-related conditions.
- **Vitex agnus castus Extract Ze 440: Diterpene and Triterpene Interactions With Dopamine D2 Receptor** (2024): Identified specific diterpene and triterpene compounds that interact with dopamine D2 receptors; molecular docking studies confirmed binding mechanisms for prolactin suppression.

## Recent safety updates

- Well-tolerated in clinical trials; common mild side effects include gastrointestinal discomfort, headache, and skin reactions; rare cases of liver toxicity signal noted in rat studies but no corresponding human reports
- Potential interactions with dopamine agonists/antagonists, estrogens, anti-estrogens, and oral contraceptives; not recommended during pregnancy; may reduce efficacy of in vitro fertilization treatments

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (seed powder), Kwatha (decoction), Capsule, Tablet, Taila (oil), Lepa (paste)

**Standard Dosage:** 1-3 g seed powder twice daily; 15-20 mL decoction twice daily; 500 mg standardized extract twice daily

**Bioavailability:** Vitex agnus-castus (Renuka/[Nirgundi](/nighantu/herb/nirgundi/)-related species) contains agnuside and casticin with moderate oral bioavailability (25-40%). Diterpene compounds (rotundifuran, vitexilactone) show variable absorption. The dopaminergic compounds that mediate prolactin-lowering effects have adequate bioavailability for hormonal activity at standard doses. Lipophilic flavonoids benefit from fat co-administration. Note: Some Ayurvedic texts identify Renuka as Vitex negundo seed; verify species identity for accurate phytochemical profile.

**Optimal Timing:** Morning on empty stomach for hormonal effects. Continuous daily use for at least 3 menstrual cycles recommended for PMS/menstrual regulation. Take consistently at the same time daily.

**Standardized Extract:** Renuka seed extract (10:1) standardized to casticin >0.5% and agnuside >0.2% (if V. agnus-castus). Total diterpenes >1%. BNO 1095 or Ze 440 are clinically validated extract specifications (European Vitex standards).

**Shelf Life:** Seeds: 18-24 months; Seed powder: 18-24 months; Extract: 24-36 months; Capsules: 24-36 months

**Storage:** Seeds and powder: airtight containers, below 25 deg C, RH <55%, protected from light. Extracts: amber containers, cool and dry. Store away from heat sources.

**Marker Compounds:** Casticin, Agnuside, Rotundifuran, Vitexilactone, Aucubin, p-Hydroxybenzoic acid, Beta-[sitosterol](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol), Penduletin
