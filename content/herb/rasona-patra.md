## How does it work?

- Organosulfur compounds in garlic leaves undergo enzymatic conversion (via alliinase) to bioactive thiosulfinates upon tissue damage, providing [antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) activity through sulfhydryl group oxidation in microbial [enzymes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes)
- [Flavonoids](/reference/flavonoids/) ([quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/), kaempferol) in garlic leaves provide [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) protection through chelation of pro-oxidant transition metals and scavenging of superoxide and hydroxyl radicals
- Chlorophyll and beta-carotene in fresh garlic leaves contribute additional antioxidant and photoprotective effects not present in garlic bulb preparations

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Garlic leaf preparations are traditionally used for infections. Studies have reported activity of organosulfur compounds against bacteria, fungi, and parasites.
- Fresh garlic greens are traditionally valued as a health-promoting food in Ayurvedic dietary recommendations. Analyses of their phenolic and flavonoid content have reported antioxidant activity.

## Recent safety updates

- Garlic leaves (green garlic) are consumed as food worldwide with excellent safety profile; milder flavor and lower organosulfur content compared to bulb
- Similar precautions as garlic bulb apply but at lower intensity due to reduced allicin content: potential for mild GI irritation, garlic odor, and theoretical anticoagulant effects
- Contact dermatitis possible in sensitized individuals; cross-reactivity with other Allium species (onion, leek) documented

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Swarasa (fresh leaf juice), Churna (dried leaf powder), Capsule, Tablet, Lepa (paste), Topical preparation

**Standard Dosage:** 5-15 mL fresh leaf juice daily; 2-4 g dried leaf powder daily; 500 mg leaf extract twice daily; topical paste as needed

**Optimal Timing:** Fresh juice: morning on empty stomach or with meals. Dried leaf powder: with meals. As culinary herb: add to dishes at end of cooking to preserve bioactives.

**Storage:** Fresh leaves: 2-8 deg C in perforated bags. Dried powder: airtight, below 25 deg C, RH <40%, nitrogen-flushed, protected from light (chlorophyll oxidation). Extract: amber containers, below 25 deg C.

**Marker Compounds:** [Alliin](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alliin) (lower than bulb), Diallyl sulfides, Kaempferol, Quercetin, Chlorophyll a and b, Folate, Beta-carotene, Ascorbic acid
