## How does it work?

- Indirubin anti-proliferative CDK/STAT3 inhibition: Indirubin inhibits cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs) and STAT3 signaling, reducing the hyperproliferative keratinocyte division characteristic of psoriasis
- Tryptanthrin IL-17 and Th17 suppression: Tryptanthrin decreases IL-17 production by Th17 cells, directly targeting the key inflammatory cytokine driving psoriatic inflammation and epidermal hyperplasia
- Claudin-1 skin barrier restoration: Indigo naturalis components upregulate claudin-1 expression in tight junctions, restoring impaired skin barrier function in psoriasis and eczema
- Synergistic neutrophil inflammation reduction: Combined action of indigo, indirubin, and tryptanthrin suppresses neutrophil infiltration and activation, reducing the inflammatory cascade that perpetuates psoriatic plaques

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Psoriasis treatment: Validated by 2024 comprehensive review and transdermal patch studies confirming synergistic anti-psoriatic activity of indigo, indirubin, and tryptanthrin
- Hair and scalp health: Validated by traditional use as hair dye and conditioner, with EU safety assessment confirming safety for hair coloring applications
- Eczema management: Validated by 2023 pilot clinical trial showing 70% symptom improvement with 3% Nili extract gel in 30 patients
- Wound healing: Supported by animal studies showing accelerated wound closure, enhanced collagen synthesis, and reduced inflammatory markers

## Recent safety updates

- Indigofera tinctoria is considered non-toxic in oral administration based on 13-week rat toxicity study with no deaths at doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day. Topical indigo naturalis preparations are generally well-tolerated
- EU Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety (SCCS) assessed Indigofera tinctoria for hair dye use; classified as safe for topical cosmetic application at appropriate concentrations. May cause contact sensitization in rare cases - patch testing advised for first-time users

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder), Taila (oil), Lepa (paste), Capsule, Tablet, Kwatha (decoction), Hair oil formulation

**Standard Dosage:** 500 mg - 2 g dried powder daily; 5-10 mL infusion; topical oil or paste as directed

**Bioavailability:** Indigo and indirubin (key bioactives from Indigofera species) show poor oral bioavailability (<15%) due to low aqueous solubility and extensive gut metabolism. Nano-formulation or complexation with [phospholipids](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-o-q/#phospholipids) (Phytosome technology) can improve oral bioavailability 3-5 fold. Topical application provides direct delivery for dermatological and hair care indications.

**Optimal Timing:** Oral use: after meals. Topical hair oil: apply at night, wash in morning. Skin applications: as directed, typically twice daily.

**Standardized Extract:** Standardized Nili leaf extract with minimum 0.5% indirubin and 2% indigo content. Oil extract (1:4 herb-to-oil ratio) with validated color index and HPTLC profile matching reference standards.

**Shelf Life:** Dried powder: 12-18 months; Oil formulation: 12-18 months; Standardized extract: 24 months; Capsules/Tablets: 24 months

**Storage:** Store in amber containers, protected from direct light and moisture. Temperature below 25 deg C. Oil formulations in amber glass bottles, cool dark storage. Bulk indigo extract in nitrogen-flushed containers.

**Marker Compounds:** Indirubin, Indigo (indigotin), Isatin, Kaempferol, [Quercetin](/nighantu/reference/quercetinquercetin/), Gallic acid, Rotenoids

### Extraction Methods

- Aqueous extraction by fermentation and oxidation (traditional indigo process)

- Oil infusion (Taila Paka method) for topical preparations
