## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Alpha-linolenic acid (ALA, omega-3 fatty acid, ~55% of seed oil)
- Secoisolariciresinol diglucoside (SDG - phytoestrogen lignan)
- Dietary fiber (both soluble [mucilage](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#mucilage) and insoluble)
- Cyclolinopeptides (cyclic [peptides](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#peptides))
- [Flavonoids](/reference/flavonoids/) ([herbacetin](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#herbacetin) diglucoside)
- Cyanogenic [glycosides](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides) (linamarin, linustatin)

## How does it work?

- [Cardioprotective](/reference/cardioprotective/) effects via ALA omega-3 fatty acid-mediated reduction of inflammatory eicosanoids and improvement of lipid profile
- Preclinical studies propose that SDG lignans, converted to enterolactone/enterodiol by gut microbiota, may modulate estrogen receptor signaling
- Anti-inflammatory mechanism via inhibition of NF-kB pathway and reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-6)
- Hypoglycemic effect through soluble fiber-mediated delayed [glucose](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#glucose) absorption and improved insulin sensitivity

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- [Laxative](/reference/laxative/) and digestive (Bhedana): traditionally used as a bulk-forming laxative.
- Skin and hair health (Tvachya, Keshya): traditionally used for skin and hair health.
- Anti-inflammatory (Shothaghna): traditionally used for inflammation. Preclinical and clinical studies have reported reductions in inflammatory markers.
- Cardiovascular health (Hridya): traditionally used for heart health. Clinical studies have reported reductions in blood pressure, cholesterol, and triglycerides.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe at recommended doses (1-2 tablespoons/day); contains cyanogenic glycosides which are destroyed by cooking; raw flaxseed in large quantities may cause cyanide toxicity
- May interact with blood-thinning medications, diabetes drugs, and hormonal therapies due to phytoestrogenic and anticoagulant effects; not recommended in high doses during pregnancy
- Excessive consumption may cause gastrointestinal bloating, flatulence, and diarrhea; adequate water intake is necessary to prevent intestinal obstruction from mucilage

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Beeja churna (seed powder), Taila (linseed oil), Capsule (oil-filled softgel), Tablet (seed meal), Mucilage preparation

**Standard Dosage:** 5-10g ground seed daily (in divided doses); 5-15ml oil daily; 1-2 softgel capsules (1000mg each); seed meal 10-20g

**Optimal Timing:** Ground seed with meals (morning) for constipation and lipid management. Oil with food for omega-3 supplementation. Mucilage soaked overnight, consumed morning for prebiotic/laxative effect.

**Storage:** Whole seeds: cool, dry place at room temperature. Oil: refrigerated after opening, amber glass or opaque containers, nitrogen-flushed headspace. Ground seed: refrigerate or freeze, use quickly.

**Marker Compounds:** Alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), Secoisolariciresinol diglucoside (SDG), Linoleic acid, Oleic acid, Linamarin (below safety threshold), Cyanogenic glycosides (HCN < 150mg/kg post-processing)

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Isabgol](/herb/isabgol/) (psyllium) for constipation and lipid management
- With [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/) for anti-inflammatory synergy
- With [Tila](/herb/tila/) (sesame) for balanced omega fatty acid supplementation
- With [Haritaki](/herb/haritaki/) for digestive regularity
