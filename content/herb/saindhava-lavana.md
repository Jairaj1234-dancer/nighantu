## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Up to 84 trace minerals and elements

- Iron (responsible for pink color in some varieties)
- [Manganese](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#manganese), zinc, chromium
- Sulfate, bicarbonate
- [Iodine](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#iodine) (trace, variable)
- [Selenium](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#selenium), copper, fluoride (trace)

## How does it work?

- Digestive stimulation: Stimulates salivary amylase and gastric acid secretion; mineral content activates digestive [enzymes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes) and promotes bile flow without causing inflammatory heat
- Electrolyte homeostasis: Balanced mineral profile (Na, K, Mg, Ca) supports cellular osmotic balance, nerve impulse transmission, and muscle contraction without pure sodium overload
- Tridosha balance: Unique cooling virya among salts prevents Pitta aggravation; slight sweetness and unctuousness pacify Vata; light quality prevents Kapha accumulation
- Cardiac support: Balanced mineral profile and lower pure sodium concentration per weight compared to refined salt may support better cardiovascular function

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Agni Deepana (digestive stimulant): traditionally used as a digestive stimulant. Rock salt has been reported to stimulate salivary and gastric enzyme production and bile secretion.
- Netra Hita (beneficial for eyes): used in classical practice in eye wash preparations with Saindhava. These preparations have been described as having an [antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) basis.
- Hridya (cardiotonic): traditionally described as a cardiotonic. Rock salt is described as having a balanced mineral profile and a lower sodium-per-weight ratio than refined salt.

## Recent safety updates

- Lower iodine content than iodized table salt; populations relying solely on rock salt may need iodine supplementation to prevent thyroid disorders
- Daily intake should be moderated (less than 5-6 g/day); excessive salt intake from any source increases hypertension risk
- Pink Himalayan salt health claims are often exaggerated in marketing; trace mineral amounts are too small for significant nutritional impact at normal consumption levels
- Purity varies by mine source; some commercial products may be contaminated with microplastics or industrial pollutants

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Crystal salt, Powder, Component in formulations

**Standard Dosage:** 1–3 g as seasoning; in formulations: as directed

**Bioavailability:** Excellent; mineral-rich salt with balanced sodium content

**Optimal Timing:** With meals; before meals for Deepana (appetizer) action

**Standardized Extract:** Natural Himalayan rock salt, the only salt recommended in Ayurveda for daily use

**Shelf Life:** Indefinite (mineral)

**Storage:** Dry, airtight container (hygroscopic)

**Marker Compounds:** Sodium chloride (95–98%), Potassium, Calcium, Magnesium, Iron, 84 trace minerals

**Quality Parameters:** NaCl 95–98%, moisture <1%, iron content (gives pink color), no added anticaking agents, heavy metals within limits

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Part of diet and formulations
