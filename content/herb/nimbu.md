## How does it work?

- [Antioxidant](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antioxidant/) activity via flavonoid-mediated (hesperidin, eriocitrin) free radical scavenging and metal chelation
- Anti-inflammatory action through diosmin and hesperidin-mediated inhibition of TNF-alpha, IL-6 and NF-kB signaling
- [Anticancer](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/anticancer/) potential via D-limonene-induced apoptosis, phase II enzyme induction, and inhibition of tumor angiogenesis
- [Antimicrobial](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antimicrobial/) activity through essential oil [monoterpenes](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-l-o/#monoterpenes) disrupting bacterial and fungal cell membrane integrity

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Digestive aid (Deepana-Pachana) use validated through carminative and stomachic effects in clinical observations
- Scurvy prevention and Vitamin C source validated historically and through nutritional studies
- Antimicrobial and preservative use validated through extensive food science and MIC studies against multiple pathogens
- Kidney stone prevention supported by urinary citrate-increasing effects documented in clinical studies

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Citrus limon (Lemon) Phenomenon - A Review of the Chemistry, Pharmacological Properties, Applications** (2024): Comprehensive review confirmed antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, [antidiabetic](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antidiabetic/), anticancer and [hepatoprotective](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/hepatoprotective/) activities attributed to [flavonoids](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/flavonoids/), limonoids, [coumarins](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-c-d/#coumarins) and terpenes.
- **Phytochemistry and biological activities of Citrus sinensis and Citrus limon: an update** (2023): Lemon [polyphenols](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-o-q/#polyphenols) (hesperidin, diosmin) showed significant vasoprotective and capillary-strengthening effects along with [antihypertensive](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/pharmacology/#antihypertensive) activity in clinical observations.
- **Features, Pharmacological Chemistry, Molecular Mechanism and Health Benefits of Lemon** (2024): D-limonene demonstrated anticancer potential through induction of phase II detoxification [enzymes](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes) and inhibition of Ras-MAPK signaling pathway in cancer cell models.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS) by FDA for food and flavoring use; lemon juice is safe at dietary levels
- Excessive consumption may cause dental erosion due to [citric acid](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-c-d/#citric-acidcitric-acid) content; phototoxicity risk from [psoralen](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-o-q/#psoralen)/furanocoumarin compounds in peel essential oil upon UV exposure

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Fresh juice, Oral liquid/syrup, Effervescent tablet, Lozenges, Spray-dried powder, Essential oil (external/aromatherapy), Cream/gel (topical)

**Standard Dosage:** 10-30 mL fresh lemon juice with water daily; 1-3 g spray-dried juice powder; 2-5 drops essential oil for aromatherapy; topical gel 2-5% citrus extract

**Bioavailability:** [Citric acid](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-c-d/#citric-acidcitric-acid) and ascorbic acid are rapidly and nearly completely absorbed (>90% bioavailability). Limonene from peel oil shows moderate oral bioavailability (~40-60%) with rapid absorption. Flavonoids (hesperidin, eriocitrin) have variable bioavailability (10-30%) improved by co-administration with [pectin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-o-q/#pectin) or bioflavonoid complex. Vitamin C degrades rapidly in solution at pH >4 and elevated temperatures.

**Optimal Timing:** Morning on empty stomach with warm water for detoxification. Before meals as appetite stimulant. Post-meal for digestive comfort.

**Standardized Extract:** Spray-dried lemon juice powder with minimum 8% citric acid and 2% ascorbic acid. Lemon peel extract standardized to hesperidin >5% and eriocitrin >2%. Essential oil: limonene >60%, [citral](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-c-d/#citral) 2-5%.

**Shelf Life:** Fresh juice: 24-48 hours refrigerated; Spray-dried powder: 12-18 months; Essential oil: 12-24 months; Effervescent tablets: 18-24 months

**Storage:** Fresh juice: 2-8 deg C, protect from light. Powder: below 25 deg C, RH <35%, moisture-barrier packaging with desiccant. Essential oil: amber glass, 15-25 deg C, tightly sealed. All forms: protect from heat and direct sunlight.

**Marker Compounds:** Limonene, Citric acid, Ascorbic acid (Vitamin C), Hesperidin, Eriocitrin, Citral (geranial + neral), Beta-pinene, Gamma-terpinene

### Extraction Methods

- Mechanical cold-pressing of fruit for juice
- Steam distillation of peel for essential oil
- Cold-press peel oil extraction
- Spray drying of juice with maltodextrin (ratio 1:1 to 1:2)
- Hydroalcoholic extraction of peel for flavonoids
- Supercritical CO2 for limonene-rich fractions
