## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Cubebin (major lignan)
- [Piperine](/reference/piperine/)
- [Sesamin](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sesamin) and hinokinin
- Cubebinionolide and cubebinone
- Dihydrocubebin, yatein, clusin
- Essential oil: [sabinene](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sabinene), alpha-copaene, delta-cadinene

## How does it work?

- Anti-inflammatory activity via cubebin-mediated inhibition of NF-kB pathway and suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokines
- Antibacterial mechanism through disruption of bacterial cell membrane integrity by [lignans](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#lignans) and essential oil components
- Proposed [antidiabetic](/reference/antidiabetic/) mechanism: alpha-amylase and alpha-glucosidase enzyme inhibition
- Proposed [hepatoprotective](/reference/hepatoprotective/) and nephroprotective mechanism: [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) activity against oxidative stress

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Digestive and carminative (Deepana-Pachana): traditionally used as a digestive and carminative.
- Respiratory disorders and cough (Kasa-hara): traditionally used as an [expectorant](/reference/expectorant/) for respiratory complaints and cough. Laboratory studies have reported [antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) activity of the essential oil against respiratory pathogens.
- Urogenital health (Mutra-roga): traditionally used for urinary tract infections. Laboratory studies have reported antimicrobial activity.
- Oral health freshener (Mukha-shodhana): traditionally used for oral care and to freshen the mouth. Laboratory studies have reported antibacterial activity.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally recognized as safe in culinary amounts; excessive consumption may cause gastrointestinal irritation and kidney inflammation
- Limited pharmacokinetic and clinical data; no systematic toxicological studies in humans; potential interactions with diabetes medications due to hypoglycemic effects

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder), Vati (tablet), Capsule, Taila (oil), Avaleha (confection)

**Standard Dosage:** 1-3 g powder twice daily; 3-5 drops essential oil (diluted); as adjuvant in formulations

**Optimal Timing:** After meals for digestive support; before meals for appetite stimulation

**Storage:** Store in tightly sealed, amber glass containers at 15-25°C. Powder formulations lose potency rapidly if exposed to air; nitrogen-flushed packaging recommended.

**Marker Compounds:** Cubebin, Cubebic acid, 1,8-Cineole, Alpha-cubebene, Beta-cubebene, Copaene, Eugenol

### Synergistic Combinations

- Ela (Cardamom) for digestive formulations
- [Lavanga](/herb/lavanga/) (Clove) for oral health preparations
- [Jatiphala](/herb/jatiphala/) (Nutmeg) for carminative synergy
- Maricha ([Black pepper](/herb/black-pepper/)) for bioavailability enhancement
