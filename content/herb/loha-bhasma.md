## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Iron oxide nanoparticles (Fe2O3/Fe3O4) in 50-200 nm range
- Magnetite phase (Fe3O4) as primary constituent
- Fe2+/Fe3+ ratio approximately 40%:60%
- Trace carbon, sodium, and zinc as contaminants
- Organic compounds from herbal processing media ([Triphala](/herb/triphala/), etc.)

## How does it work?

- Iron supplementation via bioavailable nano-iron oxide particles that enhance intestinal absorption through smaller particle size and increased surface area, bypassing typical ferrous iron GI side effects
- Hematopoietic stimulation through provision of elemental iron in oxide form, supporting hemoglobin synthesis and red blood cell production in iron-deficiency states
- Digestive fire (Agni) enhancement through iron-mediated stimulation of metabolic [enzymes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes) and improvement of nutrient absorption in the gastrointestinal tract

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Iron-deficiency anemia (Pandu Roga): traditionally used for iron-deficiency anemia.
- Digestive disorders: traditionally used to improve digestion and appetite, described as Agni-deepana (digestive fire enhancement). Clinical observations have reported improved digestion and appetite.
- General debility and weakness (Daurbalya): traditionally used for general debility and weakness. Clinical studies have reported improved hemoglobin levels and improved clinical symptoms.

## Recent safety updates

- SAFE AT HIGH DOSES: No mortality or toxicity signs up to 100x therapeutic dose in acute study; safe at 5x therapeutic dose for 60 days in subacute study
- FEWER GI SIDE EFFECTS: Clinical trials showed significantly fewer gastrointestinal complaints (constipation, nausea) compared to conventional ferrous sulfate, likely due to nano-particulate oxide form
- DOSE-DEPENDENT CAUTION: At 10x therapeutic dose, some biochemical and histopathological alterations observed. Strict therapeutic dose adherence recommended.
- IRON OVERLOAD RISK: Prolonged unsupervised use may lead to iron overload (hemochromatosis-like symptoms). Periodic hemoglobin and serum ferritin monitoring advised.
- STANDARDIZATION CONCERN: Different preparation methods yield different particle sizes and iron oxide phases, affecting bioavailability and safety. Only classically prepared products recommended.

## What is it made of?

### Mineral/Elemental Profile

- **Iron (Fe2O3/Fe3O4):** Major component (>90%)
- **Calcium (Ca):** Trace
- **Silicon (Si):** Trace
- **Manganese (Mn):** Trace

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Bhasma

**Standard Dosage:** 125–250 mg twice daily

**Optimal Timing:** After meals with honey, ghee, or [Triphala](/herb/triphala/) Kwatha

**Storage:** Airtight glass or HDPE container; avoid moisture

**Marker Compounds:** Fe3O4 (magnetite, primary), Fe2O3 (hematite), Trace minerals from Bhavana media

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Honey + ghee (unequal parts), [Triphala](/herb/triphala/) Kwatha, [Amla](/herb/amla/) juice, or [Mandura Bhasma](/herb/mandura-bhasma/) combination
