## How does it work?

- Eugenol inhibits COX-2 and iNOS expression, reducing prostaglandin E2 and nitric oxide production for anti-inflammatory and [analgesic](/reference/analgesic/) effects
- [Anticancer](/reference/anticancer/) activity was reported in cell and animal studies through multiple pathways: induction of apoptosis via mitochondrial dysfunction, cell cycle arrest at G2/M phase, and inhibition of NF-kB-mediated survival signaling
- [Antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) mechanism via disruption of bacterial cell membrane integrity, inhibition of protein synthesis, and interference with quorum sensing
- Anesthetic/analgesic effect through inhibition of voltage-gated sodium channels and TRPV1 receptor modulation in nociceptive neurons
- [Antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) activity through phenolic compound-mediated free radical scavenging and upregulation of Nrf2-dependent antioxidant [enzymes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes)

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Toothache and dental pain: traditionally used for toothache and dental pain. Eugenol is used in dental practice as an analgesic and antiseptic.
- Oral infections and wounds: traditionally used for oral infections and wound care. In vitro and clinical studies have reported broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity, including against multidrug-resistant bacteria.
- Flatulence, colic and nausea: traditionally used as a digestive aid for flatulence, colic and nausea. Pharmacological studies have reported [antispasmodic](/glossary/pharmacology/#antispasmodic) and carminative effects.
- Joint and muscle pain: traditionally used for joint and muscle pain. Studies have reported COX-2 inhibition and reduced inflammatory markers.
- Cough and sore throat: traditionally used for cough and sore throat. Studies have reported [expectorant](/reference/expectorant/) and antimicrobial activity.

## Recent safety updates

- WHO classifies eugenol as a GRAS (generally recognized as safe) substance with acceptable daily intake (ADI) of maximum 2.5 mg/kg body weight
- High-dose eugenol can cause hepatotoxicity through quinone methide intermediate formation by peroxidases; cytotoxicity also linked to membrane disruption due to high lipophilicity and uncoupling of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation
- Topical clove oil may cause contact dermatitis and mucosal irritation; oral high doses (40 mg/kg eugenol) can impair kidney function; avoid concurrent use with anticoagulant medications due to antiplatelet activity

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder), Essential oil, Capsules, Tablets, Dental preparations, Mouthwash, Kwatha (decoction), Avaleha (confection), Modaka (bolus)

**Standard Dosage:** 1-2 g powder per day; 250-500 mg extract capsules twice daily; 1-3 drops essential oil topically (diluted); 0.5-1 g in Avaleha formulations

**Bioavailability:** [Eugenol](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#eugenol) rapidly absorbed orally with approximately 50% bioavailability. Buccal absorption is rapid, useful in dental formulations. Beta-caryophyllene shows improved bioavailability in lipid vehicles. Glucuronidation is the primary metabolic pathway. Enteric coating can delay release for lower GI targeting.

**Optimal Timing:** After meals for digestive carminative action; before meals for appetite stimulation; as needed for dental analgesia

**Standardized Extract:** Steam-distilled essential oil (minimum 85% eugenol by GC). CO2 extract preserving full terpenoid profile. Standardized dry extract (4:1) with minimum 12% eugenol. Water-soluble extract for mouthwash formulations.

**Shelf Life:** 36 months for essential oil; 24 months for dry extract powder; 30 months for tablets/capsules; 18 months for mouthwash preparations

**Storage:** Essential oil: amber glass, tightly sealed, 15-25 deg C, away from direct sunlight. Dry extracts: airtight containers, below 25 deg C, RH below 60%. Clove buds whole: airtight at room temperature, up to 3 years.

**Marker Compounds:** Eugenol, Beta-caryophyllene, Eugenyl acetate, Alpha-humulene, Methyl salicylate, [Kaempferol](/reference/kaempferol/), Rhamnetin, Crategolic acid
