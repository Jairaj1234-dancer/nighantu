## How does it work?

- [Flavonoids](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/flavonoids/) (vitexin, isovitexin, [apigenin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-1-a/#apigenin)) exert [antioxidant](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antioxidant/) effects through direct free radical scavenging and chelation of pro-oxidant metal ions, reducing oxidative damage in cellular models
- Beta-sitosterol and betulin modulate inflammatory pathways by inhibiting NF-kB nuclear translocation and reducing expression of pro-inflammatory mediators (COX-2, iNOS, TNF-alpha)
- Ethyl gallate and phenolic compounds exhibit [antimicrobial](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antimicrobial/) activity by disrupting bacterial cell membrane permeability and inhibiting biofilm formation

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- [Antidiabetic](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antidiabetic/) and hypoglycemic activity validated through in vivo studies confirming traditional Ayurvedic use of Amlaparni for managing blood sugar levels
- Wound healing properties confirmed in preclinical models consistent with traditional topical application for skin conditions and wound management in folk medicine
- [Hepatoprotective](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/hepatoprotective/) effects demonstrated in animal models supporting traditional use for liver disorders

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Oxalis corniculata L. As a Source of Natural Antioxidants: Phytochemistry, Bioactivities, and Application Potential** (2025): Comprehensive review highlighting flavonoids, [polysaccharides](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-o-q/#polysaccharides), and organic acids as dominant metabolites with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, [neuroprotective](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/neuroprotective/), hypoglycemic, and [anticancer](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/anticancer/) effects.
- **Ethnopharmacology and therapeutic potentials of Oxalis corniculata: an in-depth study** (2024): Documented broad spectrum of pharmacological activities including antioxidant, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, anticancer, astringent, anthelmintic, cardio-relaxant, and [diuretic](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/diuretic/) properties.
- **A Review on Phyto-pharmacology of Oxalis corniculata** (2021): Plant demonstrated significant hepatoprotective, antidiabetic, and wound healing activities in preclinical models, supporting traditional ethnomedicinal claims.

## Recent safety updates

- High oxalic acid content is the primary safety concern; excessive consumption may cause oxalate nephropathy and kidney stone formation in susceptible individuals
- Traditional use suggests safe consumption in small therapeutic doses; no formal toxicity studies with standardized LD50 determination have been completed in human trials
- Should be used cautiously in patients with renal impairment, hyperoxaluria, or history of calcium oxalate kidney stones; further preclinical and clinical safety trials are recommended

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder), Kashayam (decoction), Swarasa (fresh juice), Capsule, Tablet

**Standard Dosage:** 3-5g powder twice daily; 40-80ml decoction twice daily; 10-20ml fresh juice

**Bioavailability:** Limited modern pharmacokinetic data. Traditional enhancement with honey, warm water, or [Trikatu](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/trikatu/) yoga recommended. The herb's acidic taste ([Amla](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/amla/) rasa) suggests presence of organic acids that are generally well absorbed orally.

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals with warm water for digestive indications; with honey for respiratory conditions

**Standardized Extract:** Limited standardization data. TLC/HPTLC fingerprinting recommended. Total organic acid content as preliminary quality parameter.

**Shelf Life:** 18 months (powder); 2 years (capsules/tablets)

**Storage:** Cool, dry place below 25°C, in airtight containers, protected from moisture and light.

**Marker Compounds:** Total organic acids, Total phenolics, Total flavonoids, Ascorbic acid (if present)
