## How does it work?

- Anti-inflammatory: Iridoid [glycosides](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides) (asperuloside, paederoside) inhibit NF-kB pathway and reduce pro-inflammatory cytokine production (TNF-alpha, IL-1beta)
- Antinociceptive: Ursolic acid and [iridoids](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#iridoids) act on central and peripheral pain pathways, inhibiting PGE2 synthesis and modulating opioid receptors
- Gastroprotective: Enhances mucosal PGE2 production, increases gastric mucus layer thickness, and reduces HCl secretion through H+/K+-ATPase inhibition

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Traditionally used as an anti-rheumatic (Vatahara). Animal studies have reported anti-inflammatory and [analgesic](/reference/analgesic/) activity in carrageenan and formalin models.
- Traditionally used for diarrhea. Animal studies have reported reduced gastrointestinal motility and fluid accumulation in a castor oil-induced diarrhea model.
- Traditionally used to protect the stomach. Research has reported a PGE2-mediated increase in gastric mucus secretion and a protective effect against ulcers.

## Recent safety updates

- Traditionally consumed as a vegetable in Northeast India with a good safety record; acute toxicity studies show no mortality at 2000 mg/kg in rodents
- Foetid odor (from sulfur-containing compounds) may cause nausea in some individuals; limited data on chronic toxicity and drug interactions

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Taila (medicated oil), Churna, Kwatha (decoction), Capsule, Tablet, [Ghrita](/herb/ghrita/), Lepa (paste)

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6 g powder daily; 20-30 mL decoction twice daily; 500-750 mg standardized extract twice daily; Prasarini Taila for external massage

**Optimal Timing:** Oral: after meals to reduce odor-related nausea. Prasarini Taila: warm oil massage before bath, or at bedtime for joint conditions. [Ghrita](/herb/ghrita/): early morning on empty stomach for Vata conditions.

**Storage:** Dried herb: airtight (odor containment critical), below 25 deg C. Taila: amber glass, room temperature. Capsules: blister packaging to contain odor. All forms: protect from moisture and light.

**Marker Compounds:** Paederoside, Asperuloside, Paederosidic acid, Ursolic acid, Oleanolic acid, [Sitosterol](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol), Stigmasterol, Methyl mercaptan (volatile marker)
