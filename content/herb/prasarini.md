## How does it work?

- Anti-inflammatory: Iridoid [glycosides](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides) (asperuloside, paederoside) inhibit NF-kB pathway and reduce pro-inflammatory cytokine production (TNF-alpha, IL-1beta)
- Antinociceptive: Ursolic acid and [iridoids](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-g-l/#iridoids) act on central and peripheral pain pathways, inhibiting PGE2 synthesis and modulating opioid receptors
- Gastroprotective: Enhances mucosal PGE2 production, increases gastric mucus layer thickness, and reduces HCl secretion through H+/K+-ATPase inhibition

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Anti-rheumatic use (Vatahara) validated through anti-inflammatory and [analgesic](/nighantu/reference/analgesic/) activity in carrageenan and formalin models
- Antidiarrheal use confirmed by reduction of gastrointestinal motility and fluid accumulation in castor oil-induced diarrhea model
- Gastroprotective use validated by PGE2-mediated increase in gastric mucus secretion and ulcer protection

## Recent safety updates

- Traditionally consumed as a vegetable in Northeast India with a good safety record; acute toxicity studies show no mortality at 2000 mg/kg in rodents
- Foetid odor (from sulfur-containing compounds) may cause nausea in some individuals; limited data on chronic toxicity and drug interactions

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Taila (medicated oil), Churna, Kwatha (decoction), Capsule, Tablet, [Ghrita](/nighantu/herb/ghrita/), Lepa (paste)

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6 g powder daily; 20-30 mL decoction twice daily; 500-750 mg standardized extract twice daily; Prasarini Taila for external massage

**Bioavailability:** Paederia foetida (Prasarini) [alkaloids](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) show moderate oral bioavailability (25-40%). Iridoid glycosides (paederoside, asperuloside) are hydrolyzed by intestinal [enzymes](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes) releasing aglycones with improved membrane permeability. Volatile sulfur compounds (methyl mercaptan, dimethyl disulfide) responsible for the characteristic odor are rapidly absorbed but also rapidly eliminated. Topical oil formulation provides sustained local delivery for musculoskeletal conditions.

**Optimal Timing:** Oral: after meals to reduce odor-related nausea. Prasarini Taila: warm oil massage before bath, or at bedtime for joint conditions. [Ghrita](/nighantu/herb/ghrita/): early morning on empty stomach for Vata conditions.

**Standardized Extract:** Prasarini whole herb extract (6:1) standardized to paederoside >1%, asperuloside >0.8%, total iridoids >3%. Taila: prepared per API with [sesame oil](/nighantu/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila) base, validated organoleptic and physicochemical parameters.

**Shelf Life:** Dried herb: 12-18 months; Churna: 12-18 months; Extract: 24 months; Taila: 24-36 months; Capsules: 24 months

**Storage:** Dried herb: airtight (odor containment critical), below 25 deg C. Taila: amber glass, room temperature. Capsules: blister packaging to contain odor. All forms: protect from moisture and light.

**Marker Compounds:** Paederoside, Asperuloside, Paederosidic acid, Ursolic acid, Oleanolic acid, [Sitosterol](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol), Stigmasterol, Methyl mercaptan (volatile marker)

### Extraction Methods

- Hydroalcoholic extraction (50-70% ethanol)
- Aqueous decoction
- Traditional Taila Paka (oil cooking) with [sesame oil](/nighantu/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila)
- Steam distillation for volatile sulfur compounds
- Methanol extraction for iridoid profiling
- Ghrita Paka for ghee-based preparation
