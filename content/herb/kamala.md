## How does it work?

- Neferine anti-photoaging through UVB mitochondrial protection: Neferine was reported in cell studies to inhibit UVB-induced oxidative stress-mediated autophagy and mitochondrial dysfunction in skin cells
- Flavonoid tyrosinase and collagenase dual inhibition: Kaempferol derivatives and [quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/) glucuronide have been reported in laboratory studies to inhibit tyrosinase (involved in melanin synthesis) and MMP-1/MMP-3 collagenase (involved in collagen breakdown)
- NF-kB and MAPK anti-inflammatory signaling: Lotus flavonoids and [alkaloids](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) modulate NF-kB, MAPK, and COX-2 pathways, with reported suppression of TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IL-6, NO, and PGE2 production
- Liensinine and [isoliensinine](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#isoliensinine) MMP-3 inhibition: Molecular dynamics studies identified liensinine and isoliensinine as potential MMP-3 inhibitors

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Skin brightening and complexion (Varnya): traditionally used for skin brightening and complexion. A clinical study of cosmetics made with lotus callus extract reported a whitening effect.
- Anti-aging and skin rejuvenation: traditionally used for skin rejuvenation. In silico and in vitro studies reported kaempferol-3-O-robinobioside as an inhibitor of skin aging [enzymes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes).
- Anti-inflammatory skin soothing (Pitta-shamana): traditionally used for soothing the skin. Studies have reported modulation of NF-kB, MAPK, and COX-2, with lower levels of inflammatory mediators in skin.

## Recent safety updates

- Nelumbo nucifera has food-grade safety status globally; all parts (seeds, rhizomes, leaves, flowers) are consumed as food in Asian cuisines. Cosmetic Ingredient Review (CIR) panel assessed lotus-derived ingredients for safety in 2025
- Nuciferine alkaloid may have sedative effects at high oral doses. Topical lotus extracts are generally well-tolerated with no significant adverse effects reported in clinical studies. Standardization of kaempferol content recommended for cosmetic consistency

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (powder - flower, seed, root used differently), Capsule, Tablet, Kashayam (decoction), Taila (seed/flower oil), Lepa (paste), Beeja Churna (seed powder)

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6g flower powder twice daily; 500mg-1g extract; 3-5g seed powder; 50-100ml root decoction

**Optimal Timing:** Flower preparations: with cool water between meals. Seed: as snack or with milk. Leaf extract: before meals. Root decoction: before meals.

**Storage:** Cool, dry place below 25°C, protected from light and moisture. Flower preparations particularly sensitive to light and moisture. Seeds in airtight containers. Nitrogen-flushed packaging for standardized extracts.

**Marker Compounds:** Nuciferine (leaf), Neferine (seed embryo), Liensinine (seed embryo), Isoliensinine, Kaempferol-3-glucoside, [Quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/), Megastigmane glycosides (flower), Nelumboroside A

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Ashoka](/herb/ashoka/) for female reproductive health (flower)
- With [Brahmi](/herb/brahmi/) (leaf/nuciferine)
- With [Chandana](/herb/chandana/) (sandalwood) for Pitta-pacifying formulations
- With Amalaki for antioxidant synergy
