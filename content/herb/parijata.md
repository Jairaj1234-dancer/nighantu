## How does it work?

- Antimalarial activity via arbortristosides disrupting plasmodium schizont maturation and erythrocytic stages
- Anti-inflammatory action through inhibition of COX-2 and LOX pathways and suppression of TNF-alpha and IL-1beta
- [Immunomodulatory](/nighantu/reference/immunomodulatory/) effects via stimulation of T-cell proliferation, NK cell activity and macrophage activation
- [Hepatoprotective](/nighantu/reference/hepatoprotective/) mechanism through restoration of [antioxidant](/nighantu/reference/antioxidant/) enzyme levels and reduction of lipid peroxidation (MDA)

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- [Antipyretic](/nighantu/reference/antipyretic/) and antimalarial use for fevers validated through Plasmodium berghei-infected mouse models and clinical observations
- Anti-arthritic use confirmed through adjuvant-induced arthritis models with significant reduction in paw edema and joint inflammation
- Anthelmintic activity validated against Ascaridia galli and Pheretima posthuma in vitro
- Hepatoprotective effects confirmed against paracetamol-induced liver damage in rodent models

## Recent safety updates

- Most pharmacological studies lack proper dosage standardization, positive and negative controls; clinical human trials are limited
- Leaves decoction used traditionally for fever is generally safe at recommended doses; excessive use may cause gastric irritation. Not recommended during pregnancy without medical supervision.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (leaf powder), Kwatha (decoction), Capsule, Tablet, Swarasa (fresh juice), Ark (distillate), Taila (oil)

**Standard Dosage:** 1-3 g leaf powder daily; 15-30 mL leaf decoction twice daily; 10-15 mL fresh leaf juice; 500-750 mg standardized extract daily

**Bioavailability:** Nyctanthic acid and arbortristosides show moderate oral bioavailability (20-35%). Iridoid glycosides undergo hydrolysis by intestinal beta-glucosidases releasing aglycones with enhanced absorption. Arbortristoside-A demonstrates anti-malarial activity with Tmax of 2-3 hours. Phenolic glycosides from leaves are moderately absorbed and undergo Phase II metabolism (glucuronidation, sulfation).

**Optimal Timing:** For fever: decoction taken early morning on empty stomach (traditional protocol). For arthritis and chronic conditions: after meals, twice daily. Fresh juice: morning on empty stomach.

**Standardized Extract:** Parijata leaf extract (8:1) standardized to arbortristosides >1.5%, nyctanthic acid >0.8%, total iridoid glycosides >3%. Fresh juice preserved with sodium benzoate (0.1%) for short-term shelf life.

**Shelf Life:** Dried leaf: 12-18 months; Fresh juice: 2-3 days refrigerated (preserved: 3-6 months); Extract: 24 months; Tablets/Capsules: 24-36 months

**Storage:** Dried leaves: airtight containers, below 25 deg C, RH <55%, protected from light. Extracts: amber containers, below 25 deg C. Fresh juice: 2-8 deg C. Taila: room temperature, amber glass.

**Marker Compounds:** Arbortristoside-A, Arbortristoside-C, Nyctanthic acid, Nyctanthin, Iridoid glycosides, D-[mannitol](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-l-o/#mannitolmannitol), Tannic acid, Beta-sitosterol, Oleanolic acid

### Extraction Methods

- Aqueous decoction (traditional Kwatha)
- Hydroalcoholic extraction (50-70% ethanol)
- Methanol extraction for iridoid glycoside enrichment
- Fresh juice expression for Swarasa
- Steam distillation for Ark preparation
- Ethyl acetate fractionation for arbortristosides
