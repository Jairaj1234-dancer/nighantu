## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- Shikonins and onosmins (naphthoquinones - most significant)
- Naphthazarins and benzoquinones
- Lycopsamine and supindine viridiflorate (pyrrolizidine [alkaloids](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) in leaves)
- Choline and [tannins](/reference/tannins/)
- [Flavonoids](/reference/flavonoids/) and phenolic compounds
- [Saponins](/reference/saponins/) and [glycosides](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides)

## How does it work?

- Studies of a hydro-alcoholic extract have reported anti-asthmatic activity, with mast cell stabilization and reduced bronchial hyper-responsiveness
- Studies have reported [analgesic](/reference/analgesic/) activity, with a dose-dependent increase in latency period
- Laboratory studies have reported [anticancer](/reference/anticancer/) mechanisms through shikonin/onosmin-mediated topoisomerase inhibition, ROS generation, and apoptosis induction
- Studies have reported anti-inflammatory action via inhibition of pro-inflammatory mediators and stabilization of lysosomal membranes

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Respiratory tonic (Shvasa-hara): traditionally used as a respiratory tonic and for asthma. Studies have reported mast cell stabilization and reduced bronchial hyper-responsiveness.
- Cooling and [antipyretic](/reference/antipyretic/) (Jvarahara): traditionally used in Unani and Ayurvedic practice as a cooling herb for fever.
- Throat and oral health (Mukha-roga): traditionally used for throat and mouth conditions. Studies have reported [antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) and anti-inflammatory activity.
- Heart tonic (Hridya): traditionally used as a heart tonic.

## Recent safety updates

- Contains pyrrolizidine alkaloids (lycopsamine, supindine viridiflorate) in leaves which are hepatotoxic with chronic use; should not be used long-term internally
- External use generally considered safe; internal use should be limited in duration and supervised; contraindicated in liver disease patients

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Swarasa (fresh juice), Churna (powder), Kashayam (decoction), Tablet, Capsule, Lepa (external paste)

**Standard Dosage:** 10-20ml fresh juice; 3-6g powder twice daily; 50-100ml decoction twice daily; 500mg extract capsule twice daily

**Optimal Timing:** After meals with warm water for respiratory and urinary conditions; traditionally used as a component in compound formulations rather than standalone

**Storage:** Airtight containers below 25 deg C, protected from moisture and light. Label must carry appropriate PA-related warnings if applicable to jurisdiction.

**Marker Compounds:** Pyrrolizidine alkaloids (PA content - SAFETY MONITORING, must be below limits), Anchusine (naphthoquinone), Onosmin, Beta-[sitosterol](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol), Flavonoids, [Mucilage](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#mucilage) content

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Vasa](/herb/vasa/) (Adhatoda) for respiratory and bronchial conditions
- With [Yashtimadhu](/herb/yashtimadhu/) for demulcent and anti-tussive synergy
- With Behman (Centaurea behen) for cardiac tonic combination (Unani use)
- With Khatmi (Althaea) for soothing urinary tract conditions
