## How does it work?

- Piperine enhances bioavailability of co-administered drugs/nutrients by inhibiting CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein efflux pump, and stimulating intestinal amino acid transporters; root has higher piperine concentration than fruit
- Piperlongumine selectively kills cancer cells by elevating reactive oxygen species (ROS) through inhibition of glutathione S-transferase pi 1 (GSTP1), sparing normal cells with adequate [antioxidant](/nighantu/reference/antioxidant/) defenses
- Sesamin and diaeudesmin from root exhibit anti-inflammatory effects through dual inhibition of COX-2 and 5-LOX pathways, reducing both prostaglandin and leukotriene synthesis

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Bioavailability enhancement (Yogavahi) validated - piperine's ability to increase absorption of co-administered compounds is well-established pharmacologically, confirming the traditional Ayurvedic concept of Yogavahi (bioenhancer)
- Respiratory disease treatment (Shwasa, Kasa) supported by documented anti-inflammatory, bronchodilatory, and [immunomodulatory](/nighantu/reference/immunomodulatory/) effects, though high-quality RCTs are still needed
- Deepana-Pachana (digestive stimulant) activity validated through studies showing enhanced gastric secretion and gastrointestinal motility

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Safety and efficacy of Piper longum L. for acute respiratory infections: A systematic review** (2025): Systematic review evaluating safety and efficacy of Piper longum preparations for acute respiratory infections; found no good quality RCTs comparing to placebo, highlighting need for controlled clinical trials.
- **Piper longum L.: A comprehensive review on traditional uses, phytochemistry, pharmacology, and health-promoting activities** (2022): Essential oils from roots and fruits documented as [antimicrobial](/nighantu/reference/antimicrobial/), antiparasitic, anti-inflammatory, [analgesic](/nighantu/reference/analgesic/), antioxidant, [anticancer](/nighantu/reference/anticancer/), [neuroprotective](/nighantu/reference/neuroprotective/), [hepatoprotective](/nighantu/reference/hepatoprotective/), and immunomodulatory agents.
- **Bioactives and Lipase Inhibitors from Underutilized Part of Piper longum: A Sustainable Source for Pseudoalkaloids and Alkamides** (2024): Root (Pipermoola) identified as therapeutically valuable nutraceutical source due to pseudoalkaloids and alkamides with lipase inhibitory activity, supporting anti-obesity potential.

## Recent safety updates

- [Pippali](/nighantu/herb/pippali/) Moola used in over 135 classical Ayurvedic formulations with long traditional safety record; however, no standardized human clinical safety data available
- Piperine's bioavailability enhancement can increase plasma levels of co-administered drugs (phenytoin, theophylline, propranolol); significant drug interaction potential requiring monitoring
- Avoid high doses during pregnancy (potential uterotonic effects); may exacerbate gastric acidity in patients with GERD or peptic ulcers

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (root powder), Kwatha (decoction), Capsule, Tablet, [Ghrita](/nighantu/herb/ghrita/), Vati

**Standard Dosage:** 1-3 g root powder daily; 15-20 mL decoction twice daily; 500 mg standardized root extract twice daily

**Bioavailability:** Piper longum root contains piperine at lower concentration than fruit (1-2% vs 4-8%), but has higher piperlongumine content. Root [alkaloids](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) show moderate-to-good oral bioavailability (30-50%). Root-specific sesquiterpenes and [lignans](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-g-l/#lignans) contribute to distinct pharmacological profile compared to fruit. Bioenhancer activity is present but less potent than fruit piperine due to lower concentration.

**Optimal Timing:** After meals for digestive benefits. Before meals with honey for respiratory conditions. Part of [Trikatu](/nighantu/herb/trikatu/) or digestive formulations taken with first morsel of food.

**Standardized Extract:** [Pippali](/nighantu/herb/pippali/)-Moola extract (6:1) standardized to piperine >2%, piperlongumine >1.5%, total alkaloids >4%. HPTLC fingerprint distinguishing root from fruit extract.

**Shelf Life:** Dried root: 18-24 months; Root powder: 18-24 months; Extract: 24 months; Capsules: 24-36 months

**Storage:** Dried root: cool, dry, ventilated storage, below 25 deg C, protected from pest infestation. Powder and extracts: airtight containers, below 25 deg C, RH <55%, protected from light.

**Marker Compounds:** Piperine, Piperlongumine, Piperlonguminine, Methyl piperate, Sesamin, Dihydrostigmasterol, [Piperanine](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-o-q/#piperanine), Brachystamide B

### Extraction Methods

- Hydroalcoholic extraction (60-70% ethanol)
- Aqueous decoction
- Methanol extraction for alkaloid profiling
- Supercritical CO2 for piperine and sesquiterpene extraction
- Traditional Churna preparation by shade-drying and milling

- [Guduchi](/nighantu/herb/guduchi/) (immune modulation with digestive support)
