## Key Phytochemical Constituents

- [Anthocyanins](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#anthocyanins) (delphinidin-3,3',5'-triglucosides - ternatin series)
- [Cyclotides](/glossary/compounds-c-d/#cyclotides) (ultra-stable macrocyclic [peptides](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#peptides))
- [Kaempferol](/reference/kaempferol/) [glycosides](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides)
- [Quercetin](/reference/quercetinquercetin/) and [myricetin](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#myricetin) glycosides
- [Taraxerol](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#taraxerol) and taraxerone ([triterpenoids](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#triterpenoids))
- [Aparajitin](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#aparajitin) (unique peptide)

## How does it work?

- [Nootropic](/glossary/pharmacology/#nootropic) and memory-enhancing effects via acetylcholinesterase inhibition and modulation of cholinergic neurotransmission
- Anti-inflammatory action through suppression of NF-kB pathway and interaction with SRC, ESR1, PI3KR1 protein targets
- [Antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) activity via anthocyanin-mediated free radical scavenging and upregulation of endogenous antioxidant [enzymes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes)
- Proposed [anxiolytic](/reference/anxiolytic/) effect through GABAergic modulation

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Memory enhancer and nootropic (Medhya [Rasayana](/reference/rasayana/)): traditionally used to support memory and learning. Animal studies have reported improved memory and learning, with a cholinergic mechanism proposed.
- Anxiolytic and antidepressant (Unmada-nashaka): traditionally used for anxious and low mood states. Preclinical studies have reported anxiolytic and antidepressant-like effects.
- Anti-diabetic (Pramehaghna): traditionally used for Prameha. In vivo studies have reported lower blood [glucose](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#glucose) and improved insulin sensitivity.
- Complexion enhancer and eye health (Varnya, [Chakshushya](/herb/chakshushya/)): traditionally used for complexion and eye health.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally recognized as safe; widely consumed as herbal tea (butterfly pea tea) globally; no significant adverse effects reported at culinary doses
- Limited human clinical trial data (only 3 clinical studies identified as of 2025); caution advised during pregnancy due to traditional emmenagogue use; potential interactions with anticoagulant and antiplatelet drugs due to blood platelet aggregation inhibition

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (root/seed powder), Swarasa (flower juice), Capsule, Tablet, Phanta (flower infusion, blue tea), Taila (medicated oil), Nasya (nasal drops)

**Standard Dosage:** 2-5g root powder twice daily; 500mg-1g extract capsule; 10-20ml flower juice; blue tea infusion ad libitum

**Optimal Timing:** Morning on empty stomach for Medhya (cognitive) effects; blue tea anytime; root powder with warm milk at bedtime for Vata pacification

**Storage:** Protect from light and moisture (anthocyanins are photosensitive). Airtight containers, cool and dry, below 25°C. Nitrogen-flush packaging for flower powder.

**Marker Compounds:** Ternatin A1-D3 (anthocyanins), Taraxerol, Taraxerone, Kaempferol glycosides, Cliotide CT1-CT12 (cyclotides), Delphinidin-3,3',5'-triglucoside, Aparajitin

### Synergistic Combinations

- With [Brahmi](/herb/brahmi/) for Medhya (nootropic) action, classical combination
- With Shankhapushpi for memory enhancement
- With [Vacha](/herb/vacha/) for cognitive support and speech disorders
- With [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/) for anxiolytic synergy
- With [Yashtimadhu](/herb/yashtimadhu/) for neuroprotection
