## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Bhavaprakasha (circa 16th century CE) and Sharangadhara Samhita. Described as a [Rasayana](/nighantu/reference/rasayana/) (rejuvenator) specifically for improving Virya (vital strength) and fertility.

## How does it work?

- [Adaptogenic](/nighantu/reference/adaptogenic/) action through modulation of HPA axis and cortisol reduction ([Ashwagandha](/nighantu/herb/ashwagandha/) [withanolides](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-t-z/#withanolides))
- BDNF boosting enhances synaptic plasticity, neuroprotection, and antidepressant effects
- [Immunomodulatory](/nighantu/reference/immunomodulatory/) action enhances both innate and adaptive immunity for faster convalescence
- Rejuvenative (Rasayana) action through combined [antioxidant](/nighantu/reference/antioxidant/), anti-inflammatory, and anabolic properties

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Stress and anxiety relief - validated by cortisol reduction in placebo-controlled study
- Chronic fatigue and debility - supported by RCT showing fatigue severity improvement
- Convalescence after febrile illness - corroborated by dengue recovery study
- Vitality and reproductive health - traditional Rasayana use supported by adaptogenic and anabolic mechanisms

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Cortisol reduction and stress relief with Ashwagandharishta** (2019): Placebo-controlled study showed significant reduction in cortisol levels and perceived stress scores after 8 weeks of daily dosing.
- **Ashwagandharishta in chronic fatigue syndrome** (2020): Randomized trial (n=80) reported significant improvement in fatigue severity scores after 12 weeks of 12 ml twice-daily dosing.
- **Ashwagandharishta as adjuvant in dengue convalescence** (2021): Small clinical trial showed 68% faster convalescence in dengue patients supplemented with Ashwagandharishta.

## Recent safety updates

- Contains 5-10% self-generated alcohol from fermentation. Contraindicated in pregnancy and during lactation. [Ashwagandha](/nighantu/herb/ashwagandha/) may alter thyroid hormone levels; monitor in thyroid patients.
- May potentiate sedative and [anxiolytic](/nighantu/reference/anxiolytic/) medications. Overdosage can cause gastric irritation, nausea, or diarrhea. Use under physician supervision in autoimmune conditions.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Arishta (fermented decoction), Capsules (modern), Syrup (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** 15-30 mL twice daily after meals with equal water, as per AFI Part I

**Bioavailability:** Fermentation enhances extraction and bioavailability of withanolides (particularly withanolide A and withaferin A). Alcohol medium improves lipophilic withanolide absorption. Honey addition (pre-fermentation) provides additional enzymatic bioconversion of [glycosides](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides).

**Optimal Timing:** After meals, twice daily, preferably after lunch and dinner. Best results with long-term use (3-6 months).

**Shelf Life:** 10 years from date of manufacture as per ASU guidelines for Arishta preparations

**Storage:** Store in well-closed amber glass or HDPE containers in a cool, dry place. Protect from direct sunlight. Temperature not exceeding 30°C.

**Marker Compounds:** Withanolide A, Withaferin A, [Withanone](/nighantu/glossary/compounds-t-z/#withanone), Withanoside IV, 12-Deoxywithastramonolide

**Quality Parameters:** pH (3.0-4.5), specific gravity (1.02-1.06), self-generated alcohol (5-12%), total solids, total sugar, withanolide A content by HPLC, withaferin A content, total acidity, microbial limits, heavy metals, absence of methanol

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Equal quantity of warm water
