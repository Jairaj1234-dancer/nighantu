## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita. Originally classified as a Medhya [Rasayana](/reference/rasayana/) (mind rejuvenator) from the Vedic period (circa 1000 BCE). Used in classical texts for neurological imbalances including neurosis, psychosis, epilepsy, and stuttering.

## How does it work?

- Cholinergic modulation by [Brahmi](/herb/brahmi/) and Shankhapushpi is a proposed mechanism linked to memory and cognition
- BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor) boosting by [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/) supports synaptic plasticity and neuroprotection
- GABAergic and serotonergic modulation by [Vacha](/herb/vacha/) and [Brahmi](/herb/brahmi/) is a proposed mechanism for [anxiolytic](/reference/anxiolytic/) and anti-epileptic activity
- [Neuroprotective](/reference/neuroprotective/) action through [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) compounds reduces oxidative stress in neural tissue

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Memory enhancement and cognitive support: traditionally used for memory and cognitive support. Animal studies in mice and a clinical study have reported effects on memory.
- Speech disorders and stuttering in children: traditionally used for speech disorders and stuttering in children.
- Anxiety and mental fatigue: traditionally used for anxiety and mental fatigue. Research has reported GABA and [serotonin](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#serotonin) modulation.
- Epilepsy (adjunct): traditionally used as an adjunct in epilepsy. A network pharmacology analysis has reported pathways linked to anticonvulsant activity.

## Recent safety updates

- Contains self-generated alcohol from fermentation. Should be used cautiously in children, pregnant women, and individuals with liver conditions.
- [Vacha](/herb/vacha/) (Acorus calamus) contains beta-asarone which has regulatory restrictions in some countries. Long-term use should be monitored by a qualified physician.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Arishta/Asava (fermented liquid), Syrup (modern adaptation)

**Standard Dosage:** 12 to 24 ml with equal water for brain health, as given in the Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, entry 1:36. The formulary attributes this formula to Bhaisajyaratnavali, Rasayanadhikara; 178-184.

**Optimal Timing:** After meals, twice daily

**Storage:** Room temperature, tightly sealed, away from direct sunlight. Do not refrigerate.

**Marker Compounds:** Bacosides A+B, [Withanolides](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#withanolides)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Equal quantity of water after meals
