## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Bhaishajya Ratnavali (Agnimandya / Ajirna Rogadhikara). Also referenced in Yoga Ratnakara and Sharangdhara Samhita. The name 'Ashtak' signifies the eight primary ingredients.

## How does it work?

- Carminative action: Asafoetida (Hing) contains ferulic acid and volatile sulfur compounds that reduce intestinal gas formation and promote gas expulsion.
- Digestive enzyme stimulation: [Trikatu](/herb/trikatu/) ([black pepper](/herb/black-pepper/), long pepper, [ginger](/herb/ginger/)) stimulates secretion of digestive [enzymes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes) including pepsin, lipase, and amylase.
- [Antispasmodic](/glossary/pharmacology/#antispasmodic) effect: Volatile oils from [cumin](/herb/cumin/) and [ajwain](/herb/ajwain/) are reported to relax intestinal smooth muscle.
- [Antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) action: Ferulic acid and [thymol](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#thymol) (from [ajwain](/herb/ajwain/)) provide broad-spectrum activity against gut pathogens (E. coli, S. aureus).
- Gastroprotective mucosal defense: Animal studies report that [ginger](/herb/ginger/) compounds protected gastric mucosa in NSAID-induced and ethanol-induced ulcer models.

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Functional dyspepsia and indigestion: traditionally used for these complaints. A clinical study reported improvement in symptoms.
- Flatulence and bloating: traditionally used for these complaints. An animal study reported a reduction in intestinal gas volume.
- IBS symptoms: traditionally used for these complaints. Preliminary clinical reports described changes in pain, bloating and stool consistency.
- Stomach lining: animal studies in ibuprofen- and ethanol-induced ulcer models have reported mucosal protection.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe at recommended doses. May cause stomach irritation in some patients, particularly those with gastric sensitivity.
- Contains rock salt ([Saindhava Lavana](/herb/saindhava-lavana/)); patients with hypertension and kidney-related diseases should use with caution and medical supervision.
- Not advised during pregnancy. Should not be taken without medical advice for prolonged periods. Those on blood pressure or cardiac medications should consult physician.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (fine powder), Capsules (modern), Tablets (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** 1 to 2 g twice daily with first morsel of food or before meals, as given in the Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, entry 7:37. The formulary's anupana: ghee. The formulary attributes this formula to Bhaisajyaratnavali, Agnimandyadirogadhikara; 37.

**Optimal Timing:** Immediately before meals or with first morsel of food

**Storage:** Store in airtight containers in a cool, dry place. Sealed packaging due to strong aroma. Temperature not exceeding 30°C.

**Marker Compounds:** [Piperine](/reference/piperine/), 6-[Gingerol](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#gingerol), [Cuminaldehyde](/glossary/compounds-c-d/#cuminaldehyde), Ferulic acid, Thymol

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Ghee, as given in the formulary; with first morsel of food
