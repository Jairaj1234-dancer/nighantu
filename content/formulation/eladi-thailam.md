## Key Ingredients

- Ela (Elettaria cardamomum / Green Cardamom) - lead herb of Eladi Gana
- Sthula Ela (Amomum subulatum / Black Cardamom)
- Kunduruka (Boswellia serrata / Indian Frankincense)
- Kumkuma (Crocus sativus / [Saffron](/herb/saffron/))
- [Chandana](/herb/chandana/) (Santalum album / Sandalwood)
- [Jatamansi](/herb/jatamansi/) (Nardostachys [jatamansi](/herb/jatamansi/))
- Nagkesar (Mesua ferrea)
- [Devadaru](/herb/devadaru/) (Cedrus deodara)
- [Bala](/herb/bala/) (Sida cordifolia)
- [Hrivera](/herb/hrivera/) (Pavonia odorata)
- Karpura (Cinnamomum camphora / [Camphor](/herb/camphor/))
- Coconut oil or [Sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila) base

## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Sahasrayogam (Taila Prakarana), specifically referencing the Eladi Gana - a traditional group of 18 aromatic herbs led by Cardamom (Ela). Available in both [sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila) and [coconut oil](/glossary/concepts-a-m/#coconut-oil) (Eladi Kera Thailam) bases.

## How does it work?

- Volatile oils (1,8-[cineole](/glossary/compounds-c-d/#cineole)) from Cardamom provide anti-inflammatory and [antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) action on skin surface
- Boswellic acids from Indian Frankincense have been reported in laboratory studies to inhibit the 5-lipoxygenase enzyme
- Santalol from Sandalwood provides cooling, anti-inflammatory, and barrier-repair effects by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines and transepidermal water loss
- Nardostachin from [Jatamansi](/herb/jatamansi/) provides calming and antipruritic (anti-itch) effects through modulation of [histamine](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#histamine) receptors in the skin

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Acne vulgaris: traditionally used for acne. A randomized controlled trial reported overall improvement and a reduction in lesions.
- Urticaria (hives) and pruritus (itching): traditionally used for hives and itching. Pharmacological studies have reported antipruritic and anti-inflammatory properties of compounds from Cardamom and Frankincense.
- Allergic dermatitis and eczema: traditionally used as a supportive therapy, with careful constitutional assessment. Pharmacological studies have reported anti-inflammatory activity of Boswellic acids and santalol.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally considered one of the safest Ayurvedic oils for regular skin use. However, documented cases of Type I and Type IV hypersensitivity exist (PMC 2014), especially when used without proper constitutional (Prakriti) assessment. Patch test mandatory before regular use.
- Can produce hypersensitivity reactions if not prescribed according to constitution, disease, season, and Dosha. Not a substitute for diagnosed eczema or dermatitis treatment. Complementary to medical treatment only. Avoid use on oozing or infected skin lesions.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Thailam (medicated oil for external use), Cream (modern), Lotion (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** External application: sufficient quantity on affected area, twice daily.

**Optimal Timing:** After bath or before bedtime for skin conditions. Can be used for facial application.

**Storage:** Store in amber glass bottles in a cool, dry place. Temperature not exceeding 25°C. Protect from light.

**Marker Compounds:** 1,8-Cineole, Alpha-terpinyl acetate, [Linalool](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#linalool), Alpha-santalol (from [Chandana](/herb/chandana/) if included), Agarospirol (from Agaru)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Not applicable, external use only
