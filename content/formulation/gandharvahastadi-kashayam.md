## Where is it described in the classical texts?

The Ayurvedic Formulary of India attributes it to Sahasrayogam (kasayaprakarana), where it is referenced for constipation and flatulence.

## How does it work?

- Prostaglandin-mediated stimulation of intestinal motility by [Eranda](/herb/eranda/) (castor root) promotes bowel evacuation
- Agni (digestive fire) enhancement by Shunthi and Musta improves overall digestive capacity
- [Haritaki](/herb/haritaki/) and [Trivrit](/herb/trivrit/) are classically described as mild purgatives
- [Vata-balancing](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#vata-balancing) properties of [Devadaru](/herb/devadaru/) and [Daruharidra](/herb/daruharidra/) are traditionally described as Vata-pacifying

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Constipation: traditionally used for constipation. A case series reported improved bowel regulation.
- Flatulence and bloating: traditionally used for flatulence and bloating, to enhance digestive fire (Agni).
- Vata disorders with low back pain: traditionally used for Vata disorders with low back pain. Its ingredients include [Devadaru](/herb/devadaru/) and [Daruharidra](/herb/daruharidra/).
- Loss of appetite: traditionally used for loss of appetite. Its ingredients include Shunthi and Musta.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe at recommended dosage. Excessive use may lead to loose stools or abdominal cramping. Not recommended during pregnancy due to purgative ingredients.
- Should be used cautiously in patients with inflammatory bowel disease. Physician supervision recommended for prolonged use to prevent electrolyte imbalance.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Kashayam (decoction), Kashayam tablet (modern), Concentrated liquid (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** 15-30 mL or 2 tablets twice daily before meals. The Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, entry 4:5 specifies 48 g of the kvatha curna to prepare the decoction, which is a quantity of drug rather than a dose taken, so it is not comparable with the volume above. The formulary attributes this formula to Sahasrayoga, kasayaprakarana; 394.

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals, or at bedtime with [Eranda](/herb/eranda/) Taila for Vatanulomana (normalizing Vata)

**Storage:** Fresh: use same day. Concentrates: amber glass at room temperature. Tablets: airtight containers at room temperature.

**Marker Compounds:** Ricinoleic acid derivatives, 6-[Gingerol](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#gingerol) (from Shunthi), [Cedrene](/glossary/compounds-c-d/#cedrene) (from [Devadaru](/herb/devadaru/)), Beta-[sitosterol](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Eranda Taila (castor oil) 5-10 mL is the classical anupana
