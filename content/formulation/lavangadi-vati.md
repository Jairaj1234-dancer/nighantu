## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Sharangdhara Samhita (Madhyama Khanda); also described in Bhaishajya Ratnavali (Kasa/Shwasa Chikitsa - cough and respiratory disorder sections) and Yoga Ratnakara

## How does it work?

- [Expectorant](/reference/expectorant/) and mucolytic: [Eugenol](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#eugenol) from [Lavanga](/herb/lavanga/) (clove) and warming herbs are traditionally used to loosen mucus and ease expectoration
- Anti-inflammatory throat and lung action: Combined eugenol, gingerols, and [piperine](/reference/piperine/) reduce inflammation in the throat, bronchi, and lung tissue
- [Antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) defense: Eugenol, piperine, and [catechins](/glossary/compounds-c-d/#catechins) from [Khadira](/herb/khadira/) provide broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity against oral and other pathogens (Streptococcus mutans, Staphylococcus aureus)
- Kapha dissolution: Hot potency (Ushna Virya) of [Trikatu](/herb/trikatu/) and clove is classically described as reducing Kapha in the respiratory tract
- [Immunomodulatory](/reference/immunomodulatory/) action: Combined ingredients demonstrate antiallergic and immunomodulatory properties, supporting immune defense against respiratory infections

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Traditionally used for cough and respiratory infections. Antimicrobial activity against S. mutans and S. aureus and immunomodulatory properties have been reported.
- Traditionally used for throat irritation and sore throat.
- Traditionally used for oral health, including dental pain and oral infections. Eugenol has been reported to have [analgesic](/reference/analgesic/) and antimicrobial properties.
- Traditionally used for Kapha-related respiratory congestion, drawing on its combined heating ingredients.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe for oral use as a lozenge; may cause mild gastric warmth or irritation in Pitta-predominant individuals; avoid in patients with gastric ulcer or hyperacidity
- Not recommended during pregnancy in medicinal doses; caution in young children with clove and pepper content; discontinue if oral mucosal irritation occurs; Acacia arabica may cause constipation in some individuals

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Vati (pill/tablet), Capsule

**Standard Dosage:** 1 g, as given in the Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, entry 12:26. 1-2 tablets to dissolve in mouth. The page's tablet count and this weight relate only through the mass of a tablet, which the formulary does not state. The formulary's anupana: warm water. The formulary attributes this formula to Vaidyajivanam, kasasvasacikitsa, 7.

**Optimal Timing:** Before or after meals as indicated

**Storage:** Airtight container, cool dry place, protected from moisture

**Marker Compounds:** Eugenol, [Cineole](/glossary/compounds-c-d/#cineole)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Warm water or as specified

### Synergistic Combinations

- Key herbs: [Lavanga](/herb/lavanga/) (clove), Karpura, [Jatiphala](/herb/jatiphala/)
