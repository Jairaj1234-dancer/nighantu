## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Sharangdhara Samhita (Madhyama Khanda); described in various Samhitas and classical texts for Mukha Roga (oral diseases); also referenced in Bhaishajya Ratnavali (Mukha Roga Chikitsa) and Yoga Ratnakara

## How does it work?

- Oral antiseptic action: [Khadira](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/khadira/) bark rich in [catechins](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-c-d/#catechins) ([flavonoids](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/flavonoids/), [catechin](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/catechin/), catechuic acid, pyrocatechin, [quercetin](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/quercetinquercetin/)) provides astringent and [antimicrobial](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antimicrobial/) action against oral pathogens including Streptococcus mutans
- Anti-inflammatory mucosal healing: [Yashtimadhu](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/yashtimadhu/) ([glycyrrhizin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycyrrhizinglycyrrhizin)) soothes inflamed oral and pharyngeal mucosa with cooling Virya (Madhura Rasa), while [Kankola](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/kankola/) and [Lavanga](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/lavanga/) reduce local inflammation
- Bioavailability enhancement: [Pippali](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/pippali/) ([piperine](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/piperine/)) boosts bioavailability of active compounds and warms channels through Ushna Virya, clearing stagnation in respiratory and oral Srotas
- Astringent tissue tonification: [Khadira](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/khadira/)'s Kashaya Rasa (astringent taste) tightens gum tissue, reduces bleeding, and strengthens oral mucosal integrity
- Antimicrobial and deodorant: [Camphor](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/camphor/) (Kapoor) and [Lavanga](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/lavanga/) ([eugenol](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-d-g/#eugenol)) provide broad-spectrum antimicrobial action and natural deodorant effect for halitosis management

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Oral health management (tonsillitis, gingivitis, stomatitis, mouth ulcers) validated through antimicrobial activity against Streptococcus and E. coli in in-vitro studies
- Cough and sore throat relief validated in clinical trial showing 65% improvement in cough scores within two weeks
- Halitosis (foul breath) management validated through combined antimicrobial and deodorant action of [camphor](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/camphor/), clove, and cardamom
- Voice clarity and hoarseness reduction validated through anti-inflammatory and mucosal-soothing actions of [Yashtimadhu](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/yashtimadhu/) and [Khadira](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/khadira/)

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **A Critical Review on [Khadiradi Vati](/ayurveda-atlas/formulation/khadiradi-vati/) - An Ayurvedic Formulation** (2024): Comprehensive review documented that the formulation's flavonoids, [phytosterols](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-o-q/#phytosterols), and [alkaloids](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) provide anti-inflammatory and [antioxidant](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antioxidant/) actions effective in treating tonsillitis, gingivitis, and other oral diseases.
- **In-vitro antimicrobial activity of [Khadiradi Vati](/ayurveda-atlas/formulation/khadiradi-vati/) against oral pathogens** (2020): AYU Journal research confirmed action against Streptococcus and E. coli strains, validating traditional oral antiseptic claims.
- **Clinical trial of [Khadiradi Vati](/ayurveda-atlas/formulation/khadiradi-vati/) on cough and throat conditions** (2018): Journal of Ayurveda and Integrative Medicine small clinical trial showed 65% improvement in cough scores within two weeks of treatment.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe for oral use as a lozenge/dissolvable tablet; contains [Gairika](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-a-m/#gairika) (red ochre mineral) which is safe in traditional doses; avoid swallowing large quantities; not recommended for children under 5 years
- [Camphor](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/camphor/) component contraindicated in very young children (risk of seizures); avoid in known allergy to any ingredient; long-term continuous use may cause oral dryness due to astringent action; pregnancy and lactation - use under guidance

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Vati (lozenge), Modern lozenges, Mouth-dissolving tablets (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** 1-2 tablets to be sucked slowly, 3-4 times daily, as per AFI Part I

**Bioavailability:** Buccal dissolution provides direct delivery of catechin and [tannins](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/tannins/) to oral mucosa. Catechin has astringent and antimicrobial action on oral tissues. [Camphor](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/camphor/) and Ela volatiles provide immediate sensory effect. No first-pass metabolism for buccally absorbed compounds.

**Optimal Timing:** As needed for Mukha roga (oral diseases), throat irritation, or after meals for oral hygiene

**Shelf Life:** 2 years from date of manufacture as per ASU guidelines for Vati

**Storage:** Store in airtight containers in a cool, dry place. Protect from moisture and heat. Temperature not exceeding 25°C. Individual wrapping recommended.

**Marker Compounds:** Catechin, [Epicatechin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-d-g/#epicatechin), [Camphor](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/camphor/), Eugenol (from [Lavanga](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/lavanga/)), 1,8-[Cineole](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-c-d/#cineole) (from Ela)

**Quality Parameters:** Weight variation (±5%), hardness (moderate for buccal use), dissolution time (5-15 min buccal), moisture (<6%), catechin content from [Khadira](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/khadira/) by HPLC, camphor content, volatile oil content, HPTLC fingerprint, microbial limits (oral product), heavy metals

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Not applicable — sucked directly in mouth. Do not chew or swallow whole.
