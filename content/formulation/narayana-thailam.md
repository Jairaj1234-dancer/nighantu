## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Bhaishajya Ratnavali (Vatavyadhi Chikitsa, verses 140-150) by Govind Das Sen. The foundational Narayana formulation containing 33 herbs (the 'Maha' version adds Ashtavarga to reach 50+ herbs). Named after Lord Narayana (Vishnu) for its protective and restorative properties.

## How does it work?

- [Sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila) lipid medium serves as a transdermal delivery vehicle for lipophilic [saponins](/reference/saponins/), [lignans](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#lignans), and phenolics
- [Dashamoola](/herb/dashamoola/)'s combined action of 10 root extracts is proposed to provide anti-inflammatory activity through simultaneous inhibition of multiple inflammatory pathways (COX, LOX, NF-kB)
- [Tagara](/herb/tagara/)'s [valepotriates](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#valepotriates) and [Jatamansi](/herb/jatamansi/)'s nardostachin provide dual nervine sedative and [antispasmodic](/glossary/pharmacology/#antispasmodic) action, addressing both the pain and spasm components of Vata disorders
- [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/)'s [withanolides](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#withanolides) and [Bala](/herb/bala/)'s [ephedrine](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#ephedrine) [alkaloids](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) are proposed to support neuromuscular function and to have [analgesic](/reference/analgesic/) effects

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Vatavyadhi (Vata-related conditions), including paralysis, tremors, and muscular atrophy: traditionally used for these conditions. Observational studies have reported improvement in neuromuscular function.
- Lower back pain and lumbar spasm: traditionally used for these complaints. A pilot clinical study reported improvement in pain with nightly oleation and Swedana.
- Osteoarthritis stiffness and swelling: traditionally used for these complaints. Observational studies have reported reduced symptoms with regular Abhyanga.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe for external application (Abhyanga). Balanced formulation suitable for regular use. Mild skin warmth is normal after application. Patch test recommended for sensitive individuals. No significant drug interactions for external use reported.
- Internal administration (Basti, Nasya, oral use) should only be under Ayurvedic practitioner supervision. Not recommended during acute Pitta flare-ups or fever. Caution in individuals on anticoagulant or anti-inflammatory medications. The Maha Narayana version is more potent but also heavier and less suitable for daily use.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Thailam (medicated [sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila)), External application

**Standard Dosage:** External massage and Basti: 30-60ml

**Bioavailability:** Oil base ([sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila)) enhances dermal absorption of lipophilic active compounds; for internal thailams, oil base enhances absorption of fat-soluble actives

**Optimal Timing:** External: before bath or at bedtime. Internal: before meals

**Shelf Life:** 3 years (medicated oils per ASU guidelines)

**Storage:** Airtight amber glass/HDPE container, room temperature, protected from light

**Marker Compounds:** Shatavarin, Withanolides

**Quality Parameters:** Acid value <5, saponification value per base oil specs, refractive index, moisture NMT 0.5%, specific gravity, marker: Shatavarin, Withanolides

**Vehicle (Anupana):** External: massage into affected area. Internal: with warm water/milk

### Synergistic Combinations

- Key herbs: [Shatavari](/herb/shatavari/), [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/), [Dashamoola](/herb/dashamoola/); oil base acts as lipid carrier for enhanced tissue penetration
