## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Bhavaprakasha Nighantu (16th century); composed of 42 medicinal plants in the standard version; Maha (expanded) version contains approximately 50 ingredients; also referenced in Sharangdhara Samhita

## How does it work?

- [Antipyretic](/reference/antipyretic/) pathway: Swertiamarin (terpenoid) in Swertia chirayita is proposed to act on hypothalamic thermoregulatory centers and to inhibit prostaglandin synthesis via COX-2 suppression
- Antimalarial action: Chirayita and [Neem](/herb/neem/) contain bitter compounds (swertiamarin, [nimbin](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#nimbin)) with documented antiplasmodial activity against Plasmodium species
- Diaphoretic and [diuretic](/reference/diuretic/): The combination is traditionally described as inducing perspiration and promoting urination
- Agni-Ama axis regulation: Bitter-cool herbs clear Pitta and neutralize toxins while warming pungents (Shunthi, [Pippali](/herb/pippali/)) kindle Agni to metabolize Ama, restoring digestive equilibrium

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Jwara Chikitsa (fever care): traditionally used for fever. A pilot study reported faster fever resolution compared to placebo.
- Antiplasmodial properties have been reported for Swertia chirata and [Neem](/herb/neem/).
- [Antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) activity against gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria has been reported in in vitro disc diffusion assays.
- Traditionally used to restore appetite and improve digestion. In classical practice this is attributed to the Agni-enhancing action of [Trikatu](/herb/trikatu/) components.

## Recent safety updates

- Extremely bitter taste may cause nausea; avoid in patients with chronic diarrhea, peptic ulcer, or gastric hyperacidity due to predominance of bitter herbs
- Not recommended during pregnancy and lactation; caution in diabetic patients as several ingredients possess hypoglycemic activity; monitor blood sugar if used alongside antidiabetic medications

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (fine powder), Capsule, Tablet

**Standard Dosage:** 2 to 4 g with warm water for fever, as given in the Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, entry 7:35. The formulary's anupana: warm water. The formulary attributes this formula to Bhaisajyaratnavali, Jvaradhikara; 308-312.

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals

**Storage:** Airtight container, cool dry place, protected from moisture

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Warm water

### Synergistic Combinations

- With Warm water as vehicle for enhanced absorption
