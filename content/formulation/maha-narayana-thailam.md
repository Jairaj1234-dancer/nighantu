## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Bhaishajya Ratnavali (Vatavyadhi Chikitsa) by Govind Das Sen. The 'Maha' (great) version is an expanded formulation of [Narayana Thailam](/formulation/narayana-thailam/) with 50-56+ herbs including the rare Ashtavarga group. Also referenced in Sushruta Samhita commentaries.

## How does it work?

- [Withanolides](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#withanolides) from [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/) have been reported to suppress NF-kB pathway activation and inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-6)
- [Sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila) base serves as a penetration enhancer, delivering lipophilic [saponins](/reference/saponins/), [lignans](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#lignans), and phenolics transdermally to deep musculoskeletal tissues
- [Guduchi](/herb/guduchi/)'s [berberine](/glossary/compounds-a-c/#berberine) and tinosporine [alkaloids](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) have been reported in preclinical studies to have [immunomodulatory](/reference/immunomodulatory/) and anti-arthritic activity
- [Rasna](/herb/rasna/)'s clerodane [diterpenes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#diterpenes) have been reported to have anti-inflammatory activity

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Osteoarthritis and joint pain: traditionally applied through Abhyanga for these conditions. A study reported reduced symptoms with Abhyanga.
- Vata-vyadhi (neuromuscular disorders) including paralysis, muscular atrophy, and tremors: traditionally used for these conditions. Pharmacological studies have reported compounds with [neuroprotective](/reference/neuroprotective/) and anti-inflammatory activity.
- Bone and joint strengthening (Asthi-Sandhi Balya): traditionally used to strengthen bones and joints. Research has reported [phytosterols](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#phytosterols) and calcium-channel active compounds from [Dashamoola](/herb/dashamoola/) and [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/).

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe for external application. Some individuals with sensitive skin may experience mild warmth or redness due to heating herbs. Not recommended for acute inflammatory pitta conditions or open wounds. Patch test advised for first-time users.
- Internal administration should only be under practitioner supervision. Caution in individuals on blood-thinning medications due to potential interaction with anti-inflammatory phytoconstituents. Heavy formulation may not be suitable for daily use in Kapha-dominant individuals.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Thailam (medicated [sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila)), External application

**Standard Dosage:** External massage or Basti (enema): 30-60ml. The Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, entry 8:45 states a dose of 6 g; that is a different unit from the figure above and the two cannot be compared without knowing the preparation's density or tablet mass. The formulary's anupana: milk, warm water. The formulary attributes this formula to Bhaisajyaratnavali, Vatavyadhyadhikara; 151-157.

**Optimal Timing:** External: before bath or at bedtime. Internal: before meals

**Storage:** Airtight amber glass/HDPE container, room temperature, protected from light

**Marker Compounds:** Withanolides, [Aegeline](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#aegeline), [Sesamin](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sesamin)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** External: massage into affected area. Internal: with warm water/milk

### Synergistic Combinations
