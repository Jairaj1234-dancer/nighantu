## Key Ingredients

- Patranga (Caesalpinia sappan / Sappan wood)
- [Khadira](/herb/khadira/) (Acacia catechu)
- [Vasaka](/herb/vasaka/) (Adhatoda vasica)
- Semal flowers (Bombax ceiba)
- [Bala](/herb/bala/) (Sida cordifolia)
- Gurhal (Hibiscus rosa-sinensis)
- [Trikatu](/herb/trikatu/) ([Pippali](/herb/pippali/), Maricha, Shunthi)
- Trivrutta (Operculina turpethum)
- [Devadaru](/herb/devadaru/) (Cedrus deodara)
- [Dhataki](/herb/dhataki/) pushpa (Woodfordia fruticosa)
- Jaggery (fermentation medium)

## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Described in Bhaishajya Ratnavali, in the chapter on stri roga (pradara).

## How does it work?

- [Hemostatic](/glossary/pharmacology/#hemostatic) and astringent action of Patranga and [Khadira](/herb/khadira/) is traditionally described as checking heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia)
- Anti-inflammatory action of [Vasaka](/herb/vasaka/) and [Devadaru](/herb/devadaru/) is traditionally used for uterine inflammation and pain
- [Bala](/herb/bala/) and Gurhal are traditionally regarded as uterine tonics

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Heavy menstrual bleeding (Raktapradar/Menorrhagia): traditionally used for heavy menstrual bleeding. Studies have reported hemostatic and astringent activity.
- Leucorrhea (Shweta Pradar): traditionally used for leucorrhea. It has been described as having uterine tonic and [antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) properties.
- PCOS and menstrual irregularity: used in practice for menstrual irregularity, including PCOS. A clinical study reported improved menstrual regularity.
- Endometriosis-related pain: used in practice for pain related to endometriosis. Clinical observations have reported reduced pain and inflammation.

## Recent safety updates

- Contains 5-10% self-generated alcohol. Not recommended during pregnancy. May interact with hormonal medications and oral contraceptives.
- Generally safe at recommended dosage. Prolonged high-dose use without medical supervision not recommended. Women on anticoagulants should use cautiously due to hemostatic properties.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Arishta/Asava (fermented liquid), Syrup (modern adaptation)

**Standard Dosage:** 15-30ml with equal water for bleeding disorders

**Bioavailability:** Self-generated alcohol enhances extraction and absorption of both water-soluble and fat-soluble compounds; fermentation produces bioactive metabolites not present in raw herbs

**Optimal Timing:** After meals, twice daily

**Shelf Life:** Indefinite per classical texts; commercially labeled 5-10 years. Improves with age.

**Storage:** Room temperature, tightly sealed, away from direct sunlight. Do not refrigerate.

**Marker Compounds:** Brazilin, Brazilein

**Quality Parameters:** Alcohol content 5-12% v/v, pH 3.0-4.5, specific gravity 1.01-1.08, total solids 15-25%, marker: Brazilin, Brazilein

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Equal quantity of water after meals

### Synergistic Combinations

- Key herbs: Patranga (Caesalpinia sappan), [Dhataki](/herb/dhataki/), other herbs; fermentation creates unique metabolite profile with enhanced therapeutic activity
