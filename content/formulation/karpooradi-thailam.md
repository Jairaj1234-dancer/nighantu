## Key Ingredients

- Karpoora (Cinnamomum camphora / [Camphor](/herb/camphor/)) - primary ingredient
- Ajamoda (Apium graveolens / Celery/Wild Celery)
- [Coconut oil](/glossary/concepts-a-m/#coconut-oil) ([Narikela](/herb/narikela/) taila) base (or [Sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila) in some preparations)

## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Sahasrayogam (Taila Prakarana). A characteristically simple 3-ingredient formulation from Kerala tradition. The name means 'oil based on [camphor](/herb/camphor/) (Karpoora)'. Primarily used as a warming chest rub and pain-relief oil.

## How does it work?

- [Camphor](/herb/camphor/) activates TRPM8 (cold-sensitive) and TRPA1 receptors on sensory neurons, producing [analgesic](/reference/analgesic/) and counter-irritant effects that override pain signals
- [Camphor](/herb/camphor/) vapors stimulate cold-sensitive receptors in respiratory epithelium, causing reflex bronchodilation and subjective relief of nasal and chest congestion
- [Apigenin](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#apigenin) from Ajamoda (celery) provides anti-inflammatory and smooth muscle relaxant effects, complementing [camphor](/herb/camphor/)'s decongestant action on airways
- Animal studies in rat paw edema models have reported that [camphor](/herb/camphor/) downregulated COX-2 and reduced prostaglandin-mediated inflammation at the site of topical application.

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Joint pain and musculoskeletal conditions: traditionally used as a topical application. A pilot clinical study reported reduced pain scores in knee osteoarthritis with topical use.
- Respiratory congestion (chest rub application): traditionally used as a chest rub. Pharmacological studies have reported TRPM8 receptor-mediated reflex bronchodilation from [camphor](/herb/camphor/) vapors.
- Kapha-predominant conditions with stiffness and congestion: used in classical practice for these conditions. Research has reported anti-inflammatory COX-2 activity and a smooth muscle relaxant action of celery compounds.

## Recent safety updates

- CAUTION: Camphor is toxic if ingested - strictly for external use only. Harmful to adults, children, and pets if swallowed. Do not add to humidifiers or nebulizers as camphor is highly flammable. Camphor application can cause eczema in sensitive individuals.
- NOT safe for infants and toddlers - camphor toxicity risk is higher in young children. Patch test mandatory for first-time users. Avoid application near eyes, nostrils (internal), and mucous membranes. Discontinue if skin irritation, rash, or eczema develops.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Thailam (medicated oil for external use), Balm (modern), Roll-on (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** External application: sufficient quantity on affected area, 2-3 times daily.

**Optimal Timing:** As needed for pain, headache, nasal congestion. Apply 2-3 times daily. Avoid near eyes and on broken skin.

**Storage:** Store in tightly sealed amber glass bottles. Camphor is highly volatile, loss occurs with loose sealing. Temperature not exceeding 25°C. Keep away from heat sources and flame (camphor is flammable).

**Marker Compounds:** Camphor, 1,8-[Cineole](/glossary/compounds-c-d/#cineole) (eucalyptol), [Borneol](/glossary/compounds-a-c/#borneol), [Camphene](/glossary/compounds-a-c/#camphene)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Not applicable, external use only. Apply and massage gently on painful area.
