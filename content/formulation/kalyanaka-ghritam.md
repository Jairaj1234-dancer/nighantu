## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Ashtanga Hrudayam Uttarasthana 6/26-33 ([Mahakalyanaka Ghritam](/glossary/concepts-a-m/#mahakalyanaka-ghritam)); Charaka Samhita ([Kalyanaka Ghrita](/formulation/kalyanaka-ghrita/)); Chakradatta (Ksheerakalyanaka [Ghrita](/herb/ghrita/)); Bhaisajya Ratnavali; Sharangadhara Samhita; Sahasrayoga

## How does it work?

- Polyherbal synergy of 28+ plant extracts in lipid medium enhances bioavailability of [neuroprotective](/reference/neuroprotective/) compounds
- [Haridra](/herb/haridra/) ([curcumin](/herb/curcumin/)) is reported to have anti-inflammatory and [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) activity
- [Sariva](/herb/sariva/) and [Daruharidra](/herb/daruharidra/) provide cooling, pitta-pacifying action that calms aggravated mental states
- Balances Tridosha with emphasis on Vata-Pitta pacification, restoring equilibrium in Manovaha Srotas (mental channels)

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Unmada (psychosis/schizophrenia): traditionally used for unmada. Clinical studies have reported improvement in psychiatric conditions including OCD.
- Cognitive deficit in children: traditionally used for cognitive support in children. A prospective clinical study reported on its safety and on cognitive improvement in children.
- Fertility enhancement: traditionally used for both male (sperm quality) and female (ovum quality) fertility.
- Apasmara (epilepsy): used in classical practice for apasmara. Pharmacological studies have reported anticonvulsant properties in its ingredients.

## Recent safety updates

- A prospective clinical study (not identified on this page) reported on its safety in children with cognitive deficits
- Caution advised for individuals with diabetes, high cholesterol, heart diseases, and hypertension due to ghee base
- Excessive consumption could lead to gastrointestinal distress; should be used under medical supervision

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Ghritam (medicated ghee), Soft gelatin capsules (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** 12 g twice daily on empty stomach, as given in the Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, entry 6:7. The formulary's anupana: warm milk, warm water. The formulary attributes this formula to Astangahrdaya, Uttarasthana, Adhyaya 6; 26-28½.

**Bioavailability:** Identical to Kalyanaka Ghrita.

**Optimal Timing:** On empty stomach, morning and evening, for Unmada (psychiatric), Apasmara (epilepsy), and cognitive conditions

**Shelf Life:** 2 years from date of manufacture as per ASU guidelines for Ghrita

**Storage:** Store in airtight glass or tin containers in a cool, dry place. Temperature not exceeding 25°C. Protect from light and moisture.

**Marker Compounds:** Bacoside A, Alpha-santalol, Beta-asarone (within limits), [Piperine](/reference/piperine/), Gallic acid

**Quality Parameters:** Same as Kalyanaka Ghrita, acid value, peroxide value, saponification value, refractive index, moisture, rancidity test, multi-marker HPTLC, microbial limits, heavy metals

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Warm water or warm milk
