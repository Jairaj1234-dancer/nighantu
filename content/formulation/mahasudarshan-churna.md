## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Bhavaprakasha Nighantu (16th century, Sudarshan group of preparations); also described in Sharangdhara Samhita; contains approximately 50 ingredients in the Maha (major) version

## How does it work?

- [Antipyretic](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antipyretic/) action: [Swertiamarin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-q-t/#swertiamarin) and [amarogentin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-1-a/#amarogentin) from Swertia chirata exert [immunomodulatory](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/immunomodulatory/) and [hepatoprotective](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/hepatoprotective/) actions; in vitro assays showed 60% inhibition of COX-2 by amarogentin providing molecular basis for anti-inflammatory and fever-reducing effects
- [Antimicrobial](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antimicrobial/) defense: [Neem](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/neem/) bark ([nimbidin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-l-o/#nimbidin)) and [Daruharidra](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/daruharidra/) ([berberine](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-a-c/#berberine)) provide broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity against gram-positive (S. aureus) and gram-negative (E. coli, K. pneumoniae) bacteria
- Three-fold detoxification: Bitter-cool herbs ([Neem](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/neem/), [Chirayata](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/chirayata/)) clear Pitta excess and neutralize toxins; warming pungents (Shunthi, [Pippali](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/pippali/)) kindle Agni to metabolize Ama; [Triphala](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/triphala/) regulates bowel to prevent toxin reabsorption
- Immunomodulation: [Guduchi](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/guduchi/) (Tinospora cordifolia) enhances phagocytic activity and modulates innate immune response against pathogens
- Mucolytic action: [Vasicine](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-t-z/#vasicine) from [Vasa](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/vasa/) (Adhatoda vasica) promotes mucolytic and bronchodilatory responses, aiding respiratory symptoms during fever

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Antipyretic action (Jwaraghna) validated in pilot study showing 25% faster fever reduction compared to placebo
- Antimicrobial activity validated against K. pneumoniae, E. coli, S. aureus, and P. vulgaris in disc diffusion assay
- Immunomodulatory activity validated through anti-inflammatory and antihistamine pharmacological studies
- Digestive improvement and appetite restoration validated through Agni-enhancing mechanisms of [Trikatu](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/trikatu/) and bitter herbs

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Validation and quantification of major biomarkers in [Mahasudarshan Churna](/ayurveda-atlas/formulation/mahasudarshan-churna/) through high-performance thin-layer chromatography** (2020): Developed validated HPTLC method for quantification of [mangiferin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-l-o/#mangiferin) (xanthonoid), oleanolic acid and ursolic acid ([triterpenoids](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-t-z/#triterpenoids)), gallic acid, [quercetin](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/quercetinquercetin/), and [curcumin](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/curcumin/) as biomarkers in the formulation.
- **PLOS One pilot study on antipyretic efficacy of Sudarshan/Mahasudarshan formulations** (2019): Pilot study found 25% faster drop in fever among subjects taking 3g twice daily compared to placebo.
- **Pharmacological studies on antipyretic, antimicrobial, [antioxidant](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antioxidant/), antihistamine, and [analgesic](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/analgesic/) activities of [Sudarshan Churna](/ayurveda-atlas/formulation/sudarshan-churna/)** (2020): Various pharmacological studies proved antipyretic, antimicrobial, antioxidant, antihistamine, and analgesic activities, plus docking studies against SARS-CoV-2 targets.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally well tolerated for short-term use during acute febrile illness; bitter taste may cause nausea in sensitive individuals; avoid in patients with chronic diarrhea or dehydration
- Caution in pregnancy and lactation due to multiple bitter and heating herbs; long-term use beyond the febrile episode not typically recommended; monitor blood [glucose](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-d-g/#glucose) as some ingredients have hypoglycemic potential

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (fine powder), Capsule, Tablet

**Standard Dosage:** 3-6g twice daily

**Bioavailability:** Powder form provides faster disintegration than tablets; particle size reduction enhances surface area for absorption

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals for fever/infection

**Shelf Life:** 2 years (as per ASU guidelines for churna)

**Storage:** Airtight container, cool dry place, protected from moisture

**Marker Compounds:** Swertimerin, [Berberine](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-a-c/#berberine), Kutkin

**Quality Parameters:** Mesh size NLT 85#, moisture NMT 6%, total ash per pharmacopoeia, assay of marker compounds: Swertimerin, [Berberine](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-a-c/#berberine), Kutkin

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Warm water or honey

### Synergistic Combinations

- With Warm water or honey as vehicle for enhanced absorption
