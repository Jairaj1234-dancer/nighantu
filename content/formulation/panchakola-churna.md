## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Sushruta Samhita and Ashtanga Hridayam (as a group - Panchakola Gana); elaborated in Chakradatta (11th-12th century CE) and Vangasena Samhita; also referenced in Sharangdhara Samhita

## How does it work?

- Digestive enzyme stimulation: [Piperine](/reference/piperine/) from [Pippali](/herb/pippali/) and [Chavya](/herb/chavya/) enhances secretion of digestive [enzymes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes) (lipase, amylase, trypsin) and stimulates gastric acid production
- Bioavailability enhancement (Yogavahi): Piperine enhances bioavailability of co-administered compounds by up to 30% through P-glycoprotein modulation and glucuronidation inhibition
- Agni kindling (Deepana): Hot potency (Ushna Virya) and pungent taste (Katu Rasa) of all five ingredients collectively kindle digestive fire (Jatharagni) and correct Mandagni (weak digestive fire)
- [Antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) action in gut: [Plumbagin](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#plumbagin) from Chitraka provides antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory action in gut mucosa, maintaining healthy intestinal environment
- Srota clearing: Hot potency clears blockages in digestive channels (Annavaha Srotas), gently purgates Ama, and improves nutrient absorption

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Agni Deepana (digestive fire stimulation): traditionally used to kindle digestive fire. A pilot trial reported improved gastric motility and reduced flatulence.
- Fat digestion: traditionally used to aid fat digestion. In vitro studies have reported lipase activation by [Chavya](/herb/chavya/) and [Pippali](/herb/pippali/) extracts.
- Bioavailability enhancement: piperine has been reported to increase the absorption of co-administered compounds.
- Ama Pachana (toxin digestion): traditionally used for the digestion of toxins.

## Recent safety updates

- A rat toxicity study of Panchakola Avaleha reported a safety profile within acceptable limits, which does not directly establish the churna's safety in humans; may cause gastric irritation or heartburn in Pitta-predominant individuals; avoid in gastric ulcer, hyperacidity, and inflammatory bowel disease
- Contraindicated during pregnancy (all five ingredients are hot in potency); not recommended in children under 5 years without physician guidance; piperine content may alter drug pharmacokinetics - caution with concurrent prescription medications

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Churna (fine powder), Capsule, Tablet

**Standard Dosage:** 1-3g before meals

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals as digestive

**Storage:** Airtight container, cool dry place, protected from moisture

**Marker Compounds:** Piperine, [Gingerol](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#gingerol), Plumbagin

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Warm water or buttermilk

### Synergistic Combinations

- With Warm water or buttermilk as vehicle for enhanced absorption
