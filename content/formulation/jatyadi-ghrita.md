## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Chakradatta (12th century CE); Sushruta Samhita (wound management context); Madhava Nidana (14th century CE); Bhavaprakasha (16th century CE) - Vrana Ropana Chikitsa

## How does it work?

- [Neem](/herb/neem/) ([nimbidin](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#nimbidin)) has been reported to show antibacterial and antifungal activity against wound-infecting pathogens including MRSA
- [Turmeric](/herb/turmeric/) ([curcumin](/herb/curcumin/)) inhibits inflammatory mediators (COX-2, NF-kB) and promotes tissue granulation and re-epithelialization
- Jasmine ([Jati](/herb/jati/)) is traditionally described as cooling and as supporting wound healing
- Copper sulfate ([Tuttha](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tuttha)) provides mild antiseptic and astringent action, promoting wound contraction
- Beeswax (Siktha) creates protective barrier over wound surface while allowing gas exchange, maintaining moist healing environment
- Ghee base provides lipid nutrition to wound bed, supporting cell membrane repair and tissue regeneration

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Dushta Vrana (chronic/infected wounds): traditionally used for chronic and infected wounds. Clinical studies have reported wound repair comparable to the antibiotic ointment mupirocin.
- Dagdha Vrana (burn wounds): traditionally used for burn wounds. Animal studies have reported faster burn wound repair.
- Antibacterial action: studies have reported activity against MRSA and multidrug-resistant organisms involved in diabetic foot infections.
- Radiation-induced wounds: an experimental study reported a reduction in wound area in a gamma-radiation skin wound model.

## Recent safety updates

- [Tuttha](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tuttha) (copper sulfate) content requires careful quality control; excessive copper may cause local irritation in sensitive individuals
- For external use only; not for deep puncture wounds without proper wound assessment

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** [Ghrita](/herb/ghrita/) (medicated ghee for topical application), Ointment (modern), Cream (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** Topical application on wounds, ulcers, burns, thin layer 1-2 times daily. The Ayurvedic Formulary of India attributes this formula to Astangahrdaya, Uttarasthana, Adhyaya 25; 67-68.

**Optimal Timing:** After wound cleaning, 1-2 times daily. Apply thin layer and cover with sterile dressing if needed.

**Storage:** Store in airtight glass or tin containers in a cool, dry place. Temperature not exceeding 25°C. Topical wound preparation requires enhanced hygiene during storage and dispensing.

**Marker Compounds:** [Berberine](/glossary/compounds-a-c/#berberine) (from [Daruharidra](/herb/daruharidra/)), Azadirachtin (from [Nimba](/herb/nimba/)), [Curcumin](/herb/curcumin/) (from [Haridra](/herb/haridra/)), [Karanjin](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#karanjin) (from Naktamala), [Jasmone](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#jasmone) (from [Jati](/herb/jati/))

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Not applicable, topical application only. Clean wound with [Triphala](/herb/triphala/) Kwath before application.
