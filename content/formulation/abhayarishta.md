## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Bhaishajya Ratnavali, Arsha Rogadhikara (Piles treatment chapter). Also referenced in Charaka Samhita for Vibandha (constipation) and Udara Roga (abdominal disorders). Known as the 'king of medicine' for piles.

## How does it work?

- Cholagogue action increases bile secretion by the liver, stimulating intestinal peristalsis for smooth fecal passage
- Venotonic activity has been reported in studies on [Haritaki](/herb/haritaki/)
- [Haritaki](/herb/haritaki/) is described as having a mild [laxative](/reference/laxative/) action
- [Vidanga](/herb/vidanga/) and [Haritaki](/herb/haritaki/) are described as having anti-inflammatory properties

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Constipation (Vibandha): traditionally used for constipation. A double-blind clinical study reported significant improvement.
- Piles/Hemorrhoids (Arsha): traditionally used for piles. Studies on [Haritaki](/herb/haritaki/) have reported venotonic and anti-inflammatory activity.
- Abdominal distension and flatulence: traditionally used for abdominal distension and flatulence. A cholagogue action that increases bile flow has been reported.
- Dysuria (difficult urination): traditionally used for difficult urination. [Gokshura](/herb/gokshura/) is described as having [diuretic](/reference/diuretic/) properties.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe at recommended dose. Contains 5-10% self-generated alcohol. Excessive use may cause loose stools or mild abdominal cramping.
- Not recommended during pregnancy due to mild purgative action. Patients with inflammatory bowel disease should use under medical supervision. Avoid in bleeding hemorrhoids without physician guidance.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Arishta (fermented liquid), Capsules (modern), Syrup (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** 12 to 24 ml twice daily after meals with equal quantity of water, as given in the Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, entry 1:1. The formulary attributes this formula to Bhaisajyaratnavali, Arsorogadhikara; 105-107.

**Optimal Timing:** After meals (Bhojana-uttara), twice daily, preferably after lunch and dinner

**Storage:** Store in a cool, dry place in well-closed amber glass or HDPE containers. Protect from direct sunlight. Temperature not exceeding 30°C.

**Marker Compounds:** Gallic acid, Chebulagic acid, Chebulinic acid, Ellagic acid, Tannic acid

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Equal quantity of lukewarm water
