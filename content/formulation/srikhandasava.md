## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Bhaishajya Ratnavali, Madatyaya Adhikara (chapter on intoxication/alcoholism). Described for controlling Pitta-provoked conditions and adverse effects of intoxication.

## How does it work?

- Sheeta Virya (cooling potency) of [Chandana](/herb/chandana/) and [Ushira](/herb/ushira/) pacifies aggravated Pitta dosha and reduces burning sensations
- In vitro assays have reported [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) activity (santalols, [flavonoids](/reference/flavonoids/), phenolics)
- [Antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) activity against S. aureus and E. coli has been reported in vitro
- [Jatamansi](/herb/jatamansi/) and [Ushira](/herb/ushira/) are traditionally described as calming and are used classically for agitation from Pitta excess

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Burning sensation (Daha) from Pitta aggravation: traditionally used for this. Studies have reported cooling and anti-inflammatory activity.
- Adverse effects of alcohol/intoxication: traditionally used for Madatyaya. Studies have reported [hepatoprotective](/reference/hepatoprotective/) and antioxidant activity.
- Burning micturition and UTI: traditionally used for these conditions. Studies have reported antimicrobial activity and cooling [diuretic](/reference/diuretic/) properties.
- Skin conditions with burning and inflammation: traditionally used for these conditions. Studies have reported antioxidant and antimicrobial activity.

*Only studies that could be traced to a published paper are listed here. Classical formulations are researched largely in journals that PubMed does not index, so an absence here reflects the reach of the index rather than the state of the evidence.*

## Recent safety updates

- Contains 5-10% self-generated alcohol. Over-dosage may lead to gastritis and related complaints. Recommended dose: 12-24 ml once or twice daily after food.
- Generally safe at therapeutic doses. Not recommended during pregnancy. Patients with gastric sensitivity should take with food. Long-term use under physician supervision advised.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Arishta/Asava (fermented liquid), Syrup (modern adaptation)

**Standard Dosage:** 12 to 24 ml with equal water, as given in the Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, entry 1:35. The formulary attributes this formula to Bhaisajyaratnavali, Madatyayadhikara; 24-26 (Devanagari line: भैषज्यरत्नावली, मदात्ययाधिकारः 24-26).

**Optimal Timing:** After meals, twice daily

**Storage:** Room temperature, tightly sealed, away from direct sunlight. Do not refrigerate.

**Marker Compounds:** Alpha-santalol, Beta-santalol

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Equal quantity of water after meals

### Synergistic Combinations

- Key herbs: [Chandana](/herb/chandana/) (sandalwood), [Dhataki](/herb/dhataki/), [Guda](/glossary/concepts-a-m/#guda); fermentation creates unique metabolite profile with enhanced therapeutic activity
