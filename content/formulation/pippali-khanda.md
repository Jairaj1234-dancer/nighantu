## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Sushruta Samhita (respiratory therapy context); Chakradatta by Chakrapani (14th century CE); Bhaishajya Ratnavali by Govind Das (17th century CE) - Rasayana Prakarana 73/18

## How does it work?

- [Piperine](/reference/piperine/) alkaloid provides potent bioavailability enhancement by inhibiting hepatic and intestinal glucuronidation [enzymes](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#enzymes)
- Volatile oils (pinene, [limonene](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#limonene)) have been reported in experimental studies to have mucolytic and bronchodilatory actions
- Piperine has been reported to have anti-inflammatory action
- Resins and [tannins](/reference/tannins/) provide mild astringent and anti-inflammatory properties
- Classically described as Deepana-Pachana (digestive stimulation), used for Mandagni

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Kasa-Shwasa (cough and asthma): traditionally used for cough and asthma. A study on tracheal tissue reported reduced spasm.
- Rasayana (rejuvenation): used in classical practice as a Vardhamana [Pippali](/herb/pippali/) protocol for promoting strength and respiratory resilience and for slowing age-related decline.
- Agnimandya (digestive weakness): traditionally used for digestive weakness. Pharmacological studies have reported that piperine enhanced digestive enzyme activity.
- Pranavaha Srotas strengthening (respiratory channel health): traditionally used to strengthen the respiratory channels. Experimental studies have reported mucolytic and bronchodilatory actions of the volatile oil.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe in moderation; excessive use may irritate stomach and digestive lining causing acidity
- Pregnant women should use cautiously
- High-quality human RCTs on pure Pippali preparations remain scarce; long-term safety and dosage standardization need further investigation

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Khanda (granular sweet preparation)

**Standard Dosage:** 5-10g twice daily

**Optimal Timing:** After meals

**Storage:** Airtight container

**Marker Compounds:** Piperine, [Piperlongumine](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#piperlongumine)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Warm milk
