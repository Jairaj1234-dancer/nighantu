## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Sahasrayogam, Kashaya Yoga Prakarana; also referenced in Ashtanga Hridayam for treating indigestion, intestinal spasm, abdominal pain, hernia, and menstrual disorders.

## How does it work?

- Traditionally described as having [antispasmodic](/glossary/pharmacology/#antispasmodic) action on uterine and intestinal smooth muscle; used for menstrual cramps and abdominal pain
- [Antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) activity (DPPH, FRAP, H2O2 scavenging) has been reported in lab assays
- [Vata-balancing](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#vata-balancing) properties through [Dashamoola](/herb/dashamoola/) and [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/) are traditionally said to support normal peristalsis and are used in constipation
- Phytoestrogenic compounds from [Shatavari](/herb/shatavari/) and [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/) support hormonal balance and reproductive health

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Dysmenorrhea and menstrual irregularity: traditionally used for these conditions. Clinical observations have reported antispasmodic and hormonal effects.
- Constipation and digestive disorders: traditionally used for these complaints. Prokinetic action has been reported for [Dashamoola](/herb/dashamoola/) and [Eranda](/herb/eranda/) root constituents.
- Polycystic ovarian disease and uterine fibroids: traditionally used for these conditions. Clinical case studies have reported a reduction in cyst size.
- Infertility support: traditionally used to support fertility. Phytoestrogenic activity has been reported for key ingredients.

## Recent safety updates

- No known side effects at recommended dosage; overdose may induce gastric irritation. Should be used under Ayurvedic physician supervision.
- In cases of shorter menstrual periods with heavy bleeding, [Sukumara Kashayam](/formulation/sukumara-kashayam/) may increase bleeding; contraindicated in menorrhagia without physician guidance.
- Individual sensitivities or allergies to specific herbal ingredients may exist, especially in Pitta-predominant conditions.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Kashayam (decoction), Kashayam tablet, Concentrated liquid

**Standard Dosage:** 15-30ml twice daily

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals, typically morning and evening

**Storage:** Fresh: refrigerate, use within 24h. Tablet/liquid: cool dry place, airtight

**Marker Compounds:** Ricinoleic acid, Shatavarin

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Warm, usually taken on empty stomach
