## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Sharangdhara Samhita (Madhyama Khanda); prominently featured in Bhaishajya Ratnavali

## How does it work?

- Ama Pachana (toxin digestion): Katu (pungent) and Tikta (bitter) Rasa with light, sharp, dry qualities and warm potency collectively digest metabolic toxins (Ama) and prevent further formation by correcting Mandagni
- Diaphoretic [antipyretic](/reference/antipyretic/): Classically described as diaphoretic and traditionally used for fever (Jwara); this mechanism has not been established for the formulation
- [Antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) defense: [Vidanga](/herb/vidanga/) ([embelin](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#embelin)), [Vacha](/herb/vacha/) (beta-asarone), and [Guduchi](/herb/guduchi/) demonstrate activity against Salmonella typhi, H. pylori, and other enteric pathogens
- Immunomodulation: [Guduchi](/herb/guduchi/) (Tinospora cordifolia) is traditionally used in chronic fevers; studies on Guduchi have reported increased phagocytic activity, which has not been shown for the formulation
- Toxin neutralization (Vishaghna): Shuddha [Vatsanabha](/herb/vatsanabha/) (processed aconite) is classically described as Vishaghna (antitoxin), while processed Bhallataka is traditionally used as antirheumatic

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Fever (Jwara Chikitsa): traditionally used for fever. Studies have reported diaphoretic and antipyretic activity.
- Toxin elimination (Ama Pachana/Vishaghna): traditionally used to clear toxins. Studies have reported antimicrobial activity against Salmonella typhi and H. pylori.
- Gastro-enteritis (Visuchika): traditionally used for gastro-enteritis. Studies have reported antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory and digestive actions.
- Immunomodulation: used in classical practice to modulate immunity. Studies on [Guduchi](/herb/guduchi/) have reported increased phagocytic activity and adaptive immune response.

## Recent safety updates

- Contains Shuddha [Vatsanabha](/herb/vatsanabha/) (purified aconite) and Shuddha Bhallataka (purified marking nut) which are toxic in unpurified form - MUST be prepared strictly according to classical Shodhana protocols; use only under qualified Ayurvedic physician supervision
- Contraindicated in pregnancy, lactation, and young children; avoid in Pitta-predominant conditions; therapeutic dose must be strictly adhered to; overdose can be dangerous; not for self-medication or OTC purchase; short-term use during acute conditions recommended

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Vati (pill/tablet), Capsule

**Standard Dosage:** 1-2 tablets twice daily

**Optimal Timing:** Before or after meals as indicated

**Storage:** Airtight container, cool dry place, protected from moisture

**Marker Compounds:** Embelin, [Tinosporin](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#tinosporin), [Piperine](/reference/piperine/)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Warm water or as specified

### Synergistic Combinations

- Key herbs: [Vidanga](/herb/vidanga/), [Guduchi](/herb/guduchi/), Shunthi, [Pippali](/herb/pippali/), [Vacha](/herb/vacha/)
