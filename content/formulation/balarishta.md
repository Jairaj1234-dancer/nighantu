## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Bhaishajya Ratnavali (13th century CE), where it was called Baladwaya Arishta or nourishing fermented tonic. Also referenced in Charaka Samhita for Balya (strength-promoting) indications.

## How does it work?

- [Bala](/herb/bala/) (Sida cordifolia) is regarded in classical practice as a nervine tonic
- [Adaptogenic](/reference/adaptogenic/) and [immunomodulatory](/reference/immunomodulatory/) effects of [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/) enhance stress resistance and immune function
- Anti-inflammatory and [analgesic](/reference/analgesic/) properties are traditionally attributed to it in joint and muscle pain
- Nutritive and restorative (Balya) action improves overall body strength and organ nourishment

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- General debility and weakness post-illness: traditionally used for general debility and weakness after illness.
- Rheumatic pain and joint disorders: traditionally used for rheumatic pain and joint disorders. Studies in arthritis models have reported anti-inflammatory activity.
- Post-paralysis rehabilitation: traditionally used during recovery after paralysis. In classical practice, [Bala](/herb/bala/) is regarded as a nervine tonic.
- Hemiplegia support: traditionally used as support in hemiplegia. Studies have reported [neuroprotective](/reference/neuroprotective/) activity and effects on neurotransmitters.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe without known serious side effects at recommended dose (12-24 ml twice daily with equal water). Some individuals with gastric sensitivity may experience stomach irritation or burning.
- Contains 5-10% self-generated alcohol. Caution in liver disorders, pregnancy, and pediatric use. Sida cordifolia contains [ephedrine](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#ephedrine) [alkaloids](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) in trace amounts; monitor in hypertensive patients.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Arishta (fermented decoction), Syrup (modern), Capsules (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** 12 to 24 ml twice daily after meals with equal water, as given in the Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, entry 1:24. The formulary attributes this formula to Bhaisajyaratnavali, Vatavyadhyadhikara; 569-571.

**Optimal Timing:** After meals, twice daily, for sustained use (minimum 1-3 months for [Rasayana](/reference/rasayana/) effect)

**Storage:** Store in well-closed amber glass or HDPE containers in a cool, dry place. Protect from sunlight. Temperature not exceeding 30°C.

**Marker Compounds:** Ephedrine, [Pseudoephedrine](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#pseudoephedrine), Beta-[sitosterol](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#sitosterol), [Ecdysterone](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#ecdysterone), Withanolide A (from [Ashwagandha](/herb/ashwagandha/) component)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Equal quantity of warm water
