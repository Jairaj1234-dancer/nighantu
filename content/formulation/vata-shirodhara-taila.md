## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Shirodhara is a classical murdha-taila procedure described in **Ashtanga Hridaya** and **Sushruta Samhita**, and is recognised in Ayurvedic texts as a primary external intervention for Vata-vaishamya of the shiras (head region): restlessness of the mind, disturbed sleep, sensory fatigue and dryness (rukshata). This formulation follows the classical Vata-shamaka taila principle — snigdha (unctuous), ushna (warming) and medhya (mind-supportive) dravyas compounded in the Tila-taila base described by Charaka and Sushruta as the *taila-uttama* for Vata-shamana.

## How does it work?

- Shirodhara induces a hypometabolic, trophotropic state with measurable decreases in sympathetic activation and subjective stress scores in healthy volunteers.
- External oleation with sesame-based warm oil supports parasympathetic tone and promotes a state described as *ananda-turiya* (blissful pre-sleep) by research groups investigating Ayurvedic oil-dripping therapy.
- Aromatic volatile fractions (linalool, eugenol, lavandula esters) contribute olfactory input during the procedure that is associated with reduced anxiety markers in independent aromatherapy trials.
- Ashwagandha withanolides and Bacopa/Valeriana constituents are associated with modulation of the HPA axis and GABAergic tone in oral preparations; their topical role in Shirodhara is traditionally framed as scalp absorption plus olfactory-limbic input.

## Traditional Uses (External Shirodhara Context)

- Traditionally used in Shirodhara to help soothe a restless, over-active Vata mind.
- Supports a feeling of deep relaxation, groundedness and settled prana when applied externally as shiro-abhyanga or shiro-dhara.
- Traditionally used in pada-abhyanga (foot anointing) as part of the Vata-pacifying evening routine described in Ashtanga Hridaya.

## Directions for Use (External Only)

- **Shirodhara:** warm to ~38–40 °C. Pour in a continuous unbroken stream from a dhara-patra over the ajna marma (forehead) for 30–45 minutes per session.
- **Shiro-abhyanga:** warm a small quantity and massage into scalp and temples in the evening; leave 30–60 min and cleanse.
- **Pada-abhyanga:** apply a small quantity to the soles of the feet before sleep, per the classical Vata-pacifying routine (Ashtanga Hridaya).

## Safety, contraindications and cautions

- Not to be ingested.
- Discontinue if irritation occurs. Avoid eyes and broken skin. Patch-test before first use.
- Not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.
- Store in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight.
