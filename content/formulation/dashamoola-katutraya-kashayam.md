## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Ashtanga Hridaya ([Dashamoola](/herb/dashamoola/) Kwatham section). Refined in the Travancore region and documented in Kerala Bhaishajya Ratnavali as Dashamoolakatutrayam Kashayam for stubborn coughs, chronic arthritis, and post-natal weakness.

## How does it work?

- COX-2 and 5-LOX inhibition by [Dashamoola](/herb/dashamoola/) roots provides broad-spectrum anti-inflammatory action
- Bioavailability enhancement by [Trikatu](/herb/trikatu/) (especially [Pippali](/herb/pippali/) and Maricha) is described as increasing systemic absorption of [glycosides](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycosides)
- [Vasa](/herb/vasa/) (Adhatoda vasica) and [Trikatu](/herb/trikatu/) components are traditionally used to support respiratory function
- The Katutraya (three pungent herbs) is described classically as warming and is traditionally used for chronic Vata-Kapha disorders

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Chronic arthritis and inflammation: traditionally used for chronic arthritis and inflammation. A randomized controlled clinical study reported reductions in CRP and ESR.
- Respiratory disorders (asthma, chronic cough): traditionally used for asthma and chronic cough. A clinical study reported improvement in a lung function measure.
- Post-natal weakness and recovery: traditionally used for post-natal weakness and recovery.
- Vata-Kapha imbalances with joint and respiratory involvement: used in classical practice for Vata-Kapha imbalances affecting the joints and respiratory system.

## Recent safety updates

- The pungent herbs ([Trikatu](/herb/trikatu/)) may aggravate Pitta dosha; use cautiously in hyperacidity and peptic ulcer.
- May potentiate anti-inflammatory and anticoagulant drugs. Monitor closely in patients on concurrent NSAID or blood-thinning therapy.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Kashayam (decoction), Kashayam tablet (modern), Concentrated liquid (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** 15-30 mL or 2 tablets twice daily before meals. The Ayurvedic Formulary of India, Part I, entry 4:9 specifies 48 g of the kvatha curna to prepare the decoction, which is a quantity of drug rather than a dose taken, so it is not comparable with the volume above. The formulary attributes this formula to Sahasrayoga, Kasayaprakarana; 107.

**Optimal Timing:** Before meals, morning and evening

**Storage:** Fresh: same day use. Concentrates: amber glass at room temperature. Tablets: airtight containers at room temperature.

**Marker Compounds:** Piperine, 6-[Gingerol](/glossary/compounds-d-g/#gingerol), [Lupeol](/reference/lupeol/) (from Dashamoola), [Marmelosin](/glossary/compounds-l-o/#marmelosin) (from Bilwa root), [Solasodine](/glossary/compounds-q-t/#solasodine) (from [Brihati](/herb/brihati/)/[Kantakari](/herb/kantakari/))

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Warm water (for tablets); taken as is (liquid)
