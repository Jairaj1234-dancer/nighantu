## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Sahasrayogam (Taila Prakarana). Available as both [Triphaladi Thailam](/formulation/triphaladi-thailam/) ([sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila) base) and Triphaladi Kera Thailam ([coconut oil](/glossary/concepts-a-m/#coconut-oil) base). Referenced for head-related disorders (Shiro Roga) in Kerala Ayurvedic tradition.

## How does it work?

- Gallic acid and chebulagic acid from [Triphala](/herb/triphala/) have reported [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/) activity
- [Guduchi](/herb/guduchi/)'s (Tinospora) [berberine](/glossary/compounds-a-c/#berberine) and tinosporine are reported to have anti-inflammatory and [immunomodulatory](/reference/immunomodulatory/) activity
- [Pterostilbene](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#pterostilbene) from Asanaka (Pterocarpus marsupium) has been reported in pharmacological studies to show retinal neuroprotective activity, relevant to eye disorders (Netra Roga)
- Volatile compounds from [Ketaki](/herb/ketaki/) (Pandanus) provide cooling and calming effects on the scalp and head region through transdermal absorption during Shiro Abhyanga (head massage)

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Chronic headache and migraine (Shirahshula): traditionally used for chronic headache, given as Nasya. Clinical observations have reported patient improvement with Nasya administration.
- Eye disorders and visual fatigue (Netra Roga): traditionally used for eye disorders and visual fatigue. Pharmacological studies have reported retinal neuroprotective activity for [pterostilbene](/glossary/compounds-o-q/#pterostilbene) from Asanaka, and antioxidant action of [Triphala](/herb/triphala/) on optic tissue.
- Premature hair greying and hair fall: traditionally used for premature greying and hair fall. [Triphala](/herb/triphala/) has been reported to have antioxidant activity. [Amla](/herb/amla/) is traditionally used to strengthen hair by nourishing the follicles.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally safe for external application on scalp and head. Avoid direct contact with eyes. For Nasya use, administer under practitioner guidance. Mild temporary heaviness in the head may occur after application - this typically resolves within 30 minutes.
- Not well-studied in large-scale clinical trials. Citrullus colocynthis (Indravalli) is a potent herb - should not be used internally without practitioner supervision. Avoid during acute fever or headache with vomiting. Patch test recommended for scalp application in sensitive individuals.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Thailam (medicated [sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila)), External application

**Standard Dosage:** External application (Shiro Abhyanga) or Nasya, as directed by a practitioner

**Bioavailability:** Oil base ([sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila)) enhances dermal absorption of lipophilic active compounds

**Optimal Timing:** External: before bath or at bedtime

**Shelf Life:** 3 years (medicated oils per ASU guidelines)

**Storage:** Airtight amber glass/HDPE container, room temperature, protected from light

**Marker Compounds:** Gallic acid, [Glycyrrhizin](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycyrrhizinglycyrrhizin)

**Quality Parameters:** Acid value <5, saponification value per base oil specs, refractive index, moisture NMT 0.5%, specific gravity, marker: Gallic acid, [Glycyrrhizin](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#glycyrrhizinglycyrrhizin)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** External: massage into affected area

### Synergistic Combinations

- Key herbs: [Triphala](/herb/triphala/), [Yashtimadhu](/herb/yashtimadhu/) for eye health; oil base acts as lipid carrier for enhanced tissue penetration
