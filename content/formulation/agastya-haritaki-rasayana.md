## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Charaka Samhita, Chikitsa Sthana 17th chapter (Kasa Chikitsa: 57-62); Ashtanga Hridayam; attributed to sage Agastya; Rasatarangini (17th century CE)

## How does it work?

- [Haritaki](/herb/haritaki/) (chebulinic acid, gallic acid) provides [antioxidant](/reference/antioxidant/), anti-inflammatory, and bronchodilatory effects on respiratory mucosa
- Dashamula group is traditionally used for its anti-inflammatory action in chronic respiratory conditions
- [Bharangi](/herb/bharangi/) (clerodendrin) is traditionally used as a [bronchodilator](/reference/bronchodilator/) to ease bronchospasm
- Pushkaramoola ([inulin](/glossary/compounds-g-l/#inulin), [alantolactone](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alantolactone)) is traditionally used for asthma; mast cell stabilization has been proposed as a mechanism
- Approximately 100 synergistic components work collectively for comprehensive respiratory support and Rasayana (rejuvenation) effects

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Tamaka Shwasa (bronchial asthma): traditionally used for chronic asthma. A review has reported bronchodilatory and anti-inflammatory activity.
- Kasa (chronic cough): used in classical practice for chronic cough, as a primary indication from Charaka Samhita Kasa Chikitsa.
- Pranvaha Srotas health (respiratory channels): traditionally used for the respiratory channels. A clinical study reported improvement in cardiorespiratory parameters.
- Pratishyaya (allergic rhinitis/sinusitis): traditionally used as a respiratory indication for allergic rhinitis and sinusitis.

## Recent safety updates

- Some formulations may contain trace heavy metals from botanical sources - appropriate quality control and testing recommended
- Generally well-tolerated; excessive doses may cause mild loose stools due to Haritaki's [laxative](/reference/laxative/) properties

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Avaleha/Lehya (semi-solid confection), Capsules (modern), Granules (modern)

**Standard Dosage:** 6-12 g twice daily, or as directed. The Ayurvedic Formulary of India attributes this formula to Astangahrdaya, Cikitsasthana, Adhyaya 3; 125-128.

**Optimal Timing:** Early morning on empty stomach and/or before bedtime

**Storage:** Store in a cool, dry place in wide-mouth glass or food-grade HDPE jars with airtight lids. Protect from moisture and sunlight. Temperature not exceeding 25°C.

**Marker Compounds:** Piperine, Gallic acid, Ellagic acid, Chebulagic acid

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Warm milk or warm water
