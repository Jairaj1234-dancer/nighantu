## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Ashtanga Hridayam (Sutrasthana); also referenced in Sharangadhara Samhita. The name 'Shadbindu' means 'six drops', indicating the classical dose of 6 drops per nostril. Used for Nasya therapy in ENT conditions.

## How does it work?

- [Kaempferol](/reference/kaempferol/) (confirmed by HPTLC) is described as having anti-inflammatory and [antimicrobial](/reference/antimicrobial/) activity; its effect in this oil on nasal and sinus mucosa has not been studied
- Valeric acid derivatives from [Tagara](/herb/tagara/) (Valeriana) are proposed to have sedative and muscle-relaxant effects
- Shogaols from Shunthi ([ginger](/herb/ginger/)) are proposed to have mucolytic and vasodilatory effects
- Rock salt ([Saindhava Lavana](/herb/saindhava-lavana/)) is proposed to have osmotic action and mild antimicrobial properties

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

*These summaries come from the source notes, which do not name the studies behind them.*

- Chronic sinusitis and nasal polyps: traditionally used as Nasya for chronic sinusitis and nasal polyps. A clinical study reported improvement in sinusitis symptoms after Nasya administration.
- Headache and migraine (Shirahshula): traditionally used for headache and migraine. The formulation contains kaempferol and [Tagara](/herb/tagara/).
- Hair loss and premature greying: traditionally used for hair loss and premature greying through scalp and nasal administration. The base is [Bhringraj](/herb/bhringraj/) juice, which contains [wedelolactone](/glossary/compounds-t-z/#wedelolactone) compounds.

## What do recent studies show?

- Shailajan S, Menon SN, Tiwari BR and others 2013. [Standardization of Shadbindu Taila: An Ayurvedic oil based medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24049414/). *Ayu*. PMID [24049414](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24049414/) · [doi:10.4103/0974-8520.115442](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-8520.115442)  
  Published in PMC, the study performed physicochemical, phytochemical, and HPTLC analysis. Five major spots were observed in the HPTLC fingerprint, with kaempferol detected and identity confirmed. Oil was found free of heavy metals and non-irritant on rabbit skin.

*Only studies that could be traced to a published paper are listed here. Classical formulations are researched largely in journals that PubMed does not index, so an absence here reflects the reach of the index rather than the state of the evidence.*

## Recent safety updates

- Found non-irritant on rabbit skin in standardization study. No heavy metals detected in properly manufactured formulations. Mild sneezing or nasal discharge may occur after instillation - this is considered a therapeutic response. Temporary burning or lacrimation possible.
- Not recommended during acute nasal bleeding, fever, or immediately after meals. Avoid in children under 7 years without practitioner supervision. Preliminary safety data are positive but large-scale double-blind studies are still needed to fully characterize pharmacokinetics of herbal [alkaloids](/glossary/compounds-1-a/#alkaloids) in human nasal mucosa.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Thailam (medicated [sesame oil](/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila)), External application

**Standard Dosage:** Nasya: 2-6 drops per nostril

**Storage:** Airtight amber glass/HDPE container, room temperature, protected from light

### Synergistic Combinations

- Key herbs: Bhringaraj, [Eranda](/herb/eranda/), Shunthi, [Tagara](/herb/tagara/) for nasya; oil base acts as lipid carrier for enhanced tissue penetration
