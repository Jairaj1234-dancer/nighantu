## Key Ingredients

- Tandulambu (Rice water / Tandulodaka)
- [Coconut oil](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-a-m/#coconut-oil) ([Narikela](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/narikela/) taila) base

## Where is it described in the classical texts?

A signature Kerala Ayurvedic formulation (Chikitsamanjari / Kerala Vaidya tradition). The name 'Murivenna' literally means 'wound oil' in Malayalam. Referenced in Ayurveda Formulary of India (modified form). Unique in using [coconut oil](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-a-m/#coconut-oil) as base instead of [sesame oil](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila).

## How does it work?

- [Karanjin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-g-l/#karanjin) and [pongamol](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-o-q/#pongamol) from [Karanja](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/karanja/) leaves provide potent anti-inflammatory and [antimicrobial](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antimicrobial/) activity, inhibiting bacterial colonization at wound sites
- [Acemannan](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-1-a/#acemannan) [polysaccharides](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-o-q/#polysaccharides) from [Aloe vera](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/aloe-vera/) stimulate macrophage activation and fibroblast proliferation, accelerating wound closure and granulation tissue formation
- [Moringa](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/moringa/) leaf [isothiocyanates](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-g-l/#isothiocyanates) provide broad-spectrum antibacterial activity and reduce oxidative stress at wound sites through potent [antioxidant](/ayurveda-atlas/reference/antioxidant/) action
- [Coconut oil](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-a-m/#coconut-oil) base provides medium-chain fatty acids (lauric acid) with inherent antimicrobial properties, and superior wound moisture retention compared to [sesame oil](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila)

## Which traditional uses are supported by research?

- Wound healing and minor trauma management - validated by in-vivo study (2021) showing enhanced collagen deposition and by Kerala Journal study (2018) showing 75% faster closure than control
- Fracture support and soft tissue trauma (sprain, dislocation) - supported by RCT (n=40) showing 60% pain decrease within 3 days of Murivenna massage for ankle sprains
- Episiotomy wound care - validated by clinical study (2021) demonstrating superior pain-relieving and tenderness-diminishing properties in postpartum wound management

## What do recent clinical trials show?

- **Wound healing potential of Murivenna in excision wound model** (2021): In-vivo study at Kerala University showed Murivenna Thailam enhanced collagen deposition in rat incision models, confirming its wound healing properties.
- **Antimicrobial efficacy of Murivenna against common wound pathogens** (2019): Study from Amrita Vishwa Vidyapeetham demonstrated broad-spectrum antimicrobial effect against Staphylococcus aureus and E. coli, supporting its use as a wound-protective oil.
- **Clinical evaluation of Murivenna application on Episiotomy Wound** (2021): Published in Journal of Ayurveda and Integrated Medical Sciences, Murivenna showed better pain-relieving and tenderness-diminishing properties compared to [Jatyadi Taila](/ayurveda-atlas/formulation/jatyadi-taila/) in episiotomy wound management in 30 patients.

## Recent safety updates

- Generally well-tolerated for external wound application. Mild stinging may occur on fresh wounds - this is transient. No significant adverse effects documented in clinical studies or traditional practice. Kerala Journal of Ayurveda (2018) found 75% faster wound closure compared to plain [sesame oil](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-m-y/#tila-taila).
- Not for internal consumption. Avoid on deep infected wounds requiring surgical management. Those with [coconut oil](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-a-m/#coconut-oil) allergy should avoid this formulation. Not to be used as sole treatment for fractures - complementary to proper orthopedic management.

## Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Thailam (medicated [coconut oil](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-a-m/#coconut-oil)), External application

**Standard Dosage:** External application on wounds and fractures

**Bioavailability:** Oil base ([coconut oil](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/concepts-a-m/#coconut-oil)) enhances dermal absorption of lipophilic active compounds; for internal thailams, oil base enhances absorption of fat-soluble actives

**Optimal Timing:** External: before bath or at bedtime. Internal: before meals

**Shelf Life:** 3 years (medicated oils per ASU guidelines)

**Storage:** Airtight amber glass/HDPE container, room temperature, protected from light

**Marker Compounds:** Karanjin, [Berberine](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-a-c/#berberine)

**Quality Parameters:** Acid value <5, saponification value per base oil specs, refractive index, moisture NMT 0.5%, specific gravity, marker: Karanjin, [Berberine](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-a-c/#berberine)

**Vehicle (Anupana):** External: massage into affected area. Internal: with warm water/milk

### Synergistic Combinations

- Key herbs: Murivenna herbs: [Karanja](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/karanja/), Sigru, [Daruharidra](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/daruharidra/); oil base acts as lipid carrier for enhanced tissue penetration
