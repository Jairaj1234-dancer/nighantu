An enclosed chamber or box in which the patient sits while medicated steam is directed onto the body (with the head kept outside). Includes Nadi Swedana (tubular/localized steam), Bashpa Swedana (full body steam box), and Prastara Swedana (bed-based steaming).

## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Charaka Samhita Sutrasthana Chapter 14; Sushruta Samhita Chikitsasthana Chapter 32. Thirteen types of Swedana are described including Sagni Sweda (with fire) and Niragni Sweda (without fire).

## How is it used traditionally?

Pre-Panchakarma preparation (Purvakarma), muscle relaxation, joint stiffness, pain relief, opening of Srotas (body channels), liquefying Ama (toxins) for elimination.

## Traditional materials

- Wood (Daru) for the chamber
- Copper or earthen vessel for boiling herbs
- Cotton cloth for draping

## Modern variants

- Electric steam generators (portable and fixed)
- Fiber-body steam chambers
- Nadi Swedan (localized steam) with electric handheld units
- Infrared-assisted Swedana units

## Regulatory status

- **India Cdsco:** Steam generators may be classified under Class A medical devices if therapeutic claims are made.
- **Us Fda:** Steam baths and saunas are generally not FDA-regulated as medical devices unless specific therapeutic claims are made.
- **Eu Mdr:** Generally classified as wellness equipment; may require Class I classification if therapeutic claims are made.
