Muslin cloth bags filled with specific therapeutic ingredients, tied into bundles (Pottali), heated in medicated oil or decoction, and applied to the body through rhythmic pressing and massage. Multiple varieties exist based on filling material.

## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Charaka Samhita Sutrasthana Chapter 14 (Pinda Sweda under Swedana Vidhi); Shashtika Shali Pinda Sweda described extensively in Kerala Ayurvedic texts (Sahasrayogam, Chikitsa Manjari).

## How is it used traditionally?

Pain management, joint disorders, neurological conditions, rejuvenation, muscle nourishment, and general Swedana (sudation) therapy.

## Traditional materials

- Muslin cloth (cotton)
- Jute string for tying
- Various herbal fillings as per type

## Modern variants

- Pre-packaged disposable Kizhi bags
- Electric warming Kizhi stations
- Reusable cotton poultice covers

## Regulatory status

- **India Cdsco:** Not classified as medical devices. The herbal contents may be regulated as Ayurvedic medicines under AYUSH Drug Licensing.
- **Us Fda:** Not specifically regulated. Would be considered general wellness or therapy equipment.
- **Eu Mdr:** Not classified as medical devices.
