In traditional Ayurvedic practice, Nadi Pariksha (pulse examination) is performed using the physician's three fingers (index, middle, ring) placed on the radial artery at the wrist. There is no traditional 'device' per se - the physician's trained fingers are the instrument. However, some accessories include wrist cushions and timing devices.

## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Sharangdhara Samhita Purva Khanda Chapter 3; Bhavaprakasha Purva Khanda Chapter 7; Yogaratnakara (Nadi Pariksha section). Nadi Pariksha became a formalized diagnostic method in medieval Ayurvedic texts, with the concept traceable to Charaka and Sushruta's clinical examination methods.

## How is it used traditionally?

Assessment of Tridosha (Vata-Pitta-Kapha) balance and imbalance through pulse quality (Gati), speed, rhythm, volume, and character. Diagnosis of Prakriti (constitution), Vikriti (current imbalance), organ health, and prognosis.

## Traditional materials

- Physician's own fingers (primary instrument)
- Wrist support/cushion
- Oil for lubricating the pulse point

## Modern variants

- Nadi Tarangini (AI-based pulse diagnostic device)
- Pulse waveform sensors
- Piezoelectric pulse sensors

## Regulatory status

- **India Cdsco:** Traditional method requires no device regulation. Modern pulse diagnostic devices (see Nadi Tarangini below) are subject to CDSCO classification.
- **Us Fda:** Traditional method not applicable. Modern sensor-based devices would require FDA classification.
- **Eu Mdr:** Traditional method not applicable.
