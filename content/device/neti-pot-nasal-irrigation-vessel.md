A small spouted vessel resembling a teapot, used to pour warm saline water through one nostril and out the other, cleansing the nasal passages. Part of Shatkarma (six purification actions) in Hatha Yoga and Dinacharya (daily routine) in Ayurveda.

## Where is it described in the classical texts?

Hatha Yoga Pradipika (Chapter 2, Shatkarma section); Gheranda Samhita; Charaka Samhita references nasal cleansing as part of Dinacharya. First introduced to Western medicine via The Lancet in 1902.

## How is it used traditionally?

Nasal congestion, sinusitis, allergies, post-nasal drip, improving breathing and Pranayama practice. Removes pollen, dust, germs, and excess mucus from nasal passages.

## Traditional materials

- Copper (Tamra) - preferred for antimicrobial properties
- Earthenware/Ceramic (Mrittika)
- Brass (Pittala)

## Modern variants

- Stainless steel neti pots
- Plastic (BPA-free) neti pots
- Squeeze bottle nasal irrigators
- Battery-operated pulsed water nasal irrigation devices

## Regulatory status

- **India Cdsco:** Not specifically classified under CDSCO MDR 2017 as a medical device.
- **Us Fda:** FDA considers nasal irrigation devices (including neti pots) as medical devices. The FDA has issued safety guidance: must use distilled, sterile, or previously boiled water - tap water is not safe. FDA has issued multiple consumer safety warnings.
- **Eu Mdr:** May be classified as Class I medical device if marketed with therapeutic claims under EU MDR 2017/745.
