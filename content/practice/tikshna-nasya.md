## What it is

Nasya is medication given through the nose. Tīkṣṇa nasya is the sharp or strong-acting end of that range: the record glosses it as strong-action nasal medication, files it under pañcakarma with the subcategory *recanaka*, and marks it vaidya-only. The record's own way of positioning it is by contrast — stronger than a gentle Aṇu Taila instillation, and more cleansing in intent.

## Where it comes from in the classical literature

The record cites Caraka Saṃhitā Siddhi Sthāna 9, the Trimarmīya Siddhi, and the chapter does carry the material. Its main subject is the three marmas, hṛdaya, śiras and basti, during purificatory procedures, and within its treatment of disorders of the head it sets out nasya, its varieties and its place. The classification given there runs: navana, nasal drops, with snehana and śodhana subtypes; avapīḍa, insufflation of a thin paste, with recana and tarpaṇa subtypes; dhmāpana, powder insufflation; dhūma, medicated smoke; and pratimarśa, oil applied within the nostrils. The record's *recanaka* subcategory corresponds to the recana division, the one directed at eliminating morbid matter from the head. The chapter also refers to śiro-virecana nasya for head conditions marked by stiffness, numbness and heaviness attributed to aggravated kapha.

One qualification. The chapter names the recana and śiro-virecana classes, but the two specific drugs the record attaches to tīkṣṇa nasya were not found in its nasya section, and the chapter does not set out named tīkṣṇa nasya formulations.

## What is used

The record names two materials: **śveta-marica** (white pepper) and **vacā**. It offers **Aṇu Taila** only as the gentle comparator, not as an ingredient. No quantity, number of drops, frequency or duration is given anywhere in the record.

## When nasya is withheld

Caraka Siddhi Sthāna 9 lists circumstances in which nasya is not to be given: during indigestion, in recent coryza, in cloudy weather, immediately after bathing, and after oleation therapies. The reason given in the chapter is that these states themselves conduce to kapha disorders. That the classical text carries a withholding list at all is the point worth taking from it, since a strong-acting preparation is precisely where such a list matters most.

## Why it needs a vaidya

The defining variable in this procedure is potency, and the record supplies no way to calibrate it: no dose, no dilution, no frequency, no endpoint. The nasal mucosa is a direct and unforgiving route, the classical framing places nasya squarely within a chapter about vital areas including the head, and the same chapter conditions its use on the patient's current state. Choosing a tīkṣṇa preparation, and choosing it over a gentle one, is a clinical decision. This is not a procedure to try at home or to approximate with pepper.
