## What the record describes

The source record sets out a two-stage protocol. Before: pañcakarma śodhana, and a residential setup with controlled diet and activity. During: a daily vājīkaraṇa dravya, with Aśvagandhā Pāka and Mūsalī Pāka named as examples, alongside a strict regimen of brahmacarya, avoidance of sun, and mantra-japa. It is filed under the vājīkaraṇa category and marked vaidya-only.

The record gives no course length, no frequency, no season, no indications, no contraindications and no adverse events.

## The citation, checked

Caraka Cikitsā-sthāna 2 is correctly the Vājīkaraṇa Adhyāya, and it does have four pādas as the record's "2.1-4" implies: Samyoga-śaramūlīya, Āsikta-kṣīrika, Māṣaparṇa-bhṛtīya and Pumān-jāta-balādika. The chapter also supports one element of the record: it states that purification should be done before vājīkaraṇa therapy, so that vitiated doṣas and toxins are removed and blocked channels opened.

But the residential framing is not in that chapter. No kuṭī, no dedicated chamber, no live-in mode is described there. Kuṭīpraveśika is a classification from the rasāyana chapter, Cikitsā-sthāna 1.1, where the three-chambered hut is specified in detail. The record has carried a rasāyana structure across to vājīkaraṇa, and that transfer could not be confirmed in Caraka.

The regimen details do not sit in the chapter either. Brahmacarya appears in Cikitsā 2, but as one of the three upastambhas or supporting pillars of life and as a code of conduct in sexual life, not as a residential rule. Sun avoidance and mantra-japa are not in the chapter at all.

So the citation is right for vājīkaraṇa in general, and unconfirmed for a kuṭīpraveśika vājīkaraṇa specifically.

## What is used

The record names Aśvagandhā Pāka and Mūsalī Pāka. Neither appears under those names in Caraka Cikitsā 2. Mūsalī is not named anywhere in that chapter. Aśvagandhā is named once, as an ingredient alongside madhuka in a vājīkaraṇa ghṛta at verse 34. These are the site's own monograph dravyas, so the herbs are real and documented; what is not documented is the pairing of these named pāka preparations with the cited chapter.

## Who the classical chapter addresses

The Caraka Samhita Online edition describes the chapter as concerned with sexual health in healthy persons and with healthy progeny, and as covering conduct related to sex, dietary regime, qualities of semen, factors affecting virility, qualities of the partner, the role of mind, and aphrodisiac and therapeutic formulations.

## Why it is vaidya-only

The record marks it vaidya-only, and the reasons are structural. It is gated behind pañcakarma śodhana, which is supervised clinical work in its own right. It requires residential supervision with controlled diet and activity. And the record itself is thin enough that no reader could evaluate it independently: no duration, no dose, no contraindications, and a classical citation that does not confirm the procedure as named.
