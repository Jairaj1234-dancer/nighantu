## What it is

Akṣitarpaṇa, literally satiation of the eye, is an external procedure (kriyā-bāhya) in which a wall of dough is built around the closed eye and warm medicated ghee is pooled inside it. The record describes the person blinking intermittently for 15 to 20 minutes while the ghee is held in place. The record marks it vaidya-only.

## Where it comes from in the classical literature

Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya, Sūtrasthāna 24, is the Tarpaṇa-Puṭapāka-Vidhi Adhyāya, the chapter on the procedure of tarpaṇa and puṭapāka. It describes round walls resembling a well, of uniform dimensions, built from a paste of barley and black gram around both eye sockets to a height of two aṅgula. Medicated ghee, chosen for the condition and liquefied by indirect heating over hot water, is poured gently into the well over the closed eyelids, up to the level of the eyelashes. The person is then asked to open the eyes gradually and keep blinking so that the eyes are exposed to the medicine. Retention is measured in mātrā-kāla counts and ranges from roughly 100 to 1000 depending on the eye condition, so duration is a clinical judgement, not a fixed number.

The record cited Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya Uttarasthāna 24. That is a different chapter, Śiroroga-pratiṣedha, on disorders of the head. The citation has been corrected to Sūtrasthāna 24.

## What is used

The record names three medicated ghees: Triphalā Ghṛta, Mahā-Triphalā Ghṛta and Drākṣādi Tarpaṇa-Ghṛta. Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya's chapter does not fix a single ghee, specifying instead that the ghee is prepared according to the disease, and it mentions muscle fat as an alternative in certain vāta conditions. The dough ring itself is made from barley and black gram paste in the classical description.

## Why it needs a vaidya

The material sits directly against the eye, the ghee is warmed before use, the choice of ghee is condition-specific in the classical text, and the retention time varies more than tenfold across indications. Each of those is a decision someone has to make on your behalf and be accountable for. The record lists no adverse events, no red flags and no aftercare, which reflects what was recorded rather than an absence of risk.
