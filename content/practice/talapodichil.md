## What it is

Talapodichil is an external treatment, classified in the source record as kriyā-bāhya. A medicated paste is applied to the head as a cap and left in place for thirty to sixty minutes.

The name is descriptive: sources on Kerala practice gloss talapodichil (also written thalapothichil) as literally meaning to cover the head. Secondary accounts treat it as a regional variant of śirolepa, the general Sanskrit term for applying medicine over the scalp and leaving it there for a period.

## Where it comes from in the classical literature

The record cites Sahasrayogam, and that could not be confirmed.

Sahasrayogam is an anonymous Kerala compendium whose name means a thousand formulations, and the sources consulted describe it as a formulary of medicinal preparations rather than a manual of external procedures. None of them locates a description of talapodichil in it, and no chapter or verse could be found.

There is a further point that argues for caution rather than for substituting a different citation. Clinical literature on śirolepa notes that the procedure is not elaborately explained anywhere in the Ayurvedic treatises, and survives mainly as scattered references. That is consistent with talapodichil being a well-established Kerala practice with a thin textual footprint, which is a perfectly respectable thing for a procedure to be, but it means this page should not be read as reporting a classical chapter.

## What it involves

The record gives a single step: medicated paste applied as a head-cap, retained thirty to sixty minutes.

Nothing else is specified. There is no preparatory step, no follow-up step, no named oil, no course length, no frequency and no season in the record. Where a clinic gives you those, they are that clinic's protocol.

## What is used

The record names three dravyas: Bhṛṅgarāja, Yaṣṭimadhu and Brāhmī. It names no oil.

## What to ask before booking

What is actually in the paste being used, and whether it matches the three dravyas named here or a different clinic formulation; how long it will stay on; whether the practitioner treats talapodichil and śirolepa as the same procedure, since the terms are used interchangeably by some clinics; and whether there is any reason in your own history, such as scalp conditions or ingredient sensitivities, to modify or skip it.
