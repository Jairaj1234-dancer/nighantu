## What it is

Talam is an external procedure, classified in the source record as kriyā-bāhya, meaning treatment applied to the outside of the body rather than taken internally. A medicated paste is placed specifically on the crown of the head and left there for a set period.

The procedure belongs to the Kerala treatment tradition rather than to the pan-Indian classical corpus, and it is usually encountered as part of a clinic's head-therapy sequence rather than on its own.

## Where it comes from in the classical literature

The record cites Sahasrayogam. That attribution could not be confirmed, and the reason is worth stating plainly rather than papering over.

Sahasrayogam is a Kerala compendium of anonymous authorship whose name means "a thousand formulations"; surviving versions carry somewhere between about 700 and 1,200 yogas, and it circulates in two main recensions with the Vaidyapriya and Sujanapriya commentaries. The sources consulted describe it as a formulary of medicinal preparations, and none of them documents its internal structure as containing procedural descriptions of external therapies such as talam. It is entirely plausible that a paste formulation used in talam appears there; what could not be verified is that the procedure itself is described there, and no chapter or verse locus could be found.

So the citation is left unverified rather than corrected. If you want a source for this page's content, the source is the record and this note, not a classical chapter.

## What it involves

The record gives two steps. A medicated paste is applied on the crown, and it is retained for thirty to forty-five minutes.

That is the whole of what the record specifies. It gives no preparatory step, no follow-up step, no oil, no temperature, no course length and no frequency. A clinic will have its own protocol around those points; those details are theirs, not the record's, and worth asking about directly.

## What is used

The record names the paste ingredients as candanādi, brāhmī and yaṣṭimadhu. Candanādi is a formulation family named for candana (sandalwood) rather than a single dravya.

## What to ask before booking

Which paste is being used and what is in it, since the record's three names cover a range of possible preparations; how long it will be left on, and against the clinic's own protocol rather than a generic figure; whether the practitioner has checked for scalp conditions or known sensitivities to any ingredient; and how talam differs, in their practice, from the fuller head-cap application called talapodichil.
