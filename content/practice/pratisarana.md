## What it is

Pratisarana is the rubbing of a medicated preparation onto the eyelid, applied briefly to the lid margin. The source record glosses it as "eyelid medicated rubbing", files it under kriyā-bāhya, the external procedures, and marks it vaidya-only.

This is a thin record. It has one procedural step and nothing else: no preparation, no after-care, no named substances, no quantities, no duration, no frequency. The page is short because the record is, and nothing has been filled in around it.

## Where it comes from in the classical literature

The record cites Suśruta Uttaratantra 18. That chapter is real and is a plausible neighbourhood: in Bhishagratna's translation it is "Preparations and medicinal measures for ocular affections in general", and it is the chapter usually identified with the netra-kriyākalpa, the local eye procedures. But the chapter's own content, as far as could be checked, deals with añjana (collyria) and varti (medicated sticks), together with tarpaṇa, puṭapāka, āścyotana, seka and śirobasti. Rubbing a paste on the lid margin was not found described there.

So the citation is left unverified. Pratisarana as a term is well attested in Ayurvedic ophthalmology and in Suśruta's Uttaratantra generally, and the eyelid-disease material sits in the Uttaratantra too, with the pathology of vartmagata roga at Uttaratantra 3. But no specific chapter and verse for pratisarana could be confirmed against a public translation in this session, and this reference does not guess at one.

## What it involves

The record gives exactly one step: a medicated paste is rubbed on the lid margin, briefly.

That is the whole of the procedural content in the source. No pūrva-karma, no paschāt-karma, no course length, no frequency, no season and no adverse-event data are recorded.

## What is used

The record names no dravyas and no oils. Any list of substances on this page would be invention, so there is none.

## Why it needs a vaidya

The simplicity of the description is misleading. What is being rubbed, in what strength, is the entire procedure, and it is being applied millimetres from the ocular surface, where anything that migrates onto the cornea acts directly on the eye. The record supplies neither the substance nor the strength, which is precisely the part a vaidya supplies.

An eyelid complaint that is painful, spreading, affecting vision, or not settling deserves an ophthalmic examination first. Pratisarana is not a substitute for finding out what the lid problem is.
