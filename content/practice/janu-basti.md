## What it is

The source record classes Jānu Basti as *kriyā-bāhya*, an external procedure, with the subcategory *jānu* (knee), and glosses it in English as a localized knee oil pool. It is not an enema, despite the word *basti*: the record's only working step keeps the oil on the outside of the knee.

## What it involves

The record gives one step for the main procedure: a dough ring is formed around the knee, warm *taila* is poured into the well the ring makes, and the oil is retained for 30 to 45 minutes.

That is the whole of what the record describes. It records no preparatory steps (*pūrva-karma*) and no follow-up steps (*paścāt-karma*), and it gives no course length, no session frequency and no seasonal guidance. Those fields are empty, which means they were not recorded, not that they do not exist in practice.

## What is used

The record names no oil, no dravya and no instrument for this procedure. It says only "warm taila". Any clinic offering it will have made its own choice of oil, and that choice is a reasonable thing to ask about.

Note the contrast with the sibling record for [Kaṭi Basti](/nighantu/practice/kati-basti/), which does specify black-gram-flour dough and two named oils. The absence here is an absence in the data.

## Where the citation stands

The record cites *Sahasrayogam*. Sahasrayogam is a Kerala compendium of Ayurvedic formulations; a modern edition catalogues 1,255 formulations arranged alphabetically, together with sections on *nidāna*, *guṇapāṭha* and *śālākya-tantra*. It is, in structure, a formulary of preparations rather than a manual of procedures, and I could not confirm that it carries a description of the Jānu Basti procedure itself. The Kerala-tradition attribution is consistent with how this procedure is generally placed; the specific textual locus is unconfirmed.

## What to ask before booking

This is a supervised procedure, so the useful preparation is questions rather than technique:

- which oil is used, and why that one for your case
- how the oil temperature is set and checked, given the oil sits against skin for 30 to 45 minutes
- whether the knee is examined before the first session
- how many sessions are being proposed, since the record itself gives no course length
