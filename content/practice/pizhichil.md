## What it is

Pizhichil is a whole-body warm oil treatment from Kerala. The person lies on a droṇī, the wooden treatment table, and therapists soak cloths in warm medicated oil and wring them out over the body so the oil falls in a continuous stream while they work. The record classes it as an external procedure (kriyā-bāhya) and glosses it "sarvāṅga oil-stream bath", sarvāṅga meaning whole-body.

It is manpower-heavy by design, which is part of why it is a clinic procedure rather than anything else. The record specifies two or four therapists synchronising their strokes.

## Where it sits in the classical literature

The record cites "Sahasrayogam; Kerala bahirparimārjana classics". Sahasrayogam is a Kerala compendium of roughly a thousand formulations, organised by preparation type: kaṣāya, ghṛta, taila, cūrṇa, ariṣṭa, āsava, lehya and a pariśiṣṭa appendix. That makes it a formulary rather than a procedure manual, and no public source we checked shows it describing pizhichil, so the citation is unverified. "Kerala bahirparimārjana classics" names no specific text and cannot be checked at all.

The classical category is clearer than the citation. Secondary Ayurvedic references classify pizhichil, also called kāya-seka, as pariṣeka sveda, one of Caraka's thirteen fire-based svedas, and as drava sveda in the Suśruta scheme, with one such reference citing Dhārā Kalpa 1 for the definition of pariṣeka. Caraka's own definition at Sūtrasthāna 14 verse 44 describes vāta-curative drugs "decocted to a degree of genial warmth" and then "affused from a pot or a shower or a douche-can over a person anointed with properly medicated unctuous substances and covered with a cloth". Note the difference: Caraka's pariṣeka pours a decoction over an oiled body, while pizhichil as recorded pours the oil itself.

## What it involves

The record gives two steps. The person lies on the droṇī and warm medicated taila is streamed continuously over the body through a cotton cloth wrung out by therapists. Two or four therapists synchronise their strokes, and the position is changed through the session: supine, lateral, prone, and sitting.

No preparation or aftercare step is recorded, and no session length, course length, frequency or season is given.

## What is used

Three medicated oils are named in the record's oil field: Mahānārāyaṇa Taila, Sahacarādi Taila and Bala-aśvagandhā Taila. The equipment named is the droṇī, the wooden table, and cotton cloth. No dravya list and no other materials are recorded, and no oil quantity or temperature is given.

## What to ask before booking

This is warm oil, a lot of it, over the whole body, delivered by several people at once, so the questions are mostly about temperature, staffing and hygiene. The record supplies none of those answers: it has no temperature, no volume, no session length, no course length, no frequency, no season, no contraindication and no adverse event.

Worth asking: how the oil temperature is maintained and checked, which of the oils is being used and why, how many therapists will be working, whether the oil is fresh for each person, and how many sessions are being proposed.
