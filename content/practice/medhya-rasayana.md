## What it is

Medhya-rasāyana is not one medicine. It is a named group of rasāyana dravyas, grouped by their action on medhā, intellect. Unlike the other practices in this section it is taken internally rather than applied.

## Where it comes from in the classical literature

Caraka Saṃhitā, Cikitsāsthāna 1.3, the Karaprācitīya Rasāyana Pāda, verse 30, names four preparations:

- maṇḍūkaparṇī as fresh juice (svarasa)
- yaṣṭīmadhu as powder (cūrṇa), taken with milk
- guḍūcī as juice, of the plant with its root and flower
- śaṅkhapuṣpī as paste (kalka)

The verse specifies a form and a vehicle for each. It does not state a weight, a frequency or a length of course.

## What is used

Brāhmī, yaṣṭīmadhu, śaṅkhapuṣpī and guḍūcī. The first of these is written as brāhmī in the record's dravya list and glossed as maṇḍūkaparṇī in its steps; maṇḍūkaparṇī is the name Caraka's verse uses. Since those names are not applied consistently to a single plant across sources, it is worth confirming which botanical any given preparation actually contains.

## What it involves

The method recorded is around 3 g of each of the four daily, or alternatively Brahma-rasāyana at 5 to 10 g in the morning on an empty stomach. Those quantities come from the record, not from Caraka's verse, which gives no weights. No ingredient list for Brahma-rasāyana is given in the record.

## Who should take care

No duration, frequency, season or contraindication is recorded here, so there is nothing in this source that supports taking these indefinitely.

One of the four, guḍūcī (Tinospora cordifolia), has been the subject of published liver-injury reports. A case series documented six patients with acute, predominantly hepatocellular liver injury after taking it, with the plant material confirmed as authentic T. cordifolia by morphology, HPTLC and DNA barcoding rather than a misidentified species. The authors proposed an immune-mediated mechanism; causality remains debated in the literature. This is a reason to involve a clinician before taking guḍūcī, particularly with existing liver disease.
