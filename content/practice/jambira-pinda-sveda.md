## What it is

Jambīra-Piṇḍa-Sveda is a piṇḍa sveda, a bolus form of sudation in which heated material is applied to the body rather than the body being placed in steam. Jambīra is lemon. The source record classes it as kriyā-bāhya, an external procedure, in the piṇḍa subcategory, and glosses it as lemon-bolus svedana. In Kerala practice it is also called Naranga Kizhi.

## What a session involves

The record gives two steps only: lemon halves with rock salt and spices, sautéed; and the result applied as a warm bolus to the affected area. It does not name the spices, does not give a bolus temperature, does not say how long the application runs, and does not say how many boluses are used or how often they are changed.

Practitioner descriptions of Naranga Kizhi fill in a version of the preparation the record leaves open: lemons sliced, fried with saindhava lavaṇa, garlic, grated coconut and a suitable oil, then tied into a cloth bolus. That is a corroborating account of the same procedure, not a reading of this record, and the two differ: the record's version mentions no coconut, no garlic and no oil.

## What is used

Jambīra, lemon, and rock salt are the only two ingredients the record names. Spices are referred to as a group without being identified. No oil is named in the record, and no instrument beyond the bolus itself.

## Where the citation stands

The record cites Sahasrayogam. Sahasrayogam is a Kerala compendium of formulations organised by preparation type, and I found no evidence it describes procedures. Independent accounts describe Jambīra-Piṇḍa-Sveda as a speciality of Kerala vaidyas within the piṇḍa-sveda class, part of the body of snehana and svedana technique codified as Keraliya Panchakarma through clinical practice rather than set out in the classical saṃhitās. The nearest confirmed classical locus for bolus sudation as a category is saṅkara sveda, first of the thirteen fire-based sudations in Caraka Sūtrasthāna 14.

## What to ask before booking

With no temperature and no duration in the record, both rest on the practitioner. Sensible things to establish are what actually goes into the bolus, since the accounts vary, how the temperature is checked before the bolus touches you, and whether the skin over the area is intact, given that the bolus combines heat, salt and citrus juice.
