## What it is

Grīvā Basti belongs to the family of localised oil-pool treatments, where basti here means a reservoir rather than the enema procedure of the same name. The source record classes it as kriyā-bāhya, an external procedure, in the grīvā subcategory, and glosses it as cervical oil pool. A wall of dough is built into a ring on the body and warm oil is held inside that ring against the skin for a set period.

## What a session involves

The record gives three steps: a dough ring is placed on the cervical region with the person prone; warm taila is pooled inside it and retained; and the retention lasts thirty to forty-five minutes. The record does not specify the dough material, the oil temperature, how the oil is kept warm during retention, or what is done afterwards.

## What is used

The record names three oils: Sahacarādi Taila, Mahānārāyaṇa Taila and Kottamcukkādi Taila. These are compound medicated oils, and each is a formulation in its own right rather than a plain carrier oil. The record names no cūrṇa, no other dravyas and no equipment beyond the dough ring.

## Where the citation stands

The record cites Sahasrayogam, and that attribution does not hold up for the procedure. A peer-reviewed conceptual review of Grīvā Basti states plainly that the classical Ayurvedic texts do not directly mention it, that its method is derived from Śiro Basti as described by the ācāryas, and that it is extensively practised within Keraliya Panchakarma. Sahasrayogam itself is a formulary organised by preparation type rather than a manual of procedures, so it is a reasonable place to look for the oils and the wrong place to look for the technique. This is better described as a Kerala clinical practice with a classical parent procedure than as a text-cited one.

## What to ask before booking

Since the record fixes no oil temperature and no dough specification, both are practitioner judgement. Reasonable things to establish are which of the oils is being used and on what basis, how the oil temperature is checked and maintained across a forty-five minute retention, and what happens if the ring leaks. The record carries no contraindications, no red flags and no adverse-event data of its own, so a practitioner's own screening is the only screening there is.
