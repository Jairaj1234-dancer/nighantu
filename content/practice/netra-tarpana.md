## What it is

Netra-tarpaṇa is the pooling of warm medicated ghee over the eye inside a wall of dough built on the face. The source record glosses it as "eye oleation pool", files it under kriyā-bāhya, the external procedures, and marks it vaidya-only.

It is one of the netra-kriyākalpa, the local eye procedures of śālākya-tantra. Because the medium sits directly against the eye for a sustained period, it is a procedure with an obvious margin for harm, and this page describes it rather than instructing in it.

## Where it comes from in the classical literature

The record's citation needs a correction. It gives Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya **Uttara** 24, but that chapter is Śirorogapratiṣedha, on diseases of the head. Tarpaṇa is Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya **Sūtrasthāna** 24, the Tarpaṇa-Puṭapāka-Vidhi Adhyāya, which pairs tarpaṇa with puṭapāka.

In that chapter, walls resembling a well and of uniform dimensions are built from a paste of barley and black gram around both eye sockets, to a height of two aṅgula. Once the structure is confirmed stable, ghee liquefied by indirect heating over hot water is poured gently into the well over the closed lids. The person then opens the eyes gradually and keeps blinking. Retention is given in mātrākāla and varies widely with the condition being addressed.

Suśruta also treats tarpaṇa: Uttaratantra 18, on medicinal measures for ocular affections in general, is one of the chapters covering the kriyākalpa group.

## What it involves

The record gives two steps:

- a dough ring is built around the closed eye and warm medicated ghṛta pooled inside it
- the person opens and closes the eyes intermittently, for around twenty minutes

The record specifies no pūrva-karma and no paschāt-karma, no course length, no frequency and no season. The classical chapter, by contrast, sets retention by condition rather than at a fixed twenty minutes, so the record's figure should be read as one protocol's number and not as the classical value.

## What is used

The medicated ghees and oils the record names are Triphalā Ghṛta, Mahā-tripalā Ghṛta and Ksīrabalā. Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya Sūtrasthāna 24 describes barley and black gram paste as the material of the retaining wall, and notes that in some conditions vasā, muscle fat, replaces ghee.

## What to ask before booking

Since this sits with a vaidya, the useful preparation is questions rather than technique. What is being pooled, and is it a pharmacy-grade preparation. How long the pool is retained and on what basis that duration is chosen. What the vaidya expects the eyes to feel like afterwards and for how long, given that vision is typically blurred while the ghee clears. Whether the procedure is being done on one eye or both.

Any eye symptom that is new, painful, or involves loss of vision is an ophthalmic emergency and belongs in front of an eye doctor first, not in a tarpaṇa appointment.
