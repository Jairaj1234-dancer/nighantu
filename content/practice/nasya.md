## What it is

Nasya is the administration of medicine through the nose. The source record glosses it as "nasal medication" and files it under pañcakarma, the five principal purificatory procedures, at practitioner level vaidya-only.

That level matters, because nasya is the one pañcakarma procedure people most often assume they can do at home from a bottle of oil. The record does not support that reading, and the sections below describe what the procedure is rather than how to perform it.

## Where it comes from in the classical literature

All four loci the record cites check out.

- **Caraka Sūtrasthāna 5** (Mātrāśitīya Adhyāya) covers quantity of food and the daily regimen, and nasya sits there among the dinacarya practices alongside abhyaṅga, gaṇḍūṣa, añjana, karṇapūraṇa and dhūmapāna.
- **Caraka Siddhisthāna 9** (Trimarmīya Siddhi) concerns the three vital sites, heart, head and bladder, in the context of purificatory procedures, and describes nasya, its types and its place in disorders of the head.
- **Suśruta Cikitsāsthāna 40** is Dhūma-Nasya-Kavalagraha Cikitsitam, the chapter on medicated smoke, nasal medication and gargles.
- **Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya Sūtrasthāna 20** is Nasya Vidhi Adhyāya, Vāgbhaṭa's dedicated chapter on the types, methods, indications and contraindications of nasal instillation.

## What it involves

The record's protocol has three phases.

**Before.** Mukha-abhyaṅga, oil massage of the face, followed by nāḍī-svedana, localised steam, to the head and neck.

**During.** Warm medicated taila, six to eight drops per nostril, with the person supine and the head extended, the oil drawn in by inhalation.

**After.** Anything reaching the throat is spat out, followed by gargling with warm water; cold, wind and smoke are avoided for twenty-four hours.

The record gives no course length, no frequency and no preferred season, and records no adverse events.

## What is used

The oils the record names are Aṇu Taila, Ṣaḍbindu Taila, Ksīrabalā and Mahāmāṣa Taila. No other dravyas are listed. Which oil is chosen, and whether nasya is appropriate at all, is a clinical judgement, and Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya Sūtrasthāna 20 is the chapter that sets out the contraindications.

## Who it is for and what to ask

The record assigns nasya to a vaidya, so the practical question for a reader is what to ask before booking rather than how to do it. Reasonable questions: which oil is being used and why that one; whether the pūrva-karma, the facial oiling and steam, is included, since the record treats it as part of the procedure; what the twenty-four-hour after-care restrictions mean for the rest of the day; and what would make the vaidya decide not to proceed.

Anyone with an acute respiratory infection, a nosebleed, recent nasal or sinus surgery, or who is pregnant, should raise it before booking rather than after. The record itself carries no contraindication list, so the person in front of you is the one who has to supply it.
