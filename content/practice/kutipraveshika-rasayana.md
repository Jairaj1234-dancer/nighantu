## What it is

Kuṭī means hut, praveśika means entering. Kuṭīpraveśika rasāyana is rejuvenation therapy undergone while living inside a specially constructed chamber for the duration of the course. It is one of two classical modes: the other, vātātapika, is the outdoor mode, and is explicitly the one in which exposure to wind and sun is permitted. That contrast is the clearest statement of what the enclosed mode is for.

The source record classifies it as rasāyana with the subcategory kuṭīpraveśika and glosses it "in-residence rasāyana".

## Where it comes from in the classical literature

Caraka Cikitsā-sthāna 1.1, the Abhaya-āmalakīya rasāyana pāda. This checks out.

The kuṭī itself is specified in verses 16 to 24. It should face east or north, have thick walls and three chambers one within the other, narrow ventilators, sufficient height and space, be well lit and pleasant to the mind, clean, comfortable across the seasons, impermeable to unwanted sound, not accessible to women, and stocked with the necessary accessories. Entry is timed: during the sun's northern course, in the bright half of the month, on an auspicious day and at a favourable muhūrta. Commentary on the design reads the thick walls and small openings as the means of keeping out wind, noise and other distractions.

## Preparation before entry

Caraka is explicit that purification comes first. Verses 24 to 28 direct that the individual be cleansed by pañcakarma, and that the rasāyana be administered only afterwards, when he is happy and has regained his strength. A preparatory combination is given: harītakī, saindhava, āmalakī, guḍa, vacā, viḍaṅga, rajanī, pippalī and ginger taken with hot water, followed by three to seven days of barley gruel with old ghee.

The source record compresses this to "pañcakarma (vamana + virecana + basti) to clear the substrate". The three named karmas are the record's specification, not a list quoted from the verses.

## What the residence involves

The record gives two elements. First, daily intake of a rasāyana dravya chosen by the vaidya, naming Āmalakī rasāyana, Triphalā rasāyana and lauha-bhasma as examples. Second, a strict regimen: no exposure to wind, sun, anger or emotion, plus mantra-japa.

On the translations read here, Caraka's kuṭī passage does not itself spell out prohibitions on wind, sun, anger or a japa practice. What the text supports is that the person does not leave the chamber until the course ends, that the construction is designed to exclude wind, sound and distraction, that celibacy is expected (hence the chamber being closed to women), and that vātātapika is the mode in which wind and sun exposure is allowed. The record's regimen list should be read as the database's own gloss rather than as verses.

The record gives no course length, no frequency and no season, so no duration is stated here.

## Why it is vaidya-only

Three things put this outside self-care, and none of them is negotiable. It is gated behind full pañcakarma, which is itself supervised clinical work. One of the named daily substances, lauha-bhasma, is a processed metal preparation. And the therapy is a period of enclosed residence with a controlled regimen, which is an inpatient setting rather than a treatment appointment. The record marks it vaidya-only.
