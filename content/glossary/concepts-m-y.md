#### What is it made of?

### Phenolics

- Isovitexin
- Gallic acid

### Flavonoids

- Quercetin
- Kaempferol

### Nutritional Profile

- **Protein:** 24g/100g
- **Fiber:** 16g/100g
- **Iron:** 6.7mg/100g
- **Folate:** 625μg/100g
- **Potassium:** 1246mg/100g
- **Magnesium:** 189mg/100g
- **Phytic acid:** 0.3-0.5% (antinutrient)

**Analytical Methods:** AOAC methods, HPLC (phenolics), ICP-OES (minerals)

#### Dosage forms and preparation

**Dosage Forms:** Whole mung bean, Split dal, Sprouts, Yusha (medicinal soup)

**Standard Dosage:** As dietary: 30–50 g; Yusha: 100–200 mL

**Bioavailability:** Highest among pulses in Ayurveda; light and easy to digest

**Optimal Timing:** Any meal; Yusha during illness/convalescence

**Standardized Extract:** Mudga Yusha (therapeutic mung soup) — premier pathya (dietary therapy)

**Shelf Life:** Whole: 12+ months; sprouts: 2–3 days; Yusha: consume fresh

**Storage:** Cool, dry place; sprouts refrigerated

**Marker Compounds:** Protein (24%), [Vitexin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-t-z/#vitexin), [Isovitexin](/ayurveda-atlas/glossary/compounds-g-l/#isovitexin), Iron, Dietary fiber

**Quality Parameters:** Moisture <12%, germination rate >85% for sprout quality, no aflatoxin

**Vehicle (Anupana):** Ghee; [cumin](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/cumin/)-[coriander](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/coriander/) seasoning for digestibility

### Vamshalochana {#vamshalochana}

Siliceous exudate from the internodes of female bamboo (*Bambusa bambos* / *B. arundinacea*) — classical Ayurveda treats it as a cooling, astringent, medhya and kasahara dravya. It is a key **prakshep dravya** in classical Avaleha pharmacy, including [Chyawanprash](/ayurveda-atlas/formulation/chyawanprash/) and related Rasayanas.

#### Identification

Translucent to opaque whitish-grey siliceous nodules, hard and brittle, sourced by splitting mature female bamboo internodes. Classical name *Tabasheer* (Unani/Persian) denotes the same material. Over 90% hydrated amorphous silica (SiO₂·nH₂O), trace potassium, calcium, magnesium, iron. Sweet-astringent taste, cold in virya, madhura-vipaka.

#### Classical references

- *Charaka Samhita* — named in medhya-rasayana contexts
- *Bhavaprakasha Nighantu* — Haritakyadi Varga; cites *Kasahara* and *Daurbalyahara* actions
- *Raja Nighantu* — "Vamsharochana"
- *Sharangadhara Samhita* — prakshep in Avaleha kalpana

#### Use in Chyawanprash

Added in the **Prakshep phase** (lukewarm, post-paka) along with [Pippali](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/pippali/), [Twak](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/twak-cinnamon/), [Ela](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/ela/), [Tejpatra](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/tejapatra/) and [Nagakesara](/ayurveda-atlas/herb/nagakesara/). Provides the sweet-cooling balancing note and the classical medhya-rasayana signature. See Prakshep Dravya.

#### Adulteration note

The commodity market is heavily adulterated with **calcium silicate** (chemically synthesized), borax, or chalk. Authentic Vamshalochana tests: soluble in HF but insoluble in HCl; gives clear silica fusion with Na₂CO₃; XRD shows amorphous halo (no sharp calcite peaks). Premium sourcing — hand-sorted nodules from Western Ghats or Northeast Indian bamboo belts.

#### Safety

Generally regarded as safe at classical prakshep doses (50–500 mg/day as part of compounded Avalehas). High silica content: avoid standalone mega-doses in renal disease.

### Venesection Instruments Blood Letting Blades {#venesection-instruments-blood-letting-blades}

Sharp instruments specifically designed for Siravyadha (venipuncture/venesection) - controlled blood-letting from specific veins. Different blade shapes for different body locations.

#### Where is it described in the classical texts?

Sushruta Samhita Sutrasthana Chapter 14; Sharira Sthana Chapter 8 describes specific veins (Sira) for blood-letting at different body locations.

#### How is it used traditionally?

Therapeutic venesection for Pitta-Rakta disorders, hypertension, skin diseases, gout, liver disorders, and conditions requiring systemic blood purification.

#### Traditional materials

- Steel blades (Kushapatra Shastra - lancet)
- Vrihimukha Shastra (fine-point blade)

#### Modern variants

- Sterile disposable lancets
- Phlebotomy needles
- Blood-letting lancets

#### Regulatory status

- **India Cdsco:** Lancets and phlebotomy needles are Class A/B medical devices under CDSCO MDR 2017.
