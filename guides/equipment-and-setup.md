## The five things you cannot skip

1. **A vessel with a controlled orifice.** Not a jug. The stream has to hold unbroken for the
   length of the session.
2. **Suspension at the right height.** Roughly ten to twelve centimetres above the forehead,
   adjustable, and stable enough not to drift or swing.
3. **A table with head support.** The neck must be neutral and the forehead level. A bed will
   not do this; the head needs to overhang slightly so oil runs off rather than pooling.
4. **A catch and return path.** A basin below the head at minimum. A closed-loop base that
   returns oil to the vessel if you are working alone.
5. **A way to warm the oil.** A water bath is the traditional method and still the most
   controllable. Never direct heat, and never a microwave, which heats unevenly and creates
   hot spots you cannot feel until they are on someone's forehead.

## Space and mess

The honest numbers. A session puts half a litre to a litre of oil in circulation. Assume:

- A floor area around the table that will get oil on it. Mat it, and use a mat you are willing
  to write off.
- Towels that will need a hot wash with degreaser. Sesame oil does not come out on a cold
  cycle.
- Hair full of oil for several hours, by design.
- A room that will hold the smell of warm sesame oil for a day.

None of this is a reason not to do it. It is a reason to set up somewhere you have decided in
advance you do not mind.

## Oil handling and reuse

Oil is recirculated within a session as standard. Reuse *between* sessions is where practice
varies, and it matters commercially for therapists.

- Strain after every session. Hair, skin and dust all end up in the basin.
- Store in a sealed container away from light.
- Reheating the same oil repeatedly degrades it. Sesame oil is relatively stable, but repeated
  heat cycling oxidises it and the smell will tell you before anything else does.
- Never carry oil between clients without a documented hygiene policy. In a professional
  setting this is a decision to make deliberately, in writing, not by default.

## Warming method

A water bath, with the oil in a metal container standing in hot water, is the traditional
approach and the one that gives the most control. Check with a thermometer, then confirm on
the inner wrist. Oil that has drifted below body temperature mid-session should be swapped or
reheated rather than tolerated.

Note that most portable equipment, including the device described on this site, delivers the
stream but does not regulate temperature. The operator warms the oil and sets the flow by
hand. Any vendor implying otherwise about a gravity-fed device is overstating what it does.
