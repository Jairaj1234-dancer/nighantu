faq:
  - q: "What is Shirodhara?"
    a: "Shirodhara is a classical Ayurvedic external practice in which a warm liquid, usually a medicated sesame-based oil, is poured in an unbroken stream onto the forehead for a set period. The name joins shiras, meaning head, and dhara, meaning stream. It belongs to the murdha taila group of head-oleation procedures described in the Ashtanga Hridaya."
  - q: "What does Shirodhara feel like?"
    a: "Most people describe drifting into a state between waking and sleeping, sometimes with a distorted sense of how much time has passed. Some fall asleep. The first minute or two can feel strange as the stream settles, after which people usually stop noticing it as a sensation."
  - q: "How often should you have Shirodhara?"
    a: "Traditionally as a course rather than a one-off: commonly 7 to 14 consecutive daily sessions in a Panchakarma setting. As general practice, weekly or fortnightly is a common pattern. A single session is the least representative version of the practice."
  - q: "Which oil is used for Shirodhara?"
    a: "A medicated sesame-based taila is the default. The specific formulation is matched to the presentation: warming and unctuous for a Vata-predominant picture, cooling and in a lighter base for Pitta, warming and penetrating with lekhana herbs for Kapha. Buttermilk, milk and decoctions are used in place of oil in some classical variants."
  - q: "How much oil does a Shirodhara session use?"
    a: "It depends on the apparatus. A traditional dhara patra filled by hand runs on roughly half a litre to a litre. A vessel meant to run a whole session without a refill holds more by design: the Surya Shirodhara takes about 1.5 litres per session, three 500 ml bottles, in a two litre vessel. Almost all of it is recovered and reused within the session; what is genuinely consumed is what soaks into hair, cloth and the catch basin."
  - q: "What does a home Shirodhara setup cost to run?"
    a: "The equipment is a one-off; the oil is the recurring cost, and it is set by how long the oil lasts rather than by how much the vessel holds. Oil is reused for at most three sessions and then discarded. So a two litre vessel charged with 1.5 litres, three 500 ml bottles, serves three sessions before it is emptied: about one bottle of oil per session. Losses to hair, cloth and the catch basin mean topping up between sessions, so treat one bottle per session as the floor rather than an exact figure. Beyond the oil, budget for towels that need a hot wash with degreaser, a mat you are willing to write off, and the time to clean up."
  - q: "Can you do Shirodhara alone, without an assistant?"
    a: "Yes, with the right equipment. The obstacle is that oil cools while it runs and traditionally an assistant reheats and returns it mid-session. Working alone, the practical answer is a vessel that holds the whole session's oil so nothing needs returning while the stream runs, with the run-off caught in the base and recovered afterwards. The trade is that temperature drifts down across the session, so the oil is warmed to the top of its range at the start. A powered unit with a pump and a heated reservoir is the alternative if that drift matters to you."
  - q: "Do you wash your hair after Shirodhara?"
    a: "Not immediately. The oil is left in the hair and the head is wrapped warm. Washing is done some hours later or the next morning, with a mild cleanser."
  - q: "Is Shirodhara the same as a head massage?"
    a: "No. Head massage, shiro abhyanga, is oil worked in by hand. Shirodhara is a continuous poured stream with no hands on the head during the pour. Both belong to the murdha taila group, and a short abhyanga usually precedes a shirodhara session."
  - q: "What should you do after a Shirodhara session?"
    a: "Rest for 10 to 20 minutes before sitting up, and sit for a minute or two before standing. Keep the head warm and covered, avoid cold wind and cold showers, eat something light and warm, and keep the rest of the day quiet."
