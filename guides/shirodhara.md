Shirodhara is the best known of the *murdha taila* procedures, the classical group of
treatments in which oil is applied to the head. A vessel is suspended above the reclining
person and a warm liquid falls in a single continuous thread onto the forehead, traditionally
onto the point between the eyebrows. The stream is kept unbroken, the oil is caught and
returned to the vessel, and the session runs for a fixed time rather than to a target.

Three things distinguish it from ordinary head massage. The liquid is medicated and chosen for
the person rather than generic. The delivery is a continuous stream rather than hands. And the
temperature and duration are specified, not improvised.

## What is actually poured

Oil is the default and the most common by a wide margin, which is why the practice is often
called *taila dhara*. The classical literature also describes the same technique with other
liquids, and the choice changes the character of the session:

| Liquid | Sanskrit term | Traditionally chosen when |
|---|---|---|
| Medicated oil | Taila dhara | The general case, and the default for a Vata-predominant picture |
| Buttermilk | Takra dhara | A heated or sharp presentation where a cooling stream is preferred |
| Milk | Ksheera dhara | A depleted or dry presentation |
| Decoction | Kashaya dhara | Where a specific herbal decoction is indicated |
| Water infusion | Jala dhara | Simple cooling applications |

This guide covers taila dhara throughout, since that is what is practised and sold almost
everywhere, and notes where the others differ.

## Where the practice sits

Shirodhara is normally given as part of a wider sequence rather than alone. In a full
Panchakarma programme it follows *abhyanga* (whole-body oleation) and precedes rest. As a
standalone treatment in a spa or clinic it is usually preceded by a short head and shoulder
abhyanga to warm the tissue, and followed by rest and a delayed hair wash.
