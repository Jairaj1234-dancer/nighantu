Shirodhara is a gentle procedure, but it is still a warm oil application to the head, given
to someone lying still for the better part of an hour. The cautions are mostly practical.

## Do not proceed

- **Broken, inflamed or infected skin** on the scalp or forehead. Oil on a compromised skin
  barrier is a straightforward problem.
- **Acute fever or active infection.** External oleation is deferred until the acute phase
  passes.
- **Recent head, neck or facial injury**, or recent surgery in that area.
- **Immediately after a heavy meal**, or during acute indigestion. The classical instruction
  is to wait until the previous meal is digested.

## Seek clearance first

- **Pregnancy.** Not automatically excluded, but positioning, duration, oil and temperature
  all need adjustment, and several herbs common in shirodhara tailas are avoided.
- **High or unstable blood pressure**, or a history of fainting or postural dizziness. Lying
  flat for forty minutes and then standing is the risk, not the oil.
- **Any neurological condition**, including seizure history.
- **Known sensitivity to sesame or to any herb in the formulation.** Patch test on the inner
  forearm twenty-four hours ahead.

## Practical hazards worth naming

- **Oil in the eyes.** The band above the eyebrows and the eye covering exist for this reason
  and are not optional.
- **Oil temperature.** The forehead is more sensitive than the hands. Test on the inner wrist.
- **Slipping.** Oil on a hard floor is genuinely dangerous. Mat the area.
- **Getting up.** Sit for a minute or two before standing.

## What this practice is not

Shirodhara is a traditional external procedure with a long history of use and a growing but
still limited research base. It is not a treatment for any diagnosed condition, and nothing on
this site should be read as saying that it is. If you are dealing with a medical problem,
treat Shirodhara as something to discuss with your clinician rather than as an alternative to
seeing one.
