In classical practice the oil is not selected from a menu of conditions. It is selected from
the *prakriti* and the current *vikriti*, the constitution and the present imbalance, and then
adjusted for the season and the person's tolerance for heat.

## The classical base

*Tila taila*, cold-pressed sesame oil, is the base. Charaka and Sushruta both describe it as
the foremost oil for Vata pacification: unctuous, warming, penetrating, and stable enough to
be heated and reheated. For Pitta-predominant work it is usually blended down with a lighter,
cooler oil, because sesame on its own is warming.

## The three profiles

### Vata

Warming and unctuous, built to ground a restless, dry, over-mobile picture. A representative
formulation compounds *Ashwagandha* (Withania somnifera), *Bala* (Sida cordifolia),
*Shatavari* (Asparagus racemosus), *Bhringraj* (Eclipta alba), *Yashtimadhu*
(Glycyrrhiza glabra), *Tulsi* (Ocimum tenuiflorum) and *Tagara* (Valeriana wallichii) in a
sesame base, sometimes with olive oil as a supporting emollient.

Traditionally used to help soothe a restless, over-active Vata mind, and to support a feeling
of groundedness and settled prana when applied externally.

### Pitta

Cooling, in a base moderated away from pure sesame. A representative formulation compounds
*Brahmi* (Bacopa monnieri), *Guduchi* (Tinospora cordifolia), *Shatavari*, *Manjistha*
(Rubia cordifolia), *Yashtimadhu*, *Dhanyaka* (Coriandrum sativum) and *Nagarmotha*
(Cyperus rotundus), in a sesame and sunflower blend that lowers the warming character of the
base.

Traditionally used to help soothe a heated, sharp, over-focused Pitta mind, and to support a
feeling of coolness and composure.

### Kapha

Warming, lighter, and penetrating, with *lekhana* (scraping) herbs to mobilise rather than
settle. A representative formulation compounds *Punarnava* (Boerhavia diffusa), *Chitrak*
(Plumbago zeylanica), *Neem* (Azadirachta indica), *Manjistha*, *Bhumi Amalaki* (Phyllanthus
niruri), *Vacha* (Acorus calamus) and *Tulsi* in a sesame base, often with a cineole-rich
aromatic such as eucalyptus.

Traditionally used where the head feels heavy and the senses dull, to support a lighter,
clearer state.

## A note on aromatics

Most contemporary shirodhara tailas carry an aromatic fraction. This is not decoration. The
person lies with the oil running across the forehead and close to the nose for the whole
session, so the olfactory input is a real part of the experience, and the aromatic profile is
chosen to match the intended direction of the session: settling for Vata, cooling for Pitta,
opening for Kapha.

## External use only

Shirodhara tailas are compounded for external application. They are not taken internally,
and the herb selection is made on that basis: what matters is scalp and skin contact plus the
aromatic fraction, not oral bioavailability. Research findings on oral dosing of the same
herbs do not transfer to this route.
