## What a clinic gives you

- **An operator who has done it before.** Stream height, oscillation, timing and reading the
  person's state are skills, and they show.
- **A second pair of hands.** Traditional practice assumes an assistant reheating and
  returning oil. That is what keeps the temperature stable across forty minutes.
- **A room built for it.** Drainage, washable surfaces, warm air, and somewhere to put an
  oily person afterwards.
- **Assessment.** Which oil, how long, how warm, and whether you should be having it at all.

## What home practice gives you

- **Frequency.** The classical pattern is a course of consecutive daily sessions, not a single
  appointment. Very few people will book fourteen clinic sessions in a row, and a single
  session is the least representative version of the practice.
- **Cost per session** falls sharply after the equipment is paid for.
- **Timing.** Evening sessions before sleep are the traditional slot and the hardest to book.

## The three real problems

**Keeping the stream unbroken.** This is the one people underestimate. Pouring from a jug or a
bottle cannot hold a continuous thread for thirty minutes, and every break resets the person's
nervous system. This is what a suspended vessel with a controlled orifice is for, and it is
the non-negotiable piece of equipment.

**Keeping the oil warm.** Oil cools while it runs. With an assistant you swap and reheat. Alone
you either accept a temperature drift over the session, warm the oil to the top of the range at
the start and let it fall through, or pause and reheat. Closed-loop recirculation helps, because
the returned oil is still warm, but no manual setup holds a fixed temperature by itself.

**The oil itself.** Half a litre to a litre of sesame oil, a person's hair full of it, a floor
around the table, towels that will need hot washing, and a room that will smell of it. This is
the part nobody mentions and the part that stops most people repeating it. Plan the cleanup
before the first session, not after.

## A realistic middle path

Have the first session or two done professionally, so you know what a correct session feels
like and get the oil selection right. Then run courses at home with equipment, and go back
periodically for assessment. That is the pattern that actually gets used.
