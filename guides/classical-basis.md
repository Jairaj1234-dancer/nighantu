It is worth being precise about what the classical texts contain, because a great deal of
what circulates online attributes very specific modern protocol detail to sources that do not
carry it.

## Murdha taila

The Ashtanga Hridaya groups head-oleation procedures under *murdha taila* and distinguishes
them by how the oil is applied. In ascending order of the quantity of oil and the length of
contact these are usually given as:

1. **Shiro abhyanga**, oil rubbed into the scalp by hand.
2. **Shiro seka** or **dhara**, oil poured over the head in a stream.
3. **Shiro pichu**, a cloth pad soaked in oil and held on the crown.
4. **Shiro basti**, oil retained on the head inside a leather or cloth collar.

Each is described as progressively more intensive. The stream form is the one this guide
covers.

## What the Samhitas actually say

The classical material establishes the category, the ordering, the general indications for
head oleation, and the principle that the oil is selected according to the presentation. It
does not give the minute-by-minute protocol used in contemporary practice.

The Sushruta Samhita contributes the surrounding framework: oleation as a preparatory measure,
the properties of sesame oil as the *taila uttama* for Vata, and the sequencing of external
therapies.

## What comes from Kerala

The detailed operational tradition is Keralan and later. Texts such as the **Sahasrayogam**,
along with an unbroken practice lineage, supply most of what a therapist is actually trained
in: the suspended pot, the height of the pot above the forehead, the width of the stream, the
oscillating movement across the forehead used in some schools, session lengths, and the
sequence of rest and washing afterwards.

This is not a criticism of the practice. It is simply where the knowledge comes from, and it
matters when someone claims a specific number of minutes is "prescribed in the Charaka
Samhita". It is not.

## The term Ajna marma

Popular writing places the stream on the "third eye" and calls it the *ajna* chakra. The
classical marma literature describes *sthapani marma* at the point between the eyebrows.
*Ajna* is a term from yogic and tantric anatomy rather than from the Ayurvedic marma
enumeration. The two are frequently conflated. The physical target point is the same.
