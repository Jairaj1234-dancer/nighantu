**Disclosure.** This site is published by Age Ayurveda, which sells a Shirodhara vessel and
the oils used with it. This page is about whether and how to offer the treatment at all, which
is a decision that runs mostly against your room and your diary rather than against anybody's
equipment. Where our own product is the relevant answer we say so, and where it is not, we say
that too.

Most operators approach this by choosing equipment first. That is the wrong end. Equipment is
the smallest of the four decisions and the only one you can change later without disruption.

## The room decides

Shirodhara puts between half a litre and 1.5 litres of warm oil over someone's head for the
better part of an hour. Most of it is caught and recirculated, but the practical reality is a
room where oil gets on the floor, the linen and the therapist.

What the room needs:

- A surface you have accepted will take oil. A mat you are willing to write off is cheaper
  than protecting a floor you care about.
- Somewhere to catch the run-off. This is the point at which a fixed installation and a
  portable vessel genuinely diverge: a plumbed dhara room needs a fit-out, and a closed-loop
  vessel does not.
- Linen that goes through a hot wash with degreaser. Sesame oil does not come out on a cold
  cycle, and this is a laundry cost rather than a one-off.
- Air. The room will hold the smell of warm sesame oil for a day.

If you have a wet room already, the fit-out question disappears. If you do not, it is the
largest number in the decision, and it is the reason self-contained vessels exist.

## The slot is 90 minutes, not 60

This is the single most common commercial mistake, and it is expensive because it is baked
into the price list.

A 40 minute pour occupies the room for about 90 minutes: 10 minutes of setup and oil warming,
the pour itself, 15 to 20 minutes of rest with the client still in the room, and 15 minutes of
strip-down, straining and cleaning. The rest period is not optional and not shortenable; it is
part of the treatment.

The therapist is free during the rest period but the room is not. If your pricing assumes room
turnover at 60 minutes, the treatment will look unprofitable for reasons that have nothing to
do with the treatment. [Notes for therapists](/shirodhara/for-therapists/) works the
turnaround through in more detail.

## Consumables

Oil is the recurring cost, and it is set by how long a charge lasts rather than by how much the
vessel holds. On a three-session reuse policy, a closed-loop vessel charged with 1.5 litres,
three 500 ml bottles, works out at roughly one 500 ml bottle per session. A hand-filled pot
uses less oil and more labour.

Apply your own cost per bottle to that and you have the number to price against. Add laundry
and the disposable covering. Do not price on the assumption of indefinite reuse: whether oil is
carried between different clients is a hygiene and consent decision before it is a financial
one, and it is worth deciding explicitly and telling clients what you decided.

## Staffing

Traditional Shirodhara assumes two people, one at the head and one keeping the oil warm and
returning it. A single therapist can run a session if the apparatus returns warm oil by itself,
which is what closed-loop recirculation is for, or by accepting a temperature drift across the
session.

Whether a qualification is required depends on where you are, and you should ask your own
regulator rather than rely on a general answer. In India, Panchakarma in a clinical setting
runs under the supervision of a practitioner holding a recognised Ayurvedic degree. Elsewhere
there is usually no regime specific to Shirodhara and it sits under whatever governs massage
and bodywork locally. Training is widely available and ranges from a one-day certificate to a
sixty-hour programme.

## What you may say about it

Name no condition. That is the whole rule, and it survives every market.

Shirodhara equipment is placed on the market as general wellness equipment rather than as a
medical device. The moment a menu, a website or a booking confirmation advertises a session as
a treatment for something named, the claim becomes yours and you have taken on obligations
your regulator will hold you to. That is true even where the classical texts list indications,
and it is true even if your supplier makes the claim first: repeating it makes it yours.

Describe the practice. Describe what people report. Let the tradition speak for itself.
[Medical device status](/shirodhara/medical-device-status/) sets out how the equipment is
actually classified in India, the United States and the EU, which is the question that comes up
when a larger operator's compliance team looks at it.

## Where a portable vessel is the wrong answer

It is worth being plain about this, since we sell one.

If you are fitting out a dedicated dhara room and will run back-to-back sessions all day, a
powered machine with a thermostat, a timer and an oscillating stream is the better instrument,
and a plumbed installation is the better room. The case for a self-contained gravity vessel is
strongest where a dedicated room is not available, where sessions are occasional rather than
continuous, or where the treatment has to move between rooms or sites. [Choosing
equipment](/shirodhara/choosing-equipment/) compares the actual devices, including where ours
loses.
